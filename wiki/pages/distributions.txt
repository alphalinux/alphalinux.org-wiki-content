===== Distributions =====

Three Linux distributions produce current releases for Alpha: Gentoo, Debian through its Ports archive, and T2 SDE. Many more supported Alpha in the late 1990s and early 2000s, when Alpha was a commercial Linux platform; the last of those ended around 2006. This page covers both. Install media and instructions are on the [[documentation:installation|Installation]] page, and the lists and channels each distribution uses on [[:community|Community]].

=== Gentoo ===

[[https://www.gentoo.org/|Gentoo Linux]] has a maintained [[https://wiki.gentoo.org/wiki/Project:Alpha|Alpha port]], installed by hand from a minimal install CD and a stage3 tarball, following the [[https://wiki.gentoo.org/wiki/Handbook:Alpha|Gentoo Handbook for Alpha]]. New stage3 tarballs, for OpenRC and systemd, are built regularly. [(>[[https://distfiles.gentoo.org/releases/alpha/autobuilds/|releases/alpha/autobuilds]], distfiles.gentoo.org)] Every package keyword has been unstable (''~alpha'') since January 2020, but the Alpha profiles, which had been demoted to experimental in 2022, are stable again since August 2024. The profile sets ''CHOST'' to ''alpha-unknown-linux-gnu'' and no ''-mcpu'', so the stages run on any Alpha processor that the kernel supports. [(>[[https://gitweb.gentoo.org/repo/gentoo.git/tree/profiles/arch/alpha/make.defaults|profiles/arch/alpha/make.defaults]], gentoo.git)] The port uses the gentoo-alpha mailing list and the ''#gentoo-alpha'' IRC channel.

In February 2003 the port had three developers, who split the work as "bjb - kernel, linker, gcc", "wwoods - kde, misc apps", and "agriffis - gnome, misc apps": Bjoern Brauel, Will Woods, and Aron Griffis. Griffis called the port "short-staffed on Alpha developers": when all three were busy at their jobs, "gentoo/alpha stalls for a while". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L1656|"Re: Fw: Gentoo/Alpha"]], Aron Griffis, gentoo-alpha, 25 Feb 2003)] The stage tarballs were built for the EV5, "the right thing for compatibility anyway". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L62|"Re: Fw: Gentoo/Alpha"]], Aron Griffis, gentoo-alpha, 28 Feb 2003)] Tavis Ormandy joined in April 2003, bringing ebuilds for the Compaq C and C++ compilers. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/gmane-2003-April.mbox#L683|"new dev: taviso"]], Aron Griffis, gentoo-alpha, 14 Apr 2003)] Alpha was not expected to be supported in Gentoo 1.4, whose focus was "sparc, ppc and of course x86". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/gmane-2003-August.mbox#L46|"Re: status of alpha port?"]], Tavis Ormandy, gentoo-alpha, 1 Aug 2003)]

The first Alpha LiveCD built with Gentoo's ''catalyst'' tool came from Jay Maynard in February 2004. It carried three kernels: one for normal use, one for systems booting with MILO, and one for the Jensen. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2004-February.mbox#L3612|"[gentoo-alpha] Alpha LiveCD available for testing"]], Jay Maynard, gentoo-alpha, 27 Feb 2004)] Bryan Østergaard (kloeri) took over release building in August 2004 [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2004-August.mbox#L696|"Re: [Fwd: Re: [gentoo-alpha] Problems running bootstrap.sh]"]], Jay Maynard, gentoo-alpha, 3 Aug 2004)] and led the port for the next few years. The 2005.0 LiveCD carried both 2.6 and 2.4 kernels because "a few systems still seems to have problems with 2.6 kernels". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/gmane-2005-January.mbox#L3179|"Re: alpha performance"]], Bryan Østergaard, gentoo-alpha, 29 Jan 2005)] Gentoo/Alpha dropped the Compaq Java runtime and development kit in July 2006, leaving the architecture without a full Java implementation, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2006-July.mbox#L485|"[gentoo-alpha] The end of compaq java. Current Gentoo/Alpha java situation."]], Jose Luis Rivero, gentoo-alpha, 13 Jul 2006)] and ended 2.4 kernel support on 19 January 2008, after every other Gentoo architecture team. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2008-January.mbox#L1|"[gentoo-alpha] End of 2.4 support in Gentoo/Alpha"]], Jose Luis Rivero, gentoo-alpha, 19 Jan 2008)] By mid 2007 Østergaard, "one of the oldest alpha Gentoo developers", had left Gentoo, and the arch tester Tobias Klausmann ran the port's main development machine. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2007-July.mbox#L68|"[gentoo-alpha] Gentoo/Alpha status report 2007"]], Jose Luis Rivero, gentoo-alpha, 21 Jul 2007)]

In September 2019 the port lost its build machine, a quad-CPU ES40 hosted without charge for many years. The replacements to hand were a DS20L that had blown a capacitor, an ES47 that was never stable enough, and 600 MHz DS10Ls, "pretty useless as a replacement". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2019-September.mbox#L1|"[gentoo-alpha] Gentoo/Alpha is without a build machine"]], Matt Turner, gentoo-alpha, 1 Sep 2019)] Turner, who had "helped maintain Gentoo's DEC Alpha port for more than 10 years", then proposed dropping the stable keywords, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2020-January.mbox#L1|"[gentoo-alpha] RFC: Dropping alpha keywords to unstable"]], Matt Turner, gentoo-alpha, 15 Jan 2020)] and every ''alpha'' keyword became ''~alpha'' on 25 January 2020, though the team had "no plans to remove support for the architecture". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2020-January.mbox#L90|"[gentoo-alpha] [RFC] News item: Stable alpha keywords removed"]], Matt Turner, gentoo-alpha, 21 Jan 2020)] The Alpha profiles were demoted to experimental on 17 May 2022, so as not to hold back the Python team. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2022-May.mbox#L1|"[gentoo-alpha] alpha profiles demoted to experimental"]], Matt Turner, gentoo-alpha, 17 May 2022)] They were restored to stable on 10 August 2024. [(>[[https://gitweb.gentoo.org/repo/gentoo.git/commit/?id=a507b96e70392fb7055a212fb39bda0bdff760df|"profiles: mark alpha as stable"]], Matoro Mahri, gentoo.git, 10 Aug 2024)]

=== Debian ===

[[https://www.debian.org/|Debian]] released Alpha as an official architecture from Debian 2.1 (Slink), in March 1999, [(>[[https://www.debian.org/releases/slink/|Debian GNU/Linux 2.1 ("slink")]], debian.org)] to Debian 5.0 (Lenny), in February 2009. [(>[[https://www.debian.org/releases/lenny/|Debian "lenny" Release Information]], debian.org)] Alpha was not a release architecture in Debian 6.0 (Squeeze) or later, and has continued since then as an unofficial port in [[https://www.debian.org/ports/alpha/|Debian Ports]], which builds the unstable distribution only. The port is maintained by [[user:creemj|Michael Cree]] and others.

Debian Ports publishes netinst CD images as dated snapshots. [(>[[https://cdimage.debian.org/cdimage/ports/snapshots/|cdimage/ports/snapshots]], cdimage.debian.org)] Since May 2023 Debian's GCC has generated EV56 code by default; see [[documentation:toolchains#distribution_baselines|Distribution baselines]]. Packages are built both on Alpha hardware and on QEMU. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2020-January.mbox#L2472|"Re: Booting Debian in qemu-system-alpha"]], John Paul Adrian Glaubitz, debian-alpha, 24 Jan 2020)] The port uses the [[https://lists.debian.org/debian-alpha/|debian-alpha]] mailing list.

=== T2 SDE ===

[[https://t2sde.org/|T2 SDE]] is a source-based system development environment that builds complete distributions for many architectures, among them Alpha. It publishes Alpha ISO images, built for EV56 since 2025, in several variants, such as a desktop and a minimal base system. [(>[[https://dl.t2sde.org/binary/|dl.t2sde.org/binary]], T2 SDE)]

==== Historical distributions ====

Many Linux distributions formerly supported Alpha. The table lists each one's last Alpha release. Dates and versions are taken from the mailing list archives described in [[history:community|Community History]].

^ Distribution ^ Last Alpha release ^ Notes ^
| BLADE | 0.3 | "Basic Linux/Alpha Distribution Eyesore", Digital's own Alpha/Linux distribution, largely the work of Jim Paradis. Version 0.1, released on 1 July 1995, was the first Alpha/Linux distribution that could be installed without a second machine, and was intended to serve until the established distributions were ported. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-July.mbox#L10349|"Linux/Alpha installable kit now available!"]], Jim Paradis, comp.os.linux.announce, 1 Jul 1995)] Version 0.2, on 9 August 1995, added TCP/IP networking and the first supported release of [[software:boot_loaders:milo|MILO]]. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L4599|"BLADE 0.2 now available! (Linux for Alpha)"]], Jim Paradis, comp.os.linux.announce, 9 Aug 1995)] Superseded during 1996 by Red Hat's Alpha release. |
| Craftworks Linux/AXP | 2.2 | Commercial distribution sold on CD, widely used on Alpha during 1996 and 1997. |
| Red Hat Linux | 7.2 | |
| SuSE Linux | 7.1 | 7.1 was the last shipping Alpha product. Former members of the SuSE Alpha team released an unofficial **SuSE Linux 8.1 Alpha Edition** in their own time, announced on 13 May 2003. |
| Mandrake Linux | 7.1 | Developed in the open on the cooker-axp list. A 7.2 release for Alpha was asked about in November 2000 but never appeared. |
| TurboLinux | 6.0 | Alpha edition shipped in July 2000. |
| AlphaCore | 3 | Unofficial port of Fedora Core to Alpha. Version 1.0, named "Svetlana" and based on Fedora Core 3, was released on 8 May 2005 by Balint Cristian and Sergey Tikhonov. |
| CentOS | 4 | The Alpha tree was declared end of life by its maintainer, Pasi Pirhonen, in August 2006. Packages continued to be rebuilt informally through RHEL 4 Update 4. The later CentOS 4 point releases on vault.centos.org carry an unchanged copy of this tree. |

A Slackware port to Alpha was attempted more than once, most seriously in 2001, but was never completed and had been abandoned by 2004.
