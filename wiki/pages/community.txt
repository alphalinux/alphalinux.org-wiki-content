===== Community =====

==== Mailing Lists ====

The Alpha/Linux kernel mailing list is **linux-alpha@vger.kernel.org**, hosted at [[https://subspace.kernel.org/vger.kernel.org.html|vger.kernel.org]] and managed by mlmmj. To subscribe, send an email to **linux-alpha+subscribe@vger.kernel.org**; the subject and body are ignored. Use **linux-alpha+subscribe-digest@vger.kernel.org** to receive a daily digest instead, and **linux-alpha+unsubscribe@vger.kernel.org** to unsubscribe. The list may also be read over NNTP at **nntp://nntp.lore.kernel.org**. Archives are located on [[https://lore.kernel.org/linux-alpha/|lore.kernel.org]], covering April 2002 onward, and [[https://marc.info/?l=linux-alpha|marc.info]], covering May 1998 onward.

Both [[https://archives.gentoo.org/gentoo-alpha/|Gentoo]] and [[https://lists.debian.org/debian-alpha/|Debian]] have distribution-related Alpha mailing lists.

=== Historic Lists ===

Several once popular Alpha Linux mailing lists are no longer hosted anywhere. The largest was **axp-list**, run by Red Hat from 22 November 1995 until its last message in February 2022, alongside the kernel-focused **axp-kernel-list**. Separate lists ran on alphalinux.org: **axp-hardware**, **axp-software**, **milo-list** (MILO development), and **high_perf** (High Performance Alpha Linux). Mandrake ran **cooker-axp** for the Alpha port of its Cooker development branch.

Mirrors of all of these, reconstructed from Pipermail and MHonArc pages, Gmane, and Wayback Machine captures, are preserved at [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives|alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives]] on GitHub. The same repository holds a complete mbox archive of the Debian **debian-alpha** list, which lists.debian.org serves only as per-message HTML.

^ List ^ Host ^ Coverage ^ Messages ^
| axp-list | Red Hat | November 1995 to February 2022 | 61,178 |
| axp-kernel-list | Red Hat | April 2002 to September 2003 | 137 |
| axp-hardware | alphalinux.org | December 1999 to September 2001 | 249 |
| axp-software | alphalinux.org | December 1999 to September 2001 | 116 |
| high_perf | alphalinux.org | November 1998 to November 2001 | 152 |
| milo-list | alphalinux.org | April 1999 to November 2001 | 89 |
| cooker-axp | Mandrake | July 2000 to November 2001 | 110 |
| debian-alpha | Debian | November 1995 to present | 19,902 |

No archive of axp-kernel-list from before April 2002 is known to survive. Headers in the surviving messages show it was a private Mailman list, and Red Hat's copy is gone.

=== Other Operating Systems ===

While not pertaining to Linux, Alpha hardware information may be found in the [[https://lists.freebsd.org/pipermail/freebsd-alpha/|FreeBSD Alpha]], [[https://mail-index.netbsd.org/port-alpha/|NetBSD Alpha]], [[http://marc.info/?l=openbsd-alpha|OpenBSD Alpha]] mailing lists.

==== IRC ====

A general Alpha discussion channel exists at **#alpha** on [[https://libera.chat/|Libera]]. Also, a Gentoo oriented channel exists at **#gentoo-alpha**, also on Libera.

==== Newsgroups ====

A few newsgroups exist for Alpha users, namely

  * [[https://groups.google.com/group/comp.os.linux.alpha/topics|comp.os.linux.alpha]] for discussion of running Linux on Alpha
  * [[https://groups.google.com/group/comp.sys.dec/topics|comp.sys.dec]] for discussion about DEC Computers
  * [[https://groups.google.com/group/linux.debian.ports.alpha|linux.debian.ports.alpha]], a gateway to the debian-alpha mailing list, for discussion of running Debian on Alpha

Google Groups stopped carrying new Usenet posts in February 2024, so these links serve historic content only.

While not pertaining to Linux or Alpha, hardware information may be found in the [[https://groups.google.com/group/comp.sys.vms/topics|comp.sys.vms]] and [[https://groups.google.com/group/comp.unix.tru64/topics|comp.unix.tru64]] newsgroups.

==== Forums ====

Gentoo has a [[https://forums.gentoo.org/viewforum.php?f=32|Gentoo on Alternative Architectures forum]].

==== Distributions ====

=== Current ===

[[https://www.gentoo.org/|Gentoo Linux]] has a maintained [[https://wiki.gentoo.org/wiki/Project:Alpha|Alpha port]].

Until [[https://www.debian.org/releases/lenny/|Debian Lenny]], [[https://www.debian.org/|Debian]] maintained an Alpha port. Support for Alpha is now maintained as part of [[https://www.debian.org/ports/alpha/|Debian Ports]].

=== Defunct ===

Many Linux distributions once supported Alpha, but do not any longer or have been discontinued. The following lists distributions that supported Alpha in the past and the last Alpha-supporting release. Dates and versions are taken from the mailing list archives described above.

^ Distribution ^ Last Alpha release ^ Notes ^
| BLADE | 0.3 | Digital's own Linux/Alpha distribution, largely the work of Jim Paradis. In use from late 1995 and superseded during 1996 by Red Hat's Alpha release. |
| Craftworks Linux/AXP | 2.2 | Commercial distribution sold on CD, widely used on Alpha during 1996 and 1997. |
| Red Hat Linux | 7.2 | |
| SuSE Linux | 7.1 | 7.1 was the last shipping Alpha product. Former members of the SuSE Alpha team released an unofficial **SuSE Linux 8.1 Alpha Edition** in their own time, announced on 13 May 2003. |
| Mandrake Linux | 7.1 | Developed in the open on the cooker-axp list. A 7.2 release for Alpha was asked about in November 2000 but never appeared. |
| TurboLinux | 6.0 | Alpha edition shipped in July 2000. |
| AlphaCore | 3 | Unofficial port of Fedora Core to Alpha. Version 1.0, named "Svetlana" and based on Fedora Core 3, was released on 8 May 2005 by Balint Cristian and Sergey Tikhonov. |
| CentOS | 4 | The Alpha tree was declared end of life by its maintainer, Pasi Pirhonen, in August 2006. Packages continued to be rebuilt informally through RHEL 4 Update 4. The later CentOS 4 point releases on vault.centos.org carry an unchanged copy of this tree. |

A Slackware port to Alpha was attempted more than once, most seriously in 2001, but was never completed and had been abandoned by 2004.

FreeBSD also discontinued their Alpha port after the 6.x series.
