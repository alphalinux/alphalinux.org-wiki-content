==== Notes ====

=== Alpha RISC Architecture for Programmers ===

Written by James S. Evans and Richard H. Eckhouse and published by Prentice Hall PTR (Upper Saddle River, NJ) in 1999, in the Hewlett-Packard Professional Books series. [(>[[https://www2.lawrence.edu/fast/EVANSJ/publications.html|Publication list for James S. Evans]], Lawrence University, accessed 19 Sep 2026)] Evans kept a companion page of additional and revised references for the book, last updated 16 June 2001, keyed to its thirteen chapters. It points at the {{wiki:hardware:cpu:21264:21264_hardware_reference_manual.pdf?linkonly|Alpha 21264 Hardware Reference Manual}} (EC-RJRZA-TE), the {{wiki:hardware:cpu:21264:21264_product_brief.pdf?linkonly|Alpha 21264 Microprocessor Product Brief}} (EC-R2YTB-TE), the {{wiki:hardware:cpu:21264:21264_compiler_writers_guide.pdf?linkonly|Compiler Writer's Guide for the Alpha 21264}}, Digital's MVI coding examples, Peter Bannon's 21364 paper in the //Compaq Technical Journal//, the //Communications of the ACM// Alpha chip project articles, and Compaq's Alpha Linux software pages. It also notes that the //Windows NT for Alpha AXP Calling Standard// had disappeared from Compaq's web site by 5 January 2001, and that some NT for Alpha documentation survived on Microsoft's MSDN site under the Visual C++ documentation even after Microsoft and Compaq abandoned the port in 1999. [(>[[https://www2.lawrence.edu/fast/EVANSJ/ARAFPREF.HTML|Alpha RISC Architecture for Programmers: Additional or Revised References]], James S. Evans, 16 Jun 2001)]

=== Alpha Implementations and Architecture ===

Written by Dileep P. Bhandarkar, who was directly involved in the design and development of Alpha and its predecessors at Digital, and published by Digital Press (Butterworth-Heinemann, Newton, MA) in 1996 at 328 pages. Daniel Tabak reviewed it for //ACM SIGARCH Computer Architecture News// and called it "an excellent and unique presentation of the Digital Alpha architecture, organization, implementation, and applications", valuable for having been written by an insider. The review lays out the contents: chapter 1 covers the history of Alpha's development at Digital, chapter 2 the design principles of modern computing systems, chapter 3 the Alpha architecture itself (registers, data types, instruction set and formats, addressing modes, memory management, exceptions and interrupts, PALcode) along with the design tradeoffs, chapter 4 a comparison with MIPS, SPARC, PA-RISC and PowerPC, chapter 5 the 21064 in detail together with the 21064A, 21066 and 21066A, chapter 6 thirty-three first generation 21064 and 21064A systems from desktop workstations to multiprocessor servers including the Cray T3D, chapter 7 the 21164 and its system applications, chapter 8 the author's simulation studies, chapter 9 a comparison of the top level implementations (21164, PowerPC 620, UltraSPARC, R10000, PA-8000, with some diagrams also covering the Pentium), and chapter 10 Digital's operating systems and compilers; an appendix lists the Alpha instruction encodings. Tabak's criticisms were that chapter 2 is too brief for a beginner with a thin reference list, that the claim on page 21 that "typical RISC machines often require 1.5 to 2.5 times the instructions required by a CISC machine" overstates the case, and that the definition of "superpipelined" on page 36 is vague next to Jouppi's. He also noted that no details of Intel's Pentium Pro were available when the book was written, so a valid comparison with the 21164 was still to come. [(>[[https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/232790.564994|"Book Review: Alpha Implementations and Architecture by Dileep P. Bhandarkar"]], Daniel Tabak, //ACM SIGARCH Computer Architecture News//, vol. 24, no. 1, Mar 1996, pp. 17-18)]
