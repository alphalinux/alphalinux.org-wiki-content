===== Companies =====

This page covers the companies that designed, built, sold, and supported Alpha hardware and software, and their part in Linux on Alpha. See also [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]] for contemporary news articles.

==== Digital, Compaq, and HP ====

=== Digital Equipment Corporation ===

Digital designed the Alpha architecture and its processors, and built AlphaServer and AlphaStation systems. Its Digital Semiconductor business, in Hudson, Massachusetts, sold processors, chipsets, evaluation boards, and OEM motherboards: "Digital Semiconductor is a Digital Equipment Corporation business." [(>{{wiki:hardware:cpu:21064:21064a_data_sheet.pdf?linkonly|21064A Data Sheet}}, January 1996, p. ii)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:cpu:21164:ev5_functional_specification_rev_1.9.pdf?linkonly|EV5 Functional Specification}}, December 1992, p. 1)] Digital Semiconductor's designs were "sold by Digital, resold by resellers, cloned and improved upon". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-July.mbox#L956|"Re: questions on the PC164"]], David A Rusling, axp-list, 12 Jul 1996)]

Digital also ran the main Alpha/Linux FTP archive, and its engineers, among them David Rusling, Jay Estabrook, and Jim Paradis, did much of the early porting work, which they sent upstream for inclusion in the main kernel tree. [(>''README'', Linux-Alpha archive, ftp.digital.com, 4 Apr 1996)] The team came from Digital Semiconductor's Alpha Migration Tools group, whose other projects were DECmigrate, FreePort Express, and JavaOS. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1996-December.mbox#L22900|"FreePort Express no longer available on Novalink"]], Richard Gorton, alpha-osf-managers, 3 Dec 1996)]

The team's distribution, BLADE, first released on 1 July 1995, was the first Alpha/Linux distribution that could be installed without a second machine, and was meant to serve until the established distributions were ported; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-July.mbox#L10349|"Linux/Alpha installable kit now available!"]], Jim Paradis, comp.os.linux.announce, 1 Jul 1995)] version 0.2, in August 1995, added networking and the first supported release of [[software:boot_loaders:milo|MILO]]. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L4599|"BLADE 0.2 now available! (Linux for Alpha)"]], Jim Paradis, comp.os.linux.announce, 9 Aug 1995)] In April 1997 the team released EM86, which ran Linux/x86 programs using components of Digital's FX!32 translator for Windows NT. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-April.mbox#L10643|"EM86: Official Announcement"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 4 Apr 1997)]

Digital did not formally support Linux, partly so as not to favor one distribution over another, but supported it with engineering work on new systems, boot code, and drivers, from a staff of four full-time engineers in 1997. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1997-May.mbox#L24457|"Digital and its support of Linux"]], Jon 'maddog' Hall, alpha-osf-managers, 5 May 1997)]

Digital sued Intel for patent infringement in May 1997, and the two settled in October 1997. Under the settlement, Digital sold its Alpha fabrication plant in Hudson to Intel, which agreed to manufacture Alpha chips for Digital. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L50829|"IBM & Alpha, looks good!"]], Stephen J. Gaudet, axp-list, 23 Jun 1998)] The Federal Trade Commission approved the deal in April 1998, on the condition that Digital also talk to Samsung, AMD, and IBM about manufacturing Alphas. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-April.mbox#L38781|"DEC/Intel deal includes AMD, Samsung. FTC okays."]], Christopher Curtis, axp-list, 24 Apr 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L50829|"IBM & Alpha, looks good!"]], Stephen J. Gaudet, axp-list, 23 Jun 1998)]

=== Compaq ===

Compaq's purchase of Digital was announced in January 1998, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L30690|"Re: Compaq's Purchase of Digital: Let the dust settle"]], Jon 'maddog' Hall, axp-list, 26 Jan 1998)] and by June 1998 Digital was "now officially part of Compaq". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L50829|"IBM & Alpha, looks good!"]], Stephen J. Gaudet, axp-list, 23 Jun 1998)] Compaq closed Digital Semiconductor's OEM business, and resellers came to depend on Alpha Processor, Inc. instead. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L78839|"Re: OT: Low end resellers in trouble"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 26 Mar 1999)] Compaq also released development tools for Alpha/Linux: a beta of Visual Threads, its rule-based analysis tool for debugging and profiling pthreads applications, was offered as a free download for Linux on Alpha and IA32 in April 2002. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-2002-April.mbox#L164|"ANNOUNCE: Visual Threads for Linux Alpha & IA32 Beta - debug/analyze pthread applications"]], Jerry Harrow, comp.os.linux.announce, 17 Apr 2002)]

Compaq's support for Linux on Alpha included:

  * It donated an Alpha to Debian in October 1998, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L8925|"Alpha machine donated by Compaq now available to developers"]], Paul Slootman, debian-alpha, 29 Oct 1998)] and gave Linus Torvalds an AlphaServer DS20 at LinuxWorld in March 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L8784|"Compaq announcement at Linux World"]], Bill Roman, axp-list, 3 Mar 1999)]
  * It released the Compaq Portable Math Library (CPML) for Linux in 1999, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/high_perf-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L439|"Free Compaq Math Library for Linux"]], Andrei A. Dergatchev, high_perf, 3 Mar 1999)] and in December 1999 Compaq C V6.2, free under a license agreement, and Compaq Fortran V1.0, free for non-commercial use and otherwise $399. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L32196|"Compaq C V6.2 for Linux Alpha is available"]], Rich Peterson, axp-list, 16 Dec 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L31455|"Compaq Fortran for Linux Alpha V1.0"]], Mark Arrasmith, axp-list, 16 Dec 1999)] Both had been in public beta since September 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-alpha-mbox/googlegroups-1999-September.mbox#L40458|"Compaq announces public Beta test for Linux Alpha C compiler"]], Compaq C/C++ Team, comp.os.linux.alpha, 10 Sep 1999)] See [[documentation:toolchains|Toolchains]].
  * It made the Tru64 UNIX build of Netscape Communicator available for Linux, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-January.mbox#L37660|"Netscape communicator for Alpha Linux is now available from Compa q."]], John Jemiolo, axp-list, 27 Jan 2000)] and worked out a license with Red Hat so that the libraries it needed could be redistributed. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-August.mbox#L19860|"Re: Bringing Tru64 UNIX software technology to Linux"]], Matt Wilson, axp-list, 25 Aug 2000)]
  * It marketed AlphaServer systems such as the DS10 with Linux as one of their operating systems. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_ds10:alphaserver_ds10_brochure.pdf?linkonly|Compaq AlphaServer DS10}}, Compaq, June 1999, p. 6)]
  * In August 2000, it released the Alpha Linux JumpStart! installation CD as an open source project. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-August.mbox#L10082|"Announcing Alpha Linux JumpStart! as an open source project"]], Beth Zeranski, axp-list, 16 Aug 2000)]

In August 1999, Compaq stopped its work on 32-bit Windows NT for Alpha (see [[#microsoft|Microsoft]]). In June 2001, Compaq announced that it would sell its Alpha technology to Intel and move Tru64 UNIX, OpenVMS, and NonStop Kernel to Intel's Itanium. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L30811|"Re: Compaq/Intel vs API"]], Island Computers US Corp, axp-list, 25 Jun 2001)] [(>''Intel takes Alpha from Compaq's hands'', The Register, 25 Jun 2001, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])] Compaq's Tru64 UNIX group said that an engineering study had concluded that "by 2004, Alpha's performance would not be dramatically different from the Itanium chip from Intel". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L34673|"Compaq/Intel agreement... postings on this list"]], Paul Henderson, tru64-unix-managers, 27 Jun 2001)] The EV7, due in late 2002, was unaffected by the agreement, but development of the EV8 stopped. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L34268|"Compaq/Intel agreement... postings on this list"]], Vincent D'Antonio, tru64-unix-managers, 27 Jun 2001)]

=== Hewlett-Packard ===

HP acquired Compaq in May 2002. Alpha documentation from then on notes that "Compaq Computer Corporation is a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Hewlett-Packard Company." [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_gs140_v6.4_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer GS60/GS60E/GS140 V6.4 Firmware Release Notes}}, December 2002, p. 2)] HP released the EV7-based AlphaServer ES47, ES80, and GS1280, [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_es47_es80_gs1280_srm_console_reference.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer ES47/ES80/GS1280 SRM Console Reference}}, December 2002, p. 2)] and the last Alpha firmware update, V7.3, in April 2007. [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alpha_systems_firmware_update_v7.3_release_notes_overview.pdf?linkonly|Alpha Systems Firmware Update V7.3 Release Notes Overview}}, April 2007, p. 2)] It also sold the Red Hat Linux 7.2 CD for Alpha (see [[#red_hat|Red Hat]]).

In December 2002 HP publicly committed to making and selling Alpha systems running Tru64 UNIX until about 2006, and to supporting Tru64 UNIX on Alpha until about 2011, "provided there is continued customer demand". It also promised "a product refresh with the EV79 processors during the life of these systems". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/gmane-2002-December.mbox#L17609|"Summary: Some questions regarding Alpha and Itanium"]], Robert Honore, tru64-unix-managers, 19 Dec 2002)] HP's plans for Tru64 UNIX itself are described under [[software:operating_systems:tru64#under_hp|Tru64 UNIX]].

==== Processor manufacturers ====

=== Mitsubishi Electric ===

Mitsubishi was a licensed second source for Alpha processors, and announced a 200 MHz version of the 21066. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-June.mbox#L9904|"Re: Multia/UDB Comments"]], Eric Smith, axp-list, 20 Jun 1996)] It designed the 21164PC together with Digital Semiconductor and had the rights to build and sell it; Digital announced the chip on 17 March 1997, to be sold by Digital Semiconductor, Mitsubishi, and Samsung. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-March.mbox#L1113|"Press/Alpha is Launched into the Volume WinNT PC Market w/ 21164PC"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 19 Mar 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-October.mbox#L41299|"21164PC & 21264 info"]], David Mosberger-Tang, axp-list, 25 Oct 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L3421|"Re: Intel (was Re: New Boards)"]], Richard Gorton, axp-list, 5 Jan 1998)] Mitsubishi also made [[hardware:motherboards:pc164sx|AlphaPC 164SX]] boards under license. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-August.mbox#L6772|"RE: Samsung EV69"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 8 Aug 2002)] In July 1998 it stopped making and marketing its low-cost Alpha processor, blaming in part the Asian financial crisis. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-July.mbox#L27739|"Re: FW: Redhat 5.1 on Multi-Platforms (or Yes, we want ALPHA sw!)"]], Thunderhawk Alpha, axp-list, 11 Jul 1998)]

=== Samsung ===

Samsung was licensed to market Alpha processors in 1996. Digital and Samsung announced the agreement on 18 June 1996, describing it as an alliance "to develop and support a high volume market for products based on Digital's industry-leading 64-bit Alpha RISC architecture"; no financial terms were given. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-June.mbox#L1749|"Press/Digital and Samsung Announce Alpha Microprocessor Agreement"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 27 Jun 1996)] [(>''Samsung Licensed to Market Digital's Alpha Microprocessors'', NY Times, 19 Jun 1996, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])] It made its own 21164 processors, from the 466 MHz KP21164-466CN to the 667 MHz KP21164-667CN, and sold the [[hardware:motherboards:pc164lx|AlphaPC 164LX]] and [[hardware:motherboards:pc164ux|AlphaPC 164UX and 164BX]] motherboards. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:pc164ux:pc164ux_windows_nt_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|AlphaPC 164UX/BX Motherboard Windows NT User's Manual}}, Samsung, 1998, p. B-2)] Samsung's literature listed Windows NT and Linux as the software options. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-December.mbox#L8949|"Re: EIDE performance"]], Maurice W. Hilarius, axp-list, 6 Dec 1997)] It also made 21264 processors, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-April.mbox#L17119|"Samsung now producing the 21264."]], Harvey J. Stein, axp-list, 12 Apr 1998)] and a Digital product brief from 1998 names Intel and Samsung as the 21264's sources. [(>{{wiki:hardware:cpu:21264:21264_product_brief.pdf?linkonly|21264 Product Brief}}, 9 Sep 1998, p. 1)]

Samsung fabricated Alphas in Korea, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-January.mbox#L17653|"RE: FYI: Howard Elias replaces Bill Heil as BCSG head - Alpha's n ew boss"]], Mike Foley, axp-list, 17 Jan 2001)] using a 0.25 micron process, "significantly better than the Hudson fab's 0.35 micron process." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-January.mbox#L23466|"RE: FYI: Howard Elias replaces Bill Heil as BCSG head - Alpha's new boss"]], Greg Lindahl, axp-list, 22 Jan 2001)] In 1998, it set up Alpha Processor, Inc. (see below). [(>''Samsung announces Alpha subsidiary'', 16 Jun 1998, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])] Samsung developed the [[hardware:motherboards:up1000|UP1000]] and [[hardware:motherboards:up1100|UP1100]] with API, which sold them, and later the [[hardware:motherboards:up1500|UP1500]] with AMD's 761 chipset. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-August.mbox#L6772|"RE: Samsung EV69"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 8 Aug 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L28391|"Re: Compaq/Intel vs API"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 25 Jun 2001)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1500:up1500_user_s_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP1500 User's Manual}}, Samsung, May 2001, p. ii)] In 2002, Samsung disbanded its motherboard design group, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-July.mbox#L19874|"RE: Samsung EV7"]], David Mihail, axp-list, 31 Jul 2002)] and by 2003 it had stopped making Alpha boards. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-January.mbox#L4438|"Re: UP1500?"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 8 Jan 2003)]

=== Intel ===

Intel bought Digital's Hudson fabrication plant in the 1997 settlement, and was obliged to make Alpha processors for Digital, and only for Digital. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L3421|"Re: Intel (was Re: New Boards)"]], Richard Gorton, axp-list, 5 Jan 1998)] In 2001, Intel took over Compaq's Alpha technology (see [[#compaq|Compaq]]).

=== IBM ===

IBM negotiated an Alpha license in 1998, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L50829|"IBM & Alpha, looks good!"]], Stephen J. Gaudet, axp-list, 23 Jun 1998)] and fabricated Alpha processors for Compaq from 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L28100|"IBM to build Alphas"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 17 Feb 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L28391|"Re: Compaq/Intel vs API"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 25 Jun 2001)]

=== AMD ===

AMD licensed the 21264's EV6 bus from Digital. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-November.mbox#L7800|"RE: Alpha cluster?"]], Paul Bunn, axp-list, 3 Nov 1999)] Its AMD-751 and AMD-761 north bridges, designed for the Athlon, also support Alpha processors, [(>{{wiki:hardware:chipsets:amd-761:amd-761_software_bios_design_guide.pdf?linkonly|AMD-761 Software/BIOS Design Guide}}, February 2002, p. 9)] and were used in the UP1000, UP1100, and UP1500. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-June.mbox#L21165|"RE: Alpha motherboards ?"]], Mike Foley, axp-list, 28 Jun 2000)] Alpha Processor's Slot B modules were meant to share a motherboard design with AMD's Athlon. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-April.mbox#L17119|"Samsung now producing the 21264."]], Harvey J. Stein, axp-list, 12 Apr 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-October.mbox#L8382|"OpenBoot, SRM, AlphaBIOS and Slot B"]], Kirk R. Erichsen, axp-list, 6 Oct 1999)] AMD dropped the EV6 bus for its Hammer processors in 2001. [(>''AMD drops EV6 for Hammer bus'', The Register, 25 Jan 2001, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])]

==== Alpha Processor, Inc. and API NetWorks ====

Alpha Processor, Inc. (API), a Samsung subsidiary, was based in Concord, Massachusetts. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:pc164lx:pc164lx_product_brief.pdf?linkonly|AlphaPC 164LX Product Brief}}, Alpha Processor, Inc., January 1999, p. 2)] It announced a commitment to the Linux market in January 1999, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-January.mbox#L15586|"simple questions from a New User"]], Hooman Hodjat, axp-list, 8 Jan 1999)] and several of its employees, among them Stig Telfer, Tom Morris, and Mike Foley, took part in the mailing lists.

API sold Samsung's processors and boards, and boards and systems that it developed alone or with Samsung:

  * [[hardware:motherboards:up1000|UP1000]] (manual dated December 1999) and [[hardware:motherboards:up1100|UP1100]] (May 2000), developed with Samsung, [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1000:up1000_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP1000 User Manual}}, 10 Dec 1999, p. ii)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1100:up1100_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP1100 User Manual}}, 31 May 2000, p. ii)]
  * [[hardware:motherboards:up2000|UP2000]] (April 2000) and [[hardware:motherboards:up2000#up2000plus|UP2000+]] (June 2000), [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up2000:up2000_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP2000 User Manual}}, 7 Apr 2000, p. ii)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up2000plus:up2000plus_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP2000+ User Manual}}, 12 Jan 2001, p. iii)]
  * the CS20, a 1U dual-processor server, which Cray used as the building block of its SuperCluster systems. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-January.mbox#L31275|"RE: Cray chooses Alpha Linux ..."]], Tom Morris, axp-list, 30 Jan 2001)]

API also wrote the APB boot loader for the UP1000, [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1000:up1000_technical_reference_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP1000 Technical Reference Manual}}, December 1999)] and partnered with MandrakeSoft (see [[#mandrakesoft|MandrakeSoft]]). The company was renamed API NetWorks, Inc. at the end of 2000. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up2000plus:up2000plus_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP2000+ User Manual}}, 12 Jan 2001, p. iii)] [(>''Alpha Changes Name; Debuts Server, 833MHz Processor'', Electronics Weekly, 7 Nov 2000, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])]

API NetWorks closed around the end of 2001. Microway bought its product assets and took over its warranties. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-May.mbox#L9331|"Re: Burning Alpha!"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 10 May 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-August.mbox#L8635|"Re: no more alpha letter on inquirer"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 9 Aug 2002)]

==== Board and system makers ====

  * **DeskStation Technology** was "historically a MIPS house" that built Alpha systems with its own system logic and its own Windows NT firmware and HAL. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L5967|"re: Linux on a Deskstation Raptor 3 - System?"]], Neal Crook, axp-list, 20 Feb 1996)] Its Alpha line, all of its own design down to the chipset, ran from the Raptor 3 through the Raptor Reflex to the dual-processor EV56 Renegade. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-June.mbox#L2856|"DeskStation Raptor Reflex 400 MHz"]], Nebojsa Novakovic, alphant, 9 Jun 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-July.mbox#L5737|"RE: Autodesk's Commitment to AlphaNT..."]], Charles J. McCallion, alphant, 14 Jul 1996)] It designed the Ruffian, later sold as the [[hardware:motherboards:pc164ux|AlphaPC 164UX]], and hired Dean Hall in 1997 to port Linux to it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-July.mbox#L2762|"Re: Deskstation Ruffian compatibility"]], Dean Hall, axp-list, 3 Jul 1997)] The rights to the Ruffian and its ARCSBIOS firmware passed to Samsung or API (sources disagree; see [[#conflicting_information|Conflicting information]]), [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-hardware-mbox/2000-July.mbox#L32|"[Axp-hardware] Re: What happened to Deskstation?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-hardware, 20 Jul 2000)] and DeskStation had gone out of business by 2000. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2000-September.mbox#L3310|"Re: Dekstation Raptor Reflex"]], Ron Farrer, debian-alpha, 13 Sep 2000)]
  * **Aspen Systems**, of Wheat Ridge, Colorado, built boards and systems based on Digital Semiconductor designs, such as the Alpine, Timberline, and Durango (see [[hardware:motherboards:eb64plus|EB64+]] and [[hardware:motherboards:pc164|PC164]]). [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-November.mbox#L10308|"Alphas"]], Randy Perry, axp-list, 5 Nov 1996)] It announced its own dual-processor EV56 board, the Twin-Peaks, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-November.mbox#L12328|"Re: Unidentified subject!"]], Randy Perry, axp-list, 6 Nov 1996)] and later resold the API NetWorks CS20. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-November.mbox#L13340|"Re: 1U DUAL 833MHz Alpha"]], Tom Heibel, axp-list, 7 Nov 2000)]

==== Linux distributions ====

=== Red Hat ===

Red Hat released Red Hat Linux/AXP in December 1995, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1995-December.mbox#L569|"Re: Any new ETA on ftp release?"]], Erik Troan, axp-list, 14 Dec 1995)] announced as release 2.1 BETA. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-December.mbox#L9990|"COMMERCIAL: Red Hat Linux/AXP 2.1 BETA"]], Erik Troan, comp.os.linux.announce, 8 Dec 1995)] The first official release followed on 27 January 1996. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L1353|""Official" RH Linux for Digital ALPHA Computers."]], Red Hat Software, comp.os.linux.announce, 27 Jan 1996)] Red Hat released 3.0.3 in June 1996, which could be installed from CD without a floppy on most Alpha hardware. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-June.mbox#L386|"Red Hat Linux/AXP 3.0.3 available for FTP"]], Marc Ewing, axp-list, 2 Jun 1996)] Red Hat Linux 4.0, in October 1996, was released for Intel, Alpha, and SPARC at the same time, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-October.mbox#L4753|"Red Hat Linux/Alpha 4.0 Available Now! (fwd)"]], Erik Troan, axp-list, 4 Oct 1996)] and the 4.x updates kept Alpha in step with the other two. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-February.mbox#L8053|"COMMERCIAL: Red Hat Linux 4.1 for Intel, Alpha, and SPARC!"]], Red Hat Software, comp.os.linux.announce, 7 Feb 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-May.mbox#L8142|"COMMERCIAL: Red Hat Linux 4.2 for Intel, Alpha, and SPARC"]], Red Hat Software, comp.os.linux.announce, 22 May 1997)] Red Hat also hosted axp-list, //"Linux and Red Hat on Alpha processors"//. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-May.mbox#L9331|"Re: Burning Alpha!"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 10 May 2002)]

Red Hat Linux 7.2 was the last release for Alpha. In January 2002, Red Hat said it had no plans "to port the full Red Hat Linux OS to Alpha", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L5659|"Is this really a good news ??"]], MILLET Mathieu, axp-list, 11 Jan 2002)] and the 7.2 CD for Alpha was only sold by HP, for $29, without support. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-August.mbox#L1|"Red Hat Linux 7.2 for Alpha CD Media Kit now available"]], John Goshdigian, axp-list, 31 Jul 2002)]

==== Software vendors ====

=== Microsoft ===

Windows NT ran on Alpha systems with the [[software:firmware:arc|ARC or AlphaBIOS]] firmware. In August 1999, InfoWorld reported that Compaq had laid off more than 100 people in Redmond who ported Windows NT to Alpha, and "effectively discontinued support of Microsoft's operating system on Alpha." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L31035|"Re: Done: Experience with large IDE drive and Promise Ultra/66 card"]], Jeff Bolle, axp-list, 22 Aug 1999)] According to Phillip Ezolt of Compaq, Compaq dropped only the 32-bit version, and Microsoft then dropped the 64-bit version. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-September.mbox#L54447|"Re: Off topic: NT on Alpha (was: Alpha Users in the Toronto area.)"]], Phillip Ezolt, axp-list, 30 Sep 1999)]
