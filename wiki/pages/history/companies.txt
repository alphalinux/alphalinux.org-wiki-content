===== Companies =====

Alpha hardware and software were designed, built, sold, and supported by a range of companies, described below with an emphasis on Linux. See also [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]] for contemporary news articles.

==== Digital, Compaq, and HP ====

=== Digital Equipment Corporation ===

Digital designed the Alpha architecture and its processors, and built AlphaServer and AlphaStation systems. Its Digital Semiconductor business, in Hudson, Massachusetts, sold processors, chipsets, evaluation boards, and OEM motherboards: "Digital Semiconductor is a Digital Equipment Corporation business." [(>{{wiki:hardware:cpu:21064:21064a_data_sheet.pdf?linkonly|21064A Data Sheet}}, January 1996, p. ii)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:cpu:21164:ev5_functional_specification_rev_1.9.pdf?linkonly|EV5 Functional Specification}}, December 1992, p. 1)] Digital Semiconductor's designs were "sold by Digital, resold by resellers, cloned and improved upon". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-July.mbox#L956|"Re: questions on the PC164"]], David A Rusling, axp-list, 12 Jul 1996)]

Digital reported shipping the 100,000th Alpha system on 11 April 1995, by which point product and services revenues from the line had "surpassed $3 billion"; Robert Palmer said Digital had shipped nearly 13,000 AlphaServer 2100-class systems in the preceding twelve months and expected to reach $4.5 billion "before our major competitors -- HP, IBM and Sun -- ship their first 64-bit system". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L924|"Press/Digital Ships 100,000th Alpha System"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 17 Apr 1995)] Prices fell steeply as the line matured: on 9 January 1997 Digital cut AlphaServer prices by as much as 47 percent, AlphaStation prices by 42 percent, and memory options by 55 percent, and introduced base-level server configurations with up to four times the memory of the ones they replaced. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-January.mbox#L409|"Press/Aggressive Digital Pricing Dramatically Increases Affordability of Alpha Computer Line"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 10 Jan 1997)]

Digital also ran the main Linux/Alpha FTP archive, and its engineers, among them David Rusling, Jay Estabrook, and Jim Paradis, did much of the early porting work. "Alpha support is now part of the base kernel distribution, and all of the work at Digital is sent to Linus for inclusion in the main source tree." [(>''README'', Linux-Alpha archive, ftp.digital.com, 4 Apr 1996)] In April 1997, Digital's Linux/Alpha team released EM86, which ran Linux/x86 programs using components of Digital's FX!32 translator for Windows NT. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-April.mbox#L10643|"EM86: Official Announcement"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 4 Apr 1997)]

Digital sued Intel for patent infringement in May 1997, and the two settled in October 1997. Under the settlement, Digital sold its Alpha fabrication plant in Hudson to Intel, which agreed to manufacture Alpha chips for Digital. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L50829|"IBM & Alpha, looks good!"]], Stephen J. Gaudet, axp-list, 23 Jun 1998)] The Federal Trade Commission approved the deal in April 1998, on the condition that Digital also talk to Samsung, AMD, and IBM about manufacturing Alphas. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-April.mbox#L38781|"DEC/Intel deal includes AMD, Samsung. FTC okays."]], Christopher Curtis, axp-list, 24 Apr 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L50829|"IBM & Alpha, looks good!"]], Stephen J. Gaudet, axp-list, 23 Jun 1998)]

=== Compaq ===

Compaq's purchase of Digital was announced in January 1998, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L30690|"Re: Compaq's Purchase of Digital: Let the dust settle"]], Jon 'maddog' Hall, axp-list, 26 Jan 1998)] and by June 1998 Digital was "now officially part of Compaq". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L50829|"IBM & Alpha, looks good!"]], Stephen J. Gaudet, axp-list, 23 Jun 1998)] Some Digital Semiconductor documents continued to carry Digital's copyright into 1999, while others from late 1998 carry Compaq's. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:pc164rx:pc164rx_product_brief.pdf?linkonly|AlphaPC 164RX Product Brief}}, 16 Oct 1998, p. 2)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:chipsets:21272:21272_hardware_reference_manual.pdf?linkonly|21272 Hardware Reference Manual}}, October 1999, p. ii)] Compaq closed Digital Semiconductor's OEM business, and resellers came to depend on Alpha Processor, Inc. instead. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L78839|"Re: OT: Low end resellers in trouble"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 26 Mar 1999)] Compaq also released development tools for Alpha Linux: a beta of Visual Threads, its rule-based analysis tool for debugging and profiling pthreads applications, was offered as a free download for Linux on Alpha and IA32 in April 2002. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-2002-April.mbox#L164|"ANNOUNCE: Visual Threads for Linux Alpha & IA32 Beta - debug/analyze pthread applications"]], Jerry Harrow, comp.os.linux.announce, 17 Apr 2002)]

Compaq's support for Linux on Alpha included:

  * It donated an Alpha to Debian in October 1998, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L8925|"Alpha machine donated by Compaq now available to developers"]], Paul Slootman, debian-alpha, 29 Oct 1998)] and gave Linus Torvalds an AlphaServer DS20 at LinuxWorld in March 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L8784|"Compaq announcement at Linux World"]], Bill Roman, axp-list, 3 Mar 1999)]
  * It released the Compaq Portable Math Library (CPML) for Linux in 1999, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/high_perf-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L439|"Free Compaq Math Library for Linux"]], Andrei A. Dergatchev, high_perf, 3 Mar 1999)] and in December 1999 Compaq C V6.2, free under a license agreement, and Compaq Fortran V1.0, free for non-commercial use and otherwise $399. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L32196|"Compaq C V6.2 for Linux Alpha is available"]], Rich Peterson, axp-list, 16 Dec 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L31455|"Compaq Fortran for Linux Alpha V1.0"]], Mark Arrasmith, axp-list, 16 Dec 1999)] Both compilers had been in public beta since September 1999, distributed as RPMs and tested mainly against Red Hat 5.2, and offered "the same state-of-the-art optimization technology for the Alpha processor as offered by the very latest Compaq compilers on Compaq's proprietary Alpha platforms", with some gcc compatibility in language features and command line handling. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-alpha-mbox/googlegroups-1999-September.mbox#L40458|"Compaq announces public Beta test for Linux Alpha C compiler"]], Compaq C/C++ Team, comp.os.linux.alpha, 10 Sep 1999)] Compaq Fortran was a full Fortran 95 implementation sharing the Tru64 UNIX, OpenVMS and Windows compiler, shipped with the Ladebug debugger and the Compaq Extended Math Library (CXML), and supported neither OpenMP nor HPF. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-alpha-mbox/googlegroups-1999-December.mbox#L13399|"Compaq Fortran for Linux Alpha Systems now available"]], Steve Lionel, comp.os.linux.alpha, 17 Dec 1999)] It was expected to be at least twice as fast as g77 for floating point code. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-April.mbox#L10804|"Re: 2x faster compiler for the Alpha!"]], Robert Harley, axp-list, 6 Apr 1999)]
  * It made the Tru64 UNIX build of Netscape Communicator available for Linux, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-January.mbox#L37660|"Netscape communicator for Alpha Linux is now available from Compa q."]], John Jemiolo, axp-list, 27 Jan 2000)] and worked out a license with Red Hat so that the libraries it needed could be redistributed. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-August.mbox#L19860|"Re: Bringing Tru64 UNIX software technology to Linux"]], Matt Wilson, axp-list, 25 Aug 2000)]
  * It marketed AlphaServer systems such as the DS10 with Linux as one of their operating systems. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_ds10:alphaserver_ds10_brochure.pdf?linkonly|Compaq AlphaServer DS10}}, Compaq, June 1999, p. 6)]
  * In August 2000, it released the Alpha Linux JumpStart! installation CD as an open source project. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-August.mbox#L10082|"Announcing Alpha Linux JumpStart! as an open source project"]], Beth Zeranski, axp-list, 16 Aug 2000)]

In August 1999, Compaq stopped its work on 32-bit Windows NT for Alpha (see [[#microsoft|Microsoft]]). In June 2001, Compaq announced that it would sell its Alpha technology to Intel and move Tru64 UNIX, OpenVMS, and NonStop Kernel to Intel's Itanium. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L30811|"Re: Compaq/Intel vs API"]], Island Computers US Corp, axp-list, 25 Jun 2001)] [(>''Intel takes Alpha from Compaq's hands'', The Register, 25 Jun 2001, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])]

=== Hewlett-Packard ===

HP merged with Compaq in 2002. Alpha documentation from then on notes that "Compaq Computer Corporation is a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Hewlett-Packard Company." [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_gs140_v6.4_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer GS60/GS60E/GS140 V6.4 Firmware Release Notes}}, December 2002, p. 2)] HP released the EV7-based AlphaServer ES47, ES80, and GS1280, [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_es47_es80_gs1280_srm_console_reference.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer ES47/ES80/GS1280 SRM Console Reference}}, December 2002, p. 2)] and the last Alpha firmware update, V7.3, in April 2007. [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alpha_systems_firmware_update_v7.3_release_notes_overview.pdf?linkonly|Alpha Systems Firmware Update V7.3 Release Notes Overview}}, April 2007, p. 2)] It also sold the Red Hat Linux 7.2 CD for Alpha (see [[#red_hat|Red Hat]]).

==== Processor manufacturers ====

=== Mitsubishi Electric ===

Mitsubishi was a licensed second source for Alpha processors, and announced a 200 MHz version of the 21066. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-June.mbox#L9904|"Re: Multia/UDB Comments"]], Eric Smith, axp-list, 20 Jun 1996)] It designed the 21164PC together with Digital Semiconductor and had the rights to build and sell it. Digital announced the chip on 17 March 1997 as a part "co-designed with Mitsubishi Electric Corporation", to be "marketed and sold by Digital Semiconductor, Mitsubishi and Samsung Electronics", priced under $300 so that Alpha PCs could sell for less than $2,600. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-March.mbox#L1113|"Press/Alpha is Launched into the Volume WinNT PC Market w/ 21164PC"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 19 Mar 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-October.mbox#L41299|"21164PC & 21264 info"]], David Mosberger-Tang, axp-list, 25 Oct 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L3421|"Re: Intel (was Re: New Boards)"]], Richard Gorton, axp-list, 5 Jan 1998)] According to Maurice Hilarius, Mitsubishi made 21164PC processors and [[hardware:motherboards:pc164sx|AlphaPC 164SX]] boards under license. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-August.mbox#L6772|"RE: Samsung EV69"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 8 Aug 2002)] In July 1998, InfoWorld reported that Mitsubishi had stopped making and marketing its low-cost Alpha processor, "blaming in part the Asian financial crisis." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-July.mbox#L27739|"Re: FW: Redhat 5.1 on Multi-Platforms (or Yes, we want ALPHA sw!)"]], Thunderhawk Alpha, axp-list, 11 Jul 1998)]

=== Samsung ===

Samsung was licensed to market Alpha processors in 1996. Digital and Samsung announced the agreement on 18 June 1996, describing it as an alliance "to develop and support a high volume market for products based on Digital's industry-leading 64-bit Alpha RISC architecture"; no financial terms were given. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-June.mbox#L1749|"Press/Digital and Samsung Announce Alpha Microprocessor Agreement"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 27 Jun 1996)] [(>''Samsung Licensed to Market Digital's Alpha Microprocessors'', NY Times, 19 Jun 1996, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])] It made its own 21164 processors, from the 466 MHz KP21164-466CN to the 667 MHz KP21164-667CN, and sold the [[hardware:motherboards:pc164lx|AlphaPC 164LX]] and [[hardware:motherboards:pc164ux|AlphaPC 164UX and 164BX]] motherboards. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:pc164ux:pc164ux_windows_nt_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|AlphaPC 164UX/BX Motherboard Windows NT User's Manual}}, Samsung, 1998, p. B-2)] Samsung's literature listed Windows NT and Linux as the software options. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-December.mbox#L8949|"Re: EIDE performance"]], Maurice W. Hilarius, axp-list, 6 Dec 1997)] It also made 21264 processors, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-April.mbox#L17119|"Samsung now producing the 21264."]], Harvey J. Stein, axp-list, 12 Apr 1998)] and a Digital product brief from 1998 names Intel and Samsung as the 21264's sources. [(>{{wiki:hardware:cpu:21264:21264_product_brief.pdf?linkonly|21264 Product Brief}}, 9 Sep 1998, p. 1)]

Samsung fabricated Alphas in Korea, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-January.mbox#L17653|"RE: FYI: Howard Elias replaces Bill Heil as BCSG head - Alpha's n ew boss"]], Mike Foley, axp-list, 17 Jan 2001)] using a 0.25 micron process, "significantly better than the Hudson fab's 0.35 micron process." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-January.mbox#L23466|"RE: FYI: Howard Elias replaces Bill Heil as BCSG head - Alpha's new boss"]], Greg Lindahl, axp-list, 22 Jan 2001)] In 1998, it set up Alpha Processor, Inc. (see below). [(>''Samsung announces Alpha subsidiary'', 16 Jun 1998, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])] Samsung developed the [[hardware:motherboards:up1000|UP1000]] and [[hardware:motherboards:up1100|UP1100]], and later the [[hardware:motherboards:up1500|UP1500]] with AMD's 761 chipset. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-August.mbox#L6772|"RE: Samsung EV69"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 8 Aug 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L28391|"Re: Compaq/Intel vs API"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 25 Jun 2001)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1500:up1500_user_s_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP1500 User's Manual}}, Samsung, May 2001, p. ii)] In 2002, Samsung disbanded its motherboard design group, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-July.mbox#L19874|"RE: Samsung EV7"]], David Mihail, axp-list, 31 Jul 2002)] and by 2003 it had stopped making Alpha boards. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-January.mbox#L4438|"Re: UP1500?"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 8 Jan 2003)]

=== Intel ===

Intel bought Digital's Hudson fabrication plant in the 1997 settlement, and was obliged to make Alpha processors for Digital, and only for Digital. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L3421|"Re: Intel (was Re: New Boards)"]], Richard Gorton, axp-list, 5 Jan 1998)] In 2001, Intel took over Compaq's Alpha technology (see [[#compaq|Compaq]]).

=== IBM ===

IBM negotiated an Alpha license in 1998, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L50829|"IBM & Alpha, looks good!"]], Stephen J. Gaudet, axp-list, 23 Jun 1998)] and fabricated Alpha processors for Compaq from 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L28100|"IBM to build Alphas"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 17 Feb 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L28391|"Re: Compaq/Intel vs API"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 25 Jun 2001)]

=== AMD ===

AMD licensed the 21264's EV6 bus from Digital. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-November.mbox#L7800|"RE: Alpha cluster?"]], Paul Bunn, axp-list, 3 Nov 1999)] Its AMD-751 and AMD-761 north bridges, designed for the Athlon, also support Alpha processors, [(>{{wiki:hardware:chipsets:amd-761:amd-761_software_bios_design_guide.pdf?linkonly|AMD-761 Software/BIOS Design Guide}}, February 2002, p. 9)] and were used in the UP1000, UP1100, and UP1500. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-June.mbox#L21165|"RE: Alpha motherboards ?"]], Mike Foley, axp-list, 28 Jun 2000)] Alpha Processor's Slot B modules were meant to share a motherboard design with AMD's Athlon. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-April.mbox#L17119|"Samsung now producing the 21264."]], Harvey J. Stein, axp-list, 12 Apr 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-October.mbox#L8382|"OpenBoot, SRM, AlphaBIOS and Slot B"]], Kirk R. Erichsen, axp-list, 6 Oct 1999)] AMD dropped the EV6 bus for its Hammer processors in 2001. [(>''AMD drops EV6 for Hammer bus'', The Register, 25 Jan 2001, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])]

==== Alpha Processor, Inc. and API NetWorks ====

Alpha Processor, Inc. (API), "A Samsung Company", was based at 130C Baker Avenue Extension in Concord, Massachusetts. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:pc164lx:pc164lx_product_brief.pdf?linkonly|AlphaPC 164LX Product Brief}}, Alpha Processor, Inc., January 1999, p. 2)] Its employees on the mailing lists included Stig Telfer from August 1998, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-August.mbox#L65114|"Re: lapack, blas, gemm under linux/axp"]], Stig Telfer, axp-list, 29 Aug 1998)] Hooman Hodjat, who announced API's "commitment to the Linux market" in January 1999, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-January.mbox#L15586|"simple questions from a New User"]], Hooman Hodjat, axp-list, 8 Jan 1999)] Tom Morris, and Mike Foley. API's spec sheets said that "API leverages the resources of Samsung to deliver products at competitive prices." [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1000:up1000_spec_sheet.pdf?linkonly|UP1000 Spec Sheet}}, January 2000, p. 1)]

API sold Samsung's processors and boards, and its own:

  * [[hardware:motherboards:up1000|UP1000]] (manual dated December 1999) and [[hardware:motherboards:up1100|UP1100]] (May 2000), [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1000:up1000_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP1000 User Manual}}, 10 Dec 1999, p. ii)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1100:up1100_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP1100 User Manual}}, 31 May 2000, p. ii)]
  * [[hardware:motherboards:up2000|UP2000]] (April 2000) and [[hardware:motherboards:up2000#up2000plus|UP2000+]] (June 2000), [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up2000:up2000_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP2000 User Manual}}, 7 Apr 2000, p. ii)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up2000plus:up2000plus_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP2000+ User Manual}}, 12 Jan 2001, p. iii)]
  * the CS20, a 1U dual-processor server, which Cray used as the building block of its SuperCluster systems. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-January.mbox#L31275|"RE: Cray chooses Alpha Linux ..."]], Tom Morris, axp-list, 30 Jan 2001)]

API also wrote the APB boot loader for the UP1000, [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up1000:up1000_technical_reference_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP1000 Technical Reference Manual}}, December 1999)] and partnered with MandrakeSoft (see [[#mandrakesoft|MandrakeSoft]]). The company was renamed API NetWorks, Inc. at the end of 2000. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:up2000plus:up2000plus_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|UP2000+ User Manual}}, 12 Jan 2001, p. iii)] [(>''Alpha Changes Name; Debuts Server, 833MHz Processor'', Electronics Weekly, 7 Nov 2000, listed on [[history:news_coverage|News Coverage]])]

When Compaq cancelled Windows NT on Alpha in August 1999, API was the vendor with the most to lose, and the Boston Globe's "Executives call future of Alpha chip secure" was passed around the AlphaNT mail list, together with a companion report that Compaq was about to finalize a deal with IBM to act as a foundry for Alpha. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L1|"Executives call future of Alpha chip secure"]], Aaron Sakovich, alphant, 31 Aug 1999)]

API NetWorks closed around the end of 2001. Microway bought its product assets and took over its warranties. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-May.mbox#L9331|"Re: Burning Alpha!"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 10 May 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-August.mbox#L8635|"Re: no more alpha letter on inquirer"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 9 Aug 2002)]

==== Board and system makers ====

  * **DeskStation Technology** was "historically a MIPS house" that built Alpha systems with its own system logic and its own Windows NT firmware and HAL. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L5967|"re: Linux on a Deskstation Raptor 3 - System?"]], Neal Crook, axp-list, 20 Feb 1996)] It designed the Ruffian, later sold as the [[hardware:motherboards:pc164ux|AlphaPC 164UX]], and hired Dean Hall in 1997 to port Linux to it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-July.mbox#L2762|"Re: Deskstation Ruffian compatibility"]], Dean Hall, axp-list, 3 Jul 1997)] The rights to the Ruffian and its ARCSBIOS firmware went to Samsung or API, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-hardware-mbox/2000-July.mbox#L32|"[Axp-hardware] Re: What happened to Deskstation?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-hardware, 20 Jul 2000)] and DeskStation had gone out of business by 2000. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2000-September.mbox#L3310|"Re: Dekstation Raptor Reflex"]], Ron Farrer, debian-alpha, 13 Sep 2000)] DeskStation announced its own web site at ''www.dti.com'' on the AlphaNT mail list in November 1995, describing itself as designing and manufacturing "modular high performance RISC workstations for the Windows NT market" and saying that "for the past six months, the fastest of these has been the 21164 at 300 MHZ". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1995-November.mbox#L1408|"DeskStation Technology's web site"]], Blaise Fanning, alphant, 8 Nov 1995)] Where most Alpha vendors built systems around Digital Semiconductor's EB164 reference board, "DeskStation's big plus is that Raptors are completely their own designs, down to the chipset and HAL for Windows NT", with system logic in reprogrammable EPLDs rather than the ASICs LSI Logic made for Digital, which let DeskStation ship each new Alpha processor months ahead of everyone else, at the cost of a slower memory bus than the Alcor designs. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-June.mbox#L2856|"DeskStation Raptor Reflex 400 MHz"]], Nebojsa Novakovic, alphant, 9 Jun 1996)] Its Alpha line ran from the Raptor 3, sold with a 275 MHz 21064A or a 266 or 300 MHz 21164, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1995-November.mbox#L138|"Re: Performance Tests"]], Gunnar Klauberg, alphant, 1 Nov 1995)] through the 400 MHz Raptor Reflex, to the dual 500 MHz EV56 Renegade, which the company was "scrambling so hard" to have ready for Siggraph in August 1996. === Red Hat ===

Red Hat released Red Hat Linux/AXP in December 1995, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1995-December.mbox#L569|"Re: Any new ETA on ftp release?"]], Erik Troan, axp-list, 14 Dec 1995)] announced as release 2.1 BETA, generally available on 6 December on CD-ROM and FTP with over 200 packages, a 150 page manual and 30 days of installation support. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-December.mbox#L9990|"COMMERCIAL: Red Hat Linux/AXP 2.1 BETA"]], Erik Troan, comp.os.linux.announce, 8 Dec 1995)] The first "Official" release followed on 27 January 1996, still numbered 2.1: built on a 1.3.57 kernel with the Alpha patches, gcc 2.7.1 and libc 0.40.2, running XFree86 3.1.2 on S3 and Mach64 boards, supporting the Avanti and Mustang boards used in the AlphaStation 200, 250 and 400 and the EB164, and able to install from an NFS mounted CD without a root floppy. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-June.mbox#L386|"Red Hat Linux/AXP 3.0.3 available for FTP"]], Marc Ewing, axp-list, 2 Jun 1996)] The boxed 3.0.3 for Alpha had gone on sale a month earlier at $99.95, with 330 packages including sources on one CD and 60 days of installation support. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-May.mbox#L11302|"COMMERCIAL: Official Red Hat 3.0.3 Linux/Alpha Available"]], Erik Troan, comp.os.linux.announce, 1 May 1996)] Red Hat Linux 4.0 in October 1996 was "the first synchronized release of *any* operating system" for Intel, Alpha, and SPARC. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-October.mbox#L4753|"Red Hat Linux/Alpha 4.0 Available Now! (fwd)"]], Erik Troan, axp-list, 4 Oct 1996)] The 4.x updates kept Alpha in step with Intel and SPARC: 4.1 was announced on 3 February 1997 [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-February.mbox#L8053|"COMMERCIAL: Red Hat Linux 4.1 for Intel, Alpha, and SPARC!"]], Red Hat Software, comp.os.linux.announce, 7 Feb 1997)] and 4.2 began shipping on 21 May 1997. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-May.mbox#L8142|"COMMERCIAL: Red Hat Linux 4.2 for Intel, Alpha, and SPARC"]], Red Hat Software, comp.os.linux.announce, 22 May 1997)] Red Hat promoted the platform as a prize: its Free Desktop Contest of May 1996 offered "A 64 bit Alpha Workstation running Red Hat Linux" for the best desktop configuration built from free RPM packaged software. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-May.mbox#L2830|"CONTEST: Red Hat Free Desktop Contest - Win an Alpha workstation!"]], Red Hat Software, comp.os.linux.announce, 21 May 1996)] Red Hat also hosted axp-list, //"Linux and Red Hat on Alpha processors"//, running under Mailman at listman.redhat.com by 2000. In January 2002, Red Hat said it had no plans "to port the full Red Hat Linux OS to Alpha", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L5659|"Is this really a good news ??"]], MILLET Mathieu, axp-list, 11 Jan 2002)] and the 7.2 CD for Alpha was only sold by HP, for $29, without support. ==== Software vendors ====

=== Microsoft ===

Windows NT ran on Alpha systems with the [[software:firmware:arc|ARC or AlphaBIOS]] firmware. In August 1999, InfoWorld reported that Compaq had laid off more than 100 people in Redmond who ported Windows NT to Alpha, and "effectively discontinued support of Microsoft's operating system on Alpha." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L31035|"Re: Done: Experience with large IDE drive and Promise Ultra/66 card"]], Jeff Bolle, axp-list, 22 Aug 1999)] According to Phillip Ezolt of Compaq, Compaq dropped only the 32-bit version, and Microsoft then dropped the 64-bit version. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-September.mbox#L54447|"Re: Off topic: NT on Alpha (was: Alpha Users in the Toronto area.)"]], Phillip Ezolt, axp-list, 30 Sep 1999)]

Microsoft's commitment had been doubted a year earlier, when the renewal form for MSDN Universal subscriptions said that "Development tools are Windows-based versions for the Intel platform only. Macintosh, RISC, and all other versions must be acquired separately", and MSDN customer service confirmed the wording. Microsoft's Tod Nielsen answered a subscriber directly that it was "a mistake in the renewal form that's getting fixed - Alpha tools are included in MSDN Universal." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L777|"Fw: MSDN drops Alpha development tools"]], Paul Brophy, alphant, 27 Oct 1998)] The Alpha release of Visual C++ 6.0 followed the x86 one by months, arriving in the fourth quarter of 1998, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L291|"RE: MSVC++ 6.0 Alpha"]], Aaron Sakovich, alphant, 27 Oct 1998)] and the MSDN download manager, which subscribers needed to download anything at all, was never ported: "The Download Manager is an x86 application. Currently there are no plans for an alpha version. The only way to download to an alpha is to download from an x86 machine and then copy the file to the alpha machine through a network." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L2451|"No Alpha MSDN Download manager"]], Rich Peters, alphant, 16 Nov 1998)] Aaron Sakovich ran an "Open Letter to Microsoft" for signature on www.alphant.com from November 1998. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L116|"Alpha NT art gallery"]], Aaron Sakovich, alphant, 2 Nov 1998)]

[(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-April.mbox#L36357|"Accelerated-X 4.1.2 for AlphaLinux"]], Max Weninger, axp-list, 23 Apr 1999)]
  * **Syndesis Corporation** sold Megahedron, a programmable 3D graphics engine driven by an interpreted language called SMPL, for $99 in October 1996. One CD carried executables for Windows 95, Windows NT on x86 and Alpha, Linux, and SGI IRIX, and a time-limited Linux demo was offered as a 450 KB download. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-October.mbox#L19827|"COMMERCIAL: Free demo of Megahedron: a programmable 3D graphics engine, $99 Win32/AXP/Linux/SGI"]], John Foust, comp.os.linux.announce, 4 Oct 1996)]
  * **Cygnus Solutions** employed Richard Henderson, who maintained the Alpha back end in egcs and gcc and answered toolchain questions on axp-list from a Cygnus address through the late 1990s. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-December.mbox#L16312|"Re: new library and compiler"]], Richard Henderson, axp-list, 11 Dec 1997)] Red Hat acquired Cygnus in 2000.
  * **Oracle** was asked about repeatedly on axp-list between 1998 and 2002, but no Linux/Alpha release of its database appeared, and users asked instead whether the Tru64 build could be run. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L13331|"Oracle 8 on Alpha/Linux?"]], Adrian Gartland, axp-list, 16 Oct 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-February.mbox#L8829|"Oracle on Alpha Linux"]], Tony Hoover, axp-list, 7 Feb 2001)]
