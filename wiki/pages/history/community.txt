===== Community History =====

The Alpha Linux community's mailing lists, newsgroups, and distributions, most of them long gone. See [[:community|Community]] for the lists, channels, and distributions still in use, and [[history:alphalinux.org|History of alphalinux.org]] for the site that hosted several of the lists below.

==== Mailing lists ====

The first Alpha Linux list of all was Digital's own. Jim Paradis announced **linux-axp@amt.tay1.dec.com** in November 1994, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L36280|"Linux/Alpha Progress Report - Part 2"]], Jim Paradis, comp.os.linux.announce, 18 Nov 1994)] and moved it to majordomo as **alpha-linux** in January 1995, replacing "the old, unreliable, hand-maintained list". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-January.mbox#L2126|"ANNOUNCEMENT: Linux/Alpha Developers' Kit!"]], Jim Paradis, comp.os.linux.announce, 23 Jan 1995)] No archive of it is known to survive.

Several once popular Alpha Linux mailing lists are no longer hosted anywhere. The largest was **axp-list**, run by Red Hat from 22 November 1995 until its last message in February 2022, alongside the kernel-focused **axp-kernel-list**. Separate lists ran on alphalinux.org: **axp-hardware**, **axp-software**, **milo-list** (MILO development), and **high_perf** (High Performance Alpha Linux). Mandrake ran **cooker-axp** for the Alpha port of its Cooker development branch.

Mirrors of all of these, reconstructed from Pipermail and MHonArc pages, Gmane, and Wayback Machine captures, are preserved at [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives|alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives]] on GitHub. The same repository holds a complete mbox archive of the Debian **debian-alpha** list, which lists.debian.org serves only as per-message HTML.

axp-kernel-list, for "Alpha kernel issues", was running by 3 August 1998, when it appeared in Red Hat's periodic list of its mailing lists. [(>[[https://mail.gnome.org/archives/gtk-list/1998-August/msg00060.html|"Mailing List Lists"]], listmaster@redhat.com, gtk-list, 3 Aug 1998)] No archive of it from before April 2002 is known to survive. Headers in the surviving messages show it was by then a private Mailman list, and Red Hat's copy is gone.

**gentoo-alpha** began with a test message from Gentoo's mail administrator on 17 December 2002. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2002-December.mbox#L1|"test"]], Sascha Schwabbauer, gentoo-alpha, 17 Dec 2002)] The port's developers also met on **#gentoo-alpha** on irc.freenode.net from at least February 2003. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L62|"Re: Fw: Gentoo/Alpha"]], Aron Griffis, gentoo-alpha, 28 Feb 2003)] The list is still open but nearly silent: by 2019 one subscriber's previous list mail was from 2015. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2019-September.mbox#L266|"Re: [gentoo-alpha] Gentoo/Alpha is without a build machine"]], Don Wilburn, gentoo-alpha, 2 Sep 2019)] The archive combines Gentoo's public-inbox archive, Gmane, and marc.info for the first four months.

=== DEC OSF/1 and Tru64 UNIX ===

The longest-lived Alpha list of all was not about Linux. **alpha-osf-managers** was a list for system managers of Alpha systems running DEC OSF/1, run on Majordomo at Oak Ridge National Laboratory by Dave Sill, who also ran the older **decstation-managers** list. In June 1993 he named comp.unix.osf.osf1 "the best newsgroup, and the alpha-osf-managers mailing list ... the best mailing list" for Alpha OSF/1, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-misc-mbox/googlegroups-1993-June.mbox#L2772|"Re: Is there a newsgroup/e-mail for DEC's Alpha OSF machines ?"]], Dave Sill, comp.unix.osf.misc, 30 Jun 1993)] and the DEC OSF/1 FAQ pointed readers to it from its first posting. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1993-July.mbox#L5398|"comp.unix.ultrix DEC OSF/1 Frequently Asked Questions"]], Win Treese, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 2 Jul 1993)] In August 1995 Sill put archives of both lists, going back to October 1993, on ftp.ornl.gov. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-August.mbox#L3412|"Official FTP archives available"]], Dave Sill, alpha-osf-managers, 29 Aug 1995)]

The list had an unusual rule, which is why its archive reads as a series of questions and summaries. It was "a quick-turnaround trouble shooting aid for those who administer and manage Alpha AXP systems runnning OSF/1". Answers went privately to whoever asked, and the asker then posted a summary back to the list with "SUMMARY" as the first word of the subject. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-July.mbox#L5571|"Alpha-OSF-Managers Policy Statement"]], Dave Sill, alpha-osf-managers, 18 Jul 1995)] Digital's engineers answered there too, most prolifically Tom Blinn of the UNIX Software Group, whose posts are the best surviving record of when each release of the operating system shipped (see [[software:operating_systems#tru64_unix|Tru64 UNIX]]). Blinn had joined Digital in November 1983 and worked on the operating system "since the beginning (back before the release of DEC OSF/1 V1.3)"; he retired from HP in October 2005. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/gmane-2005-October.mbox#L1089|"The meaning of life and everything.."]], Thomas P. Blinn, tru64-unix-managers, 7 Oct 2005)]

The list followed the operating system's renames. It became **tru64-unix-managers** on 16 March 1999, with the old address to be disabled around 16 April. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L16680|"Administrivia: list name changed"]], Dave Sill, tru64-unix-managers, 16 Mar 1999)] The monthly policy statement then restricted it to "HP Tru64 UNIX (formerly Compaq Tru64 UNIX (formerly Digital UNIX (formerly Digital OSF/1))) system management questions and summaries". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/gmane-2011-March.mbox#L109|"Administrivia: Tru64-UNIX-Managers information and policy statement"]], Dave Sill, tru64-unix-managers, 9 Mar 2011)] It never formally closed. Traffic fell from about 3,300 messages in 2003 to a dozen in 2012 as members retired their last Alphas, with posts such as "Just turned off my last Alpha" in June 2012. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/gmane-2012-June.mbox#L113|"Farewell."]], Joe Fletcher, tru64-unix-managers, 28 Jun 2012)] That March 2011 policy statement was the last, and the last message is dated 28 September 2012. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/gmane-2012-September.mbox#L1|"New disks on HSZ50 not usable"]], Ralf Borowski, tru64-unix-managers, 28 Sep 2012)]

^ List ^ Host ^ Coverage ^ Messages ^
| alpha-osf-managers, tru64-unix-managers | Oak Ridge National Laboratory | April 1994 to September 2012 | 56,084 |

The archive is assembled from marc.info, the only source before May 2002, and Gmane after that. marc.info rebuilds headers without Message-IDs and obscures addresses, and its copies of the list's 1994 months hold only a handful of messages.

=== Other operating systems ===

While not pertaining to Linux, Alpha hardware and porting information may be found in the [[https://lists.freebsd.org/pipermail/freebsd-alpha/|FreeBSD Alpha]], [[https://mail-index.netbsd.org/port-alpha/|NetBSD Alpha]], and [[http://marc.info/?l=openbsd-alpha|OpenBSD Alpha]] mailing lists. These three lists are mirrored in the same [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives|archive repository]], assembled from marc.info, mail-index.netbsd.org, Gmane and the FreeBSD Pipermail archive. They are the best surviving record of the parts of Alpha that Linux shares with the BSDs: PALcode and console behavior, chipset errata, unaligned access and floating-point trap handling.

^ List ^ Project ^ Coverage ^
| freebsd-alpha | FreeBSD | January 1998 to December 2010 |
| port-alpha | NetBSD | January 1996 to present |
| alpha (openbsd-alpha) | OpenBSD | September 2001 to present |

FreeBSD retired its Alpha port after the 6.x series and closed the list at the end of 2010. The NetBSD and OpenBSD lists are still active.

**alphant**, the Windows NT on Alpha mail list, was not a Linux list, but it covered the same hardware, and Digital's own engineers answered questions on it much as they did on axp-list. Aaron Sakovich started it in September 1995 on majordomo at Net Results, an Internet provider in Huntsville, Alabama, alongside the Alpha NT file archive and FAQ he ran there: "This mail list is devoted to discussions on Digital's Alpha systems running Microsoft's Windows NT operating system." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1995-September.mbox#L29|"Welcome message & list information"]], Aaron Sakovich, alphant, 18 Sep 1995)] Net Results moved it and its other lists off its mail hub and onto a dedicated list machine, "a DECStation 5000, running Ultrix, with 128Mb of memory", in February 1996. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L7306|"ADMINISTRIVIA - possible disruption in mail list"]], Aaron Sakovich, alphant, 22 Feb 1996)] By late 1998 the list was running on a LISTSERV at Allen-Bradley as **alphant@kludge.mke.ra.rockwell.com**, and on 28 October 1998 Peter Lizon moved it to his own domain: "The new address is alphant@alphant.com". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L3074|"Moving over?"]], Dan Schwartz, alphant, 29 Oct 1998)] It later moved again, to a Sunbelt Software Lyris server. Every host is gone, and only what the Wayback Machine captured survives.

^ List ^ Host ^ Coverage ^ Messages ^
| alphant | Net Results, Allen-Bradley, Lizon Corporation | September 1995 to July 1996, October 1998 to April 2000 | 2,225 |

The surviving archive has two pieces, separated by the move to Allen-Bradley: 1,949 messages from September 1995 to July 1996, from a Hypermail archive mirrored at TU Delft, and 276 from October 1998 to April 2000, from the MHonArc archive on www.alphant.com. Nothing of the Rockwell era, and nothing after April 2000, is known to survive.

==== Newsgroups ====

A few newsgroups carried Alpha discussion:

  * [[https://groups.google.com/group/comp.os.linux.alpha/topics|comp.os.linux.alpha]] for discussion of running Linux on Alpha
  * [[https://groups.google.com/group/comp.sys.dec/topics|comp.sys.dec]] for discussion about DEC Computers
  * [[https://groups.google.com/group/linux.debian.ports.alpha|linux.debian.ports.alpha]], a gateway to the debian-alpha mailing list, for discussion of running Debian on Alpha

Digital ran two moderated groups of its own, **biz.digital.announce** for press releases and fact sheets and **biz.digital.articles** for the //Customer Update// product announcements. Both carried the company's own descriptions of Alpha hardware as it shipped, and both are useful for dating and specifying machines that the surviving manuals do not cover. Neither ran for as long as the archives suggest: the last real //Customer Update// article is from December 1995 and the last press release from April 1997, and everything the archives hold after that is spam.

Announcements of Alpha ports, kits and distributions were posted to **comp.os.linux.announce**, which is where Digital's early Linux/Alpha work was made public.

comp.os.linux.alpha was created in 1997 on David Mosberger-Tang's proposal. His Request For Discussion, posted on 26 April 1997, argued that Linux/Alpha discussion was scattered across Red Hat's mailing list and groups such as comp.sys.dec, and measured the interest by the list's traffic: in the 22 days from 1 to 22 April 1997, axp-list carried over 950 messages from more than 200 different people. A newsgroup would give "a focused and vendor-neutral place for discussing issues related to Linux on Alpha". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-April.mbox#L4252|"RFD: comp.os.linux.alpha"]], David Mosberger, comp.os.linux.announce, 26 Apr 1997)] Voting closed on 30 June 1997 and the group passed 687 to 9, with the newsgroups line "Linux on Digital Alpha machines." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-July.mbox#L1|"RESULT: comp.os.linux.alpha passes 687:9"]], David Bostwick, comp.os.linux.announce, 2 Jul 1997)] The vote itself was run by a neutral votetaker, David Bostwick of the Usenet Volunteer Votetakers, who posted the first call for votes on 9 June 1997 and the last on 19 June, both cross-posted to comp.os.linux.misc and comp.sys.dec as well as the newgroup groups. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-June.mbox#L169|"CFV: comp.os.linux.alpha"]], David Bostwick, comp.os.linux.announce, 9 Jun 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-June.mbox#L2373|"2nd CFV: comp.os.linux.alpha"]], David Bostwick, comp.os.linux.announce, 19 Jun 1997)]

Local Linux user groups were demonstrating Alpha hardware before the newsgroup existed. The Washington DC group met on 17 January 1996 to watch Bob Segrest, a Digital support engineer "for exotic applications (such as Linux)", show "how to put together a smoking-fast 275 MHz Alpha system from stock PC components" and discuss the Alpha Linux distributions then available. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L19183|"LOCAL: (Wash. DC Linux user group) Jan 17 meeting: Alpha Linux Demo"]], Przemek Klosowski, comp.os.linux.announce, January 1996)] The Toronto group met on 29 April 1997 for "Linux on the DEC Alpha", presented by Drew Sullivan of Systems Software Consulting, whose announcement noted that "current Linux kernel development by Linus Torvalds is done entirely on the DEC Alpha platform". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-April.mbox#L3015|"LOCAL: Linux on the DEC Alpha (Toronto)"]], Laszlo Herczeg, comp.os.linux.announce, 25 Apr 1997)]

DEC OSF/1 had its own groups, **comp.unix.osf.osf1** and **comp.unix.osf.misc**, which also covered OSF/1 on other vendors' hardware. The DEC OSF/1 FAQ (''Archive-name: dec-faq/osf1'', with ''dec-faq/common'' shared with ULTRIX) began as part 3 of the comp.unix.ultrix FAQ, edited by Win Treese, and was cross-posted to comp.unix.osf.osf1 and comp.sys.dec and archived on gatekeeper.dec.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1993-July.mbox#L5398|"comp.unix.ultrix DEC OSF/1 Frequently Asked Questions"]], Win Treese, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 2 Jul 1993)] When Treese left Digital for Open Market in September 1994, Steve Lionel of DEC Fortran development took over the ULTRIX and OSF/1 FAQs. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1994-September.mbox#L15189|"What's happening with the FAQ"]], Win Treese, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 13 Sep 1994)] The last edition in the archive is from August 1995, when Lionel planned to merge the two parts into a "Digital-UNIX" FAQ. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L8638|"DEC OSF/1 AXP Frequently Asked Questions"]], Steve Lionel, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 21 Aug 1995)]

comp.unix.tru64 was created by renaming comp.unix.osf.osf1, after Jerry Leslie asked in September 1999 "isn't it time for Tru64 to have its own newsgroup ?" [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-misc-mbox/googlegroups-1999-September.mbox#L137|"Time For a True64 Unix Newsgroup ?"]], Jerry Leslie, comp.unix.osf.misc, 16 Sep 1999)] The vote closed on 20 March 2000: the rename passed 134 to 7, and the removal of comp.unix.osf.misc passed 127 to 10, the proponents having found no on-topic traffic that was not about Tru64 and noting that "the OSF has become 'The Open Group' and discontinued all operating system development." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-misc-mbox/googlegroups-2000-March.mbox#L2139|"RESULT: comp.unix.tru64 rename and removal of comp.unix.osf.misc pass"]], Neil Crellin, comp.unix.osf.misc, 21 Mar 2000)]

Google Groups stopped carrying new Usenet posts in February 2024, so these links serve historic content only. Mbox archives of all nine groups, built from the Internet Archive's ''usenet-comp'' and ''usenet-biz'' collections and as complete as those collections allow, are kept alongside the mailing lists in [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives|alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives]].

=== Gentoo ===

Gentoo still supports Alpha (see [[:community#distributions|Community]]), and the gentoo-alpha list records the port's history from its start.

In February 2003 the port had three developers, who split the work as "bjb - kernel, linker, gcc", "wwoods - kde, misc apps", and "agriffis - gnome, misc apps": Bjoern Brauel, Will Woods, and Aron Griffis. Griffis called the port "short-staffed on Alpha developers": when all three were busy at their jobs, "gentoo/alpha stalls for a while". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L1656|"Re: Fw: Gentoo/Alpha"]], Aron Griffis, gentoo-alpha, 25 Feb 2003)] Test install images were kept under ''releases/1.4_rc1/alpha'', [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L102|"Re: [gentoo-alpha] Re: [rjmc@cjok.fm: Gentoo on Dec Alpha]"]], Sigurd Stordal, gentoo-alpha, 26 Feb 2003)] and the stage tarballs were built for the EV5, "the right thing for compatibility anyway". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L62|"Re: Fw: Gentoo/Alpha"]], Aron Griffis, gentoo-alpha, 28 Feb 2003)] Tavis Ormandy joined in April 2003, bringing ebuilds for the Compaq C and C++ compilers. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/gmane-2003-April.mbox#L683|"new dev: taviso"]], Aron Griffis, gentoo-alpha, 14 Apr 2003)] Alpha was not expected to be supported in Gentoo 1.4, whose focus was "sparc, ppc and of course x86", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/gmane-2003-August.mbox#L46|"Re: status of alpha port?"]], Tavis Ormandy, gentoo-alpha, 1 Aug 2003)] and Will Woods built a test LiveCD by hand in September 2003, work he described as "slow, painful, and messy". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/gmane-2003-September.mbox#L148|"Re: LiveCD update?"]], Will Woods, gentoo-alpha, 15 Sep 2003)]

The first Alpha LiveCD built with Gentoo's ''catalyst'' tool came from Jay Maynard in February 2004. It carried three kernels: one for normal use, one for systems booting with MILO, and one for the Jensen. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2004-February.mbox#L3612|"[gentoo-alpha] Alpha LiveCD available for testing"]], Jay Maynard, gentoo-alpha, 27 Feb 2004)] Bryan Østergaard (kloeri) took over release building in August 2004 [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2004-August.mbox#L696|"Re: [Fwd: Re: [gentoo-alpha] Problems running bootstrap.sh]"]], Jay Maynard, gentoo-alpha, 3 Aug 2004)] and led the port for the next few years. The 2005.0 LiveCD carried both 2.6 and 2.4 kernels because "a few systems still seems to have problems with 2.6 kernels". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/gmane-2005-January.mbox#L3179|"Re: alpha performance"]], Bryan Østergaard, gentoo-alpha, 29 Jan 2005)] Gentoo/Alpha dropped the Compaq Java runtime and development kit in July 2006, leaving the architecture without a full Java implementation, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2006-July.mbox#L485|"[gentoo-alpha] The end of compaq java. Current Gentoo/Alpha java situation."]], Jose Luis Rivero, gentoo-alpha, 13 Jul 2006)] and ended 2.4 kernel support on 19 January 2008, after every other Gentoo architecture team. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2008-January.mbox#L1|"[gentoo-alpha] End of 2.4 support in Gentoo/Alpha"]], Jose Luis Rivero, gentoo-alpha, 19 Jan 2008)] By mid 2007 Østergaard, "one of the oldest alpha Gentoo developers", had left Gentoo, and the arch tester Tobias Klausmann ran the port's main development machine. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2007-July.mbox#L68|"[gentoo-alpha] Gentoo/Alpha status report 2007"]], Jose Luis Rivero, gentoo-alpha, 21 Jul 2007)]

In September 2019 the port lost its build machine, a quad-CPU ES40 hosted without charge for many years. The replacements to hand were a DS20L that had blown a capacitor, an ES47 that was never stable enough, and 600 MHz DS10Ls, "pretty useless as a replacement". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2019-September.mbox#L1|"[gentoo-alpha] Gentoo/Alpha is without a build machine"]], Matt Turner, gentoo-alpha, 1 Sep 2019)] Matt Turner, who had "helped maintain Gentoo's DEC Alpha port for more than 10 years", then proposed dropping the stable keywords, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2020-January.mbox#L1|"[gentoo-alpha] RFC: Dropping alpha keywords to unstable"]], Matt Turner, gentoo-alpha, 15 Jan 2020)] and every ''alpha'' keyword became ''~alpha'' on 25 January 2020, though the team had "no plans to remove support for the architecture". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2020-January.mbox#L90|"[gentoo-alpha] [RFC] News item: Stable alpha keywords removed"]], Matt Turner, gentoo-alpha, 21 Jan 2020)] The Alpha profiles were demoted to experimental on 17 May 2022, so as not to hold back the Python team. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2022-May.mbox#L1|"[gentoo-alpha] alpha profiles demoted to experimental"]], Matt Turner, gentoo-alpha, 17 May 2022)] They have since been restored to stable.
