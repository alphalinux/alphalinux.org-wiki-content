===== History of alphalinux.org =====

==== Origins ====

By mid-1998 the Alpha Linux community had no central home. The primary reference was David Mosberger-Tang's personal site at **azstarnet.com/~axplinux**, which had gone quiet. Several smaller sites were appearing independently, and users were losing track of what information was where. Scott Lewis put the problem to **axp-list** in September 1998: //"Is anybody else concerned that there may be too many miscellaneous web sites without a central alpha linux reference site? Yeah, I know we have the azstarnet axp site, but it is a bit outdated, especially the links."// [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L58904|"Fwd: Re: New Alph Website"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)]

Rich Payne had been running one such site at **adam.cheshire.net/~rdp/alpha** on his machine, talisman, at his ISP in New Hampshire. He had started it because he was //"new to the Alpha world myself and was surprised at the lack of a cental site"//, and he was already thinking about a domain: //"I think before long I'll move it to its own domain (www.alphalinux.org anyone??)"//. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L58904|"Fwd: Re: New Alph Website"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)]

The consolidation was settled in a thread B. James Phillippe opened on 30 September 1998, "All These New Alpha Sites(tm)", which argued for a single top-level site chosen on two criteria: that other people be able to update it, and that its owner be committed to keeping it current. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L60716|"All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], B. James Phillippe, axp-list, 30 Sep 1998)] Rich replied that he was already in talks about hosting, welcomed co-maintainers, and asked the list to choose between **alphalinux.org**, **linuxalpha.org**, and **axplinux.org**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L61788|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)] Maurice Hilarius argued for the shorter **axplinux.org**; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L49078|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 30 Sep 1998)] Rich's concern was whether new users would recognize "axp" as Alpha at all. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L49132|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)] Mark Hansel confirmed it from the newcomer's side: //"It took me time to figure out why this list was called axp-list ... If you want the unwashed masses visiting your site ... avoid the axp."// [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L49434|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Mark Hansel, axp-list, 30 Sep 1998)] Scott Lewis agreed, and **alphalinux.org** carried the thread. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L50550|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Scott Lewis, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)]

Barrett Lyon, a data and network security consultant at Network Presence and a prolific **axp-list** contributor, had the name: //"I have that domain reged, and I also have a beefy connection. I'm willing to do anything needed to get this site up."// [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L51627|"Re: alphalinux.org"]], Barrett G. Lyon, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)] A skeptical whois from Rich drew the correction that it had been //"reged with Internic today"//, paid for by Barrett personally. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L51993|"Re: alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 30 Sep 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L52391|"Re: alphalinux.org"]], Barrett G. Lyon, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)] Rich built and maintained the site itself.

==== Launch ====

By 1 November 1998 Rich was signing list emails with **%%http://www.alphalinux.org%%** and looking for the azstarnet.com maintainers, //"looking to update the FAQ and various other docs and would like to migrate them to alphalinux.org"//. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L2406|"Alpha Linux WebSite"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 1 Nov 1998)] On 12 November 1998 he announced on **axp-list**: //"the site makeover to %%www.alphalinux.org%% has been put into place."// [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L35539|"alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 12 Nov 1998)]

Community response was immediate. The day before the launch Jonathan L Dubois had asked for the site to archive the Alpha mailing lists, //"Redhat periodicly fails to update its archive and the search engine there often returns garbled results"//; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L30376|"axp mailing list archive mirror?"]], Jonathan L Dubois, axp-list, 11 Nov 1998)] Peter Petrakis and Maurice Hilarius both backed it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L30402|"Re: axp mailing list archive mirror?"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 11 Nov 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L30541|"Re: axp mailing list archive mirror?"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 11 Nov 1998)] Within a day of launch Rich was setting up archiving of both **axp-list** and the Debian list. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L38178|"alphalinux.org news"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 13 Nov 1998)] The site's initial purpose was aggregating existing documentation: the Alpha FAQ, SRM HOWTO, kernel build guides, and high-performance computing resources.

Within two weeks of launch, Adam C. Powell IV at NIST started a dedicated high-performance mailing list after Rich assembled a high-performance page on the new site at **%%http://www.alphalinux.org/docs/high_perf.html%%**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L62383|"High-performance on AXP-Linux"]], Adam C. Powell IV, axp-list, 23 Nov 1998)] That list, **high_perf**, later migrated to alphalinux.org as one of several community lists hosted there.

Downtime was a recurring theme from the start: the site was out within a day of launch, and down again on 20 November 1998. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L38178|"alphalinux.org news"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 13 Nov 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L59212|"alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 20 Nov 1998)]

==== Growing the Team ====

Barrett Lyon became the primary news editor and day-to-day voice of the site through 1999 and into 2000. Peter Petrakis, already a prolific **axp-list** participant since at least 1998, was posting under an **alphalinux.org** address by 12 February 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L21256|"Re: Multia woes"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 12 Feb 1999)] He built **%%ftp.alphalinux.org%%**, a public FTP archive for RPMs and other Alpha software, which by the end of that month carried the latest GNOME release and //"a nearly complete mirror of all the Alpha stuff from ftp.digital.com"//. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L46823|"ftp.alphalinux.org has more cool stuff :)"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 28 Feb 1999)] The archive took community uploads through an **incoming/** directory, with the rule that packages be built with ''-mcpu=ev4'' so every Alpha could use them, optimized builds being welcome alongside an EV4-compatible version. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-May.mbox#L35703|"quick announcments"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 16 May 1999)] It filled a long-standing gap, since Red Hat's own update servers were frequently unreliable.

Daniel Frasnelli contributed unofficial kernel 2.2.2 patches that let Alpha users compile the new kernel weeks before an official fix appeared, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L37654|"[patches] patch-2.2.2-nonx86,patch-2.2.2-filter"]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 23 Feb 1999)] and was speaking for the site by March 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L35255|"AlphaLinux Org. web status..."]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 13 Mar 1999)]

On the site's first anniversary, Dan Frasnelli posted a request for comment on **axp-list** that doubles as a summary of the first year: //"Over one year has passed since the dream of an Alpha Linux Organization became a reality. In that time, our membership has grown and we have received donations of web space, money, and hardware from diverse sources."// The group by then ran an administrative list at **admin@alphalinux.org**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-October.mbox#L2999|"AlphaLinux Org RFC"]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 4 Oct 1999)]

Ron Farrer had been active on **axp-list** as an Alpha-only hardware vendor throughout 1998–99; by April 2000 he was writing bylined articles for the site, starting with a guide to installing Netscape using Compaq's Tru64 libraries. In May 2000 he and Rich Payne launched **AlphaNews.net**, //"created by members of the Alpha Linux Organization"// as a general Alpha news site. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-May.mbox#L17418|"ANNOUNCE: AlphaNews.net up and running!"]], Ron Farrer, axp-list, 25 May 2000)] Philip Ezolt came aboard in March 2000 to centralize games and demos on Alpha, announced alongside a new job-openings section on the site. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-March.mbox#L21468|"ALO news (RE: Linux/Alpha work)"]], Dan Frasnelli, axp-list, 12 Mar 2000)]

==== Infrastructure ====

The site ran on Rich Payne's machine //talisman//, co-located first at cheshire.net and later at mv.com. The public server lived in California, hosted free of charge by TheShell.com, whose network also carried Barrett Lyon's doxx.net: //"They have graciously allowed us to use their bandwidth at no charge due to the fact that one of the gents that started theshell.com likes Alphas."// [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L28414|"alphalinux.org and linuxalpha.org (was Re: Nvidia video cards)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 31 Jan 2002)] Upstream outages were the site's chronic weakness: a March 1999 disappearance turned out to be Pacific Bell losing every northern California circuit. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L35255|"AlphaLinux Org. web status..."]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 13 Mar 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L35463|"www.alphalinux.org status"]], Barrett G. Lyon, axp-list, 14 Mar 1999)] When the FTP archive went down in September 1999 its contents stayed reachable over HTTP at **%%http://www.alphalinux.org/ftpsite%%**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-September.mbox#L12319|"ftp.alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 7 Sep 1999)] In April 1999 Compaq donated an AlphaServer 1200 to the project.

The mailing list archives were online at **%%http://www.alphalinux.org/archives%%** by May 1999, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-May.mbox#L27981|"Re: axp-list archives"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 13 May 1999)] and searchable from April 2000 at **%%/archives/search.shtml%%**, though indexing lagged the live list by months. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-June.mbox#L3037|"Re: list archives"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 5 Jun 2000)]

In addition to the main site and FTP archive, alphalinux.org hosted several mailing lists:

  * **high_perf** — high-performance Alpha Linux, November 1998 – November 2001
  * **milo-list** — MILO bootloader development, April 1999 – November 2001
  * **axp-hardware** and **axp-software** — distribution-neutral hardware and software discussion, launched 4 December 1999

The last two came out of a suggestion by Ron Farrer that //"we should start a new list, alphalinux.org?, for general Alpha and Linux discussions"//, since **axp-list** was a Red Hat list; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L7509|"lists (was: Re: dell Dselling alphas?)"]], Ron Farrer, axp-list, 4 Dec 1999)] Rich answered the same day with //"OK, it's done ... I've set up two"//, initially run under majordomo. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L7715|"Re: lists (was: Re: dell Dselling alphas?)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 4 Dec 1999)] By May 2000 the lists had moved to Mailman at **lists.alphalinux.org**, running on talisman.mv.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/high_perf-mbox/2000-May.mbox#L1|"Welcome to the High-perf mailing list"]], high-perf-request, high_perf, 21 May 2000)]

In September 2000 the site added a hardware compatibility database, //"an effort to try to keep track of which devices work in which alpha systems"//, open for anyone to submit entries. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-September.mbox#L19450|"ANNOUNCE Hardware Compatibility Database"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 8 Sep 2000)]

Distribution was not always the site's to decide. In January 2000 Compaq asked for the Netscape Communicator RPM to be pulled from **ftp.alphalinux.org**, since its license with Netscape required Compaq to track downloads; the argument made to Rich was that download numbers were what would earn Alpha further investment from Netscape and Compaq. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-January.mbox#L38062|"RE: Comments on Netscape/Alpha on ftp.alphalinux.org? (fwd)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 27 Jan 2000)]

==== Industry Backdrop ====

The site operated during Alpha's most commercially turbulent years. Compaq ended Windows NT on Alpha in August 1999, concentrating its software support on Tru64 Unix and Linux — a shift that made alphalinux.org more strategically important even as it signaled long-term uncertainty for the platform. The site tracked this closely, covering Compaq's compiler releases for Linux (Fortran beta July 1999, C beta September 1999, C++ public beta March 2000), hardware announcements from Alpha Processor Inc., and the Samsung manufacturing relationship that briefly promised 1.4–1.6 GHz chips by 2000.

==== Decline ====

By 2002 the site was mostly unreachable, and Rich explained why on the lists. The hosting had not been withdrawn so much as gone unanswered, and the domain itself was the problem: //"the domain name is owned by the person mentioned above and he has become 'bored' with computers and I seem unable to get a hold of him. So it doesn't look good for alphalinux.org."// A backup site at **linuxalpha.org** ran on a machine someone at Compaq had offered. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L28414|"alphalinux.org and linuxalpha.org (was Re: Nvidia video cards)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 31 Jan 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L18553|"Re: Wither alphalinux.org?"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 23 Jan 2002)]

The domain came back by the end of that year: asked on New Year's Day 2003 which site was current, Rich answered //"we finally managed to get the domain name back"//, the site by then running on a machine at crl.dec.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-January.mbox#L1377|"Re: What's the current website? -- alphalinux.org, alphadriven.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 1 Jan 2003)] The content, however, stopped being updated.

==== Revival: the Wiki Era (2008–2012) ====

By 2008 alphalinux.org had gone years without updates. In April 2008, Matt Turner emailed Peter Petrakis proposing that he take over maintenance. Peter agreed and suggested starting with a wiki section to see if it could attract contributors.

Matt created a MediaWiki installation in July 2008 and announced it on **axp-list**: [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-July.mbox#L1157|"AlphaLinux.org gets a Wiki"]], Matt Turner, axp-list, 22 Jul 2008)]

<blockquote>
I've been working with Peter Petrakis for the last two to three months creating a wiki for AlphaLinux.org. It will provide a central location for Alpha related topics such as Alpha based computers, Alpha/Linux support, SRM versions, Alpha assembly and optimization, and Alpha software.
</blockquote>

The announcement called on the community to contribute hardware photographs, hard-to-find SRM versions, documentation, assembly programming examples, and information about important Alpha bugs. It also noted that this was //"the first time that AlphaLinux.org has been updated in years"//, with a forum and a move to a content management system planned; the forum was put to the list in January 2009 and never built. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2009-January.mbox#L3395|"Trying to gauge usage of a possible AlphaLinux.org Forum"]], Matt Turner, axp-list, 13 Jan 2009)] The wiki was used to coordinate work across distributions, for instance a patches page where the remaining Alpha ports could collect links to fixes still in flight. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2009-April.mbox#L506|"AlphaLinux Wiki: Patches page created"]], Matt Turner, axp-list, 18 Apr 2009)]

Matt also kept a news section on the wiki, tracking developments in the Linux kernel, gcc, glibc, and X.Org as they related to Alpha. Notable events he documented included:

  * **November 2009**: Matt created a DEC Alpha Linux kernel tree to collect Alpha patches; Linus Torvalds pulled it into mainline for 2.6.32.
  * **October 2009**: Keith Packard pulled fixes from Matt Turner's xserver tree, making X.Org work on Alpha for the first time since the 1.5 series.
  * **October 2009**: Debian demoted Alpha as a release architecture.
  * **September 2010**: Linus pulled patches removing the Big Kernel Lock from arch/alpha and fixing a 14-year-old signal handling bug present since Linux 1.3.95.
  * **December 2011**: Uros Bizjak fixed the long-standing gcc bug 27468, which caused gcc to emit extra sign-extending instructions.
  * **May 2012**: Måns Rullgard's patches implementing Tru64 syscalls — enabling Tru64 binaries including the C compiler to run on Linux — were pulled by Linus.

The wiki attracted a small but dedicated community, collectively running dozens of Alpha systems ranging from EV45-based AlphaStations to quad-EV68 and dual-EV7 AlphaServers.

In June 2012 the server crashed and the wiki database was lost. It had not been backed up. Peter Petrakis summarized the situation in IRC:

<blockquote>
mysqldump: Got error: 1146: Table 'ALwiki.mw_account_credentials' doesn't exist when using LOCK TABLES ... so... every other database survived, except the one that mattered the most
</blockquote>

==== Transfer to Gareth Randall (2015) ====

In 2015, Peter Petrakis and Rich Payne — who still held the domain — transferred alphalinux.org to Gareth Randall, who had expressed interest in carrying the site forward. Gareth maintained the site and a modestly populated wiki until mid-2026, when he transferred ownership to Matt Turner.

==== Transfer to Matt Turner (2026) ====

In 2026, Gareth Randall transferred alphalinux.org to Matt Turner. Matt created the [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org|alphalinux-org]] organization on GitHub to host repositories for Alpha packages (aboot, milo), mailing list archives, and the content of this wiki — publicly archived so the work of the community is not lost again.
