===== History of alphalinux.org =====

==== Origins ====

By mid-1998 the Alpha/Linux community had no central website. David Mosberger-Tang's site at **azstarnet.com/~axplinux**, the main reference, had gone quiet, and several smaller sites had appeared, among them Rich Payne's at **adam.cheshire.net/~rdp/alpha**, which he was already thinking of moving to **www.alphalinux.org**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L58904|"Fwd: Re: New Alph Website"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)]

In a September 1998 **axp-list** thread calling for a single top-level site with an owner committed to keeping it current, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L60716|"All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], B. James Phillippe, axp-list, 30 Sep 1998)] Payne offered to host one with co-maintainers and asked the list to choose a name. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L61788|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)] The list chose **alphalinux.org** over **linuxalpha.org** and **axplinux.org**, partly because newcomers might not recognize "axp" as Alpha. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L49434|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Mark Hansel, axp-list, 30 Sep 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L50550|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Scott Lewis, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)] Barrett Lyon, a network security consultant and frequent **axp-list** contributor, registered the domain at his own expense and offered hosting, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L51627|"Re: alphalinux.org"]], Barrett G. Lyon, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L52391|"Re: alphalinux.org"]], Barrett G. Lyon, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)] and Payne built and maintained the site.

==== Launch ====

The site opened in late October 1998, with the content of Payne's old pages, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L6491|"www.alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, debian-alpha, 27 Oct 1998)] and a redesign was announced on **axp-list** on 12 November 1998. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L35539|"alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 12 Nov 1998)] Its initial purpose was to gather existing documentation: the Alpha FAQ, which Payne sought to migrate from azstarnet.com, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L2406|"Alpha Linux WebSite"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 1 Nov 1998)] the SRM HOWTO, kernel build guides, and high-performance computing resources. At subscribers' request it also archived **axp-list** and the Debian Alpha list. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L30376|"axp mailing list archive mirror?"]], Jonathan L Dubois, axp-list, 11 Nov 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L38178|"alphalinux.org news"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 13 Nov 1998)]

Within two weeks of the redesign, Adam C. Powell IV of NIST started a high-performance mailing list, **high_perf**, prompted by a high-performance page on the site; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L62383|"High-performance on AXP-Linux"]], Adam C. Powell IV, axp-list, 23 Nov 1998)] the list later moved to alphalinux.org.

==== Growing the team ====

Lyon became the primary news editor and day-to-day voice of the site through 1999 and into 2000. Peter Petrakis, a frequent **axp-list** participant since at least 1998, was posting under an **alphalinux.org** address by 12 February 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L21256|"Re: Multia woes"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 12 Feb 1999)] He built **%%ftp.alphalinux.org%%**, a public FTP archive for RPMs and other Alpha software, which by the end of that month carried the latest GNOME release and a nearly complete mirror of the Alpha material on ftp.digital.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L46823|"ftp.alphalinux.org has more cool stuff :)"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 28 Feb 1999)] The archive took community uploads through an **incoming/** directory, with the rule that packages be built with ''-mcpu=ev4'' so every Alpha could use them, optimized builds being welcome alongside an EV4-compatible version. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-May.mbox#L35703|"quick announcments"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 16 May 1999)]

Daniel Frasnelli was speaking for the site by March 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L35255|"AlphaLinux Org. web status..."]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 13 Mar 1999)] On the site's first anniversary he summarized its first year on **axp-list**: the group had received donations of web space, money, and hardware, and ran an administrative list at **admin@alphalinux.org**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-October.mbox#L2999|"AlphaLinux Org RFC"]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 4 Oct 1999)]

Ron Farrer, an Alpha hardware vendor active on **axp-list**, was writing articles for the site by April 2000, and in May 2000 he and Payne launched **AlphaNews.net**, a general Alpha news site. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-May.mbox#L17418|"ANNOUNCE: AlphaNews.net up and running!"]], Ron Farrer, axp-list, 25 May 2000)] Phillip Ezolt joined in March 2000 to collect games and demos for Alpha, alongside a new job-openings section on the site. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-March.mbox#L21468|"ALO news (RE: Linux/Alpha work)"]], Dan Frasnelli, axp-list, 12 Mar 2000)]

==== Infrastructure ====

The web server for alphalinux.org was in California, where TheShell.com hosted it free of charge. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L701|"Re: where has alphalinux.org gone ?"]], Larry Snyder, axp-list, 2 Aug 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L28414|"alphalinux.org and linuxalpha.org (was Re: Nvidia video cards)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 31 Jan 2002)] Payne's own machine, //talisman//, at mv.com, handled his alphalinux.org mail and, later, the project's mailing lists. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L2447|"Re: X Servers and availability"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 3 Aug 1999)] In April 1999 Compaq donated an AlphaServer 1200 to the project.

The mailing list archives were online at **%%http://www.alphalinux.org/archives%%** by May 1999, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-May.mbox#L27981|"Re: axp-list archives"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 13 May 1999)] and searchable from April 2000. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-June.mbox#L3037|"Re: list archives"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 5 Jun 2000)]

In addition to the main site and FTP archive, alphalinux.org hosted several mailing lists:

  * **high_perf**, high-performance Alpha/Linux, November 1998 to November 2001
  * **milo-list**, MILO boot loader development, April 1999 to November 2001
  * **axp-hardware** and **axp-software**, distribution-neutral hardware and software discussion, launched 4 December 1999

The last two followed a suggestion by Farrer for a general Alpha and Linux list independent of Red Hat, which ran **axp-list**; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L7509|"lists (was: Re: dell Dselling alphas?)"]], Ron Farrer, axp-list, 4 Dec 1999)] Payne set up both lists the same day, initially under majordomo. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L7715|"Re: lists (was: Re: dell Dselling alphas?)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 4 Dec 1999)] By May 2000 the lists had moved to Mailman at **lists.alphalinux.org**, running on talisman.mv.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/high_perf-mbox/2000-May.mbox#L1|"Welcome to the High-perf mailing list"]], high-perf-request, high_perf, 21 May 2000)]

In September 2000 the site added a hardware compatibility database, open to submissions from anyone, to track which devices worked in which Alpha systems. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-September.mbox#L19450|"ANNOUNCE Hardware Compatibility Database"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 8 Sep 2000)]

==== Decline ====

By 2002 the site was mostly unreachable. Its domain registrant, Lyon, could no longer be reached, and a backup site at **linuxalpha.org** ran on a machine offered by a Compaq employee. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L28414|"alphalinux.org and linuxalpha.org (was Re: Nvidia video cards)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 31 Jan 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L18553|"Re: Wither alphalinux.org?"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 23 Jan 2002)]

The domain was recovered by the end of that year, and the site then ran on a machine at crl.dec.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-January.mbox#L1377|"Re: What's the current website? -- alphalinux.org, alphadriven.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 1 Jan 2003)] The content, however, stopped being updated.

==== Revival: the wiki era (2008 to 2012) ====

By 2008 alphalinux.org had gone years without updates. In April 2008, Matt Turner emailed Petrakis proposing that he take over maintenance. Petrakis agreed and suggested starting with a wiki section to see if it could attract contributors.

Turner created a MediaWiki installation in July 2008 and announced it on **axp-list** as a central location for Alpha hardware, Alpha/Linux support, SRM versions, Alpha assembly and optimization, and Alpha software. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-July.mbox#L1157|"AlphaLinux.org gets a Wiki"]], Matt Turner, axp-list, 22 Jul 2008)]

The announcement asked the community to contribute hardware photographs, hard-to-find SRM versions, documentation, assembly programming examples, and information about important Alpha bugs, and noted that this was the first update to the site in years. The wiki was used to coordinate work across distributions, for instance a patches page where the remaining Alpha ports could collect links to fixes still in flight. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2009-April.mbox#L506|"AlphaLinux Wiki: Patches page created"]], Matt Turner, axp-list, 18 Apr 2009)]

Turner also kept a news section on the wiki, tracking developments in the Linux kernel, GCC, glibc, and X.Org as they related to Alpha. Notable events it recorded included:

  * **October 2009**: Keith Packard pulled fixes from Matt Turner's xserver tree, making X.Org work on Alpha for the first time since the 1.5 series.
  * **October 2009**: Debian demoted Alpha as a release architecture.
  * **November 2009**: Matt Turner created a DEC Alpha/Linux kernel tree to collect Alpha patches; Linus Torvalds pulled it into mainline for 2.6.32.
  * **September 2010**: Torvalds pulled patches removing the Big Kernel Lock from arch/alpha and fixing a 14-year-old signal handling bug present since Linux 1.3.95.
  * **December 2011**: Uros Bizjak fixed the long-standing GCC bug 27468, which caused GCC to emit extra sign-extending instructions.
  * **May 2012**: Måns Rullgård's patches implementing more OSF/1 system calls, improving Linux's ability to run Tru64 UNIX binaries, were merged for Linux 3.5. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/torvalds/c/7a8bb98ca965|"alpha: implement various OSF/1 stat syscalls"]], Linux 3.5)]

The wiki attracted a small number of regular contributors, whose systems ranged from EV45-based AlphaStations to quad-EV68 and dual-EV7 AlphaServers.

In June 2012 the server crashed and the wiki database, which had not been backed up, was lost.

==== Later ownership (2015 to present) ====

In 2015, Petrakis and Payne, who still held the domain, transferred alphalinux.org to Gareth Randall, who maintained the site and a small wiki until mid-2026. Randall then transferred the domain to Turner, who created the [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org|alphalinux-org]] organization on GitHub to host repositories for Alpha packages (aboot, MILO), mailing list archives, and the content of this wiki. The wiki was rebuilt on DokuWiki in September 2026.
