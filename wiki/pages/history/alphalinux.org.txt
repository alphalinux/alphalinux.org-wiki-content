===== History of alphalinux.org =====

==== Origins ====

By mid-1998 the Alpha/Linux community had no central home. The primary reference was David Mosberger-Tang's personal site at **azstarnet.com/~axplinux**, which had gone quiet, while several smaller sites were appearing independently. In September 1998 Scott Lewis raised on **axp-list** the concern that there were too many miscellaneous sites and no current central reference. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L58904|"Fwd: Re: New Alph Website"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)]

Rich Payne had been running one such site at **adam.cheshire.net/~rdp/alpha** as part of his home ISP account in New Hampshire. He had started it because of the lack of a central site, and was already considering moving it to its own domain, suggesting **www.alphalinux.org**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L58904|"Fwd: Re: New Alph Website"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)]

On 30 September 1998 B. James Phillippe opened an **axp-list** thread arguing for a single top-level site that others could update and whose owner was committed to keeping it current. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L60716|"All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], B. James Phillippe, axp-list, 30 Sep 1998)] Payne offered to host it with co-maintainers and asked the list to choose between **alphalinux.org**, **linuxalpha.org**, and **axplinux.org**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L61788|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)] The list settled on **alphalinux.org**, partly because newcomers might not recognize "axp" as Alpha. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L49434|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Mark Hansel, axp-list, 30 Sep 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L50550|"Re: All These New Alpha Sites(tm)"]], Scott Lewis, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)]

Barrett Lyon, a data and network security consultant at Network Presence and a frequent **axp-list** contributor, registered the domain at his own expense and offered hosting. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L51627|"Re: alphalinux.org"]], Barrett G. Lyon, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L52391|"Re: alphalinux.org"]], Barrett G. Lyon, axp-list, 1 Oct 1998)] Payne built and maintained the site itself.

==== Launch ====

By 1 November 1998 Payne was signing list email with **%%http://www.alphalinux.org%%** and seeking the azstarnet.com maintainers in order to migrate the FAQ and other documents to the new site. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L2406|"Alpha Linux WebSite"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 1 Nov 1998)] He announced the relaunched site on **axp-list** on 12 November 1998. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L35539|"alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 12 Nov 1998)]

The day before the launch, Jonathan L Dubois had asked for the site to archive the Alpha mailing lists, citing gaps and poor search results in Red Hat's archive; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L30376|"axp mailing list archive mirror?"]], Jonathan L Dubois, axp-list, 11 Nov 1998)] Peter Petrakis and Maurice Hilarius supported the idea. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L30402|"Re: axp mailing list archive mirror?"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 11 Nov 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L30541|"Re: axp mailing list archive mirror?"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 11 Nov 1998)] Within a day of launch Payne was setting up archiving of both **axp-list** and the Debian list. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L38178|"alphalinux.org news"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 13 Nov 1998)] The site's initial purpose was aggregating existing documentation: the Alpha FAQ, SRM HOWTO, kernel build guides, and high-performance computing resources.

Within two weeks of launch, Adam C. Powell IV at NIST started a dedicated high-performance mailing list after Payne assembled a high-performance page on the new site at **%%http://www.alphalinux.org/docs/high_perf.html%%**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L62383|"High-performance on AXP-Linux"]], Adam C. Powell IV, axp-list, 23 Nov 1998)] That list, **high_perf**, later migrated to alphalinux.org as one of several community lists hosted there.

==== Growing the team ====

Lyon became the primary news editor and day-to-day voice of the site through 1999 and into 2000. Petrakis, a frequent **axp-list** participant since at least 1998, was posting under an **alphalinux.org** address by 12 February 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L21256|"Re: Multia woes"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 12 Feb 1999)] He built **%%ftp.alphalinux.org%%**, a public FTP archive for RPMs and other Alpha software, which by the end of that month carried the latest GNOME release and a nearly complete mirror of the Alpha material on ftp.digital.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L46823|"ftp.alphalinux.org has more cool stuff :)"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 28 Feb 1999)] The archive took community uploads through an **incoming/** directory, with the rule that packages be built with ''-mcpu=ev4'' so every Alpha could use them, optimized builds being welcome alongside an EV4-compatible version. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-May.mbox#L35703|"quick announcments"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 16 May 1999)]

Daniel Frasnelli contributed unofficial kernel 2.2.2 patches that let Alpha users compile the new kernel weeks before an official fix appeared, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-February.mbox#L37654|"[patches] patch-2.2.2-nonx86,patch-2.2.2-filter"]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 23 Feb 1999)] and was speaking for the site by March 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L35255|"AlphaLinux Org. web status..."]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 13 Mar 1999)] On the site's first anniversary he posted a request for comment on **axp-list** summarizing its first year, during which the group had received donations of web space, money, and hardware; it by then ran an administrative list at **admin@alphalinux.org**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-October.mbox#L2999|"AlphaLinux Org RFC"]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 4 Oct 1999)]

Ron Farrer had been active on **axp-list** as an Alpha-only hardware vendor throughout 1998 and 1999; by April 2000 he was writing bylined articles for the site, starting with a guide to installing Netscape using Compaq's Tru64 libraries. In May 2000 he and Payne launched **AlphaNews.net**, a general Alpha news site created by members of the Alpha Linux Organization. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-May.mbox#L17418|"ANNOUNCE: AlphaNews.net up and running!"]], Ron Farrer, axp-list, 25 May 2000)] Phillip Ezolt joined in March 2000 to centralize games and demos on Alpha, announced alongside a new job-openings section on the site. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-March.mbox#L21468|"ALO news (RE: Linux/Alpha work)"]], Dan Frasnelli, axp-list, 12 Mar 2000)]

==== Infrastructure ====

Before the domain existed, Payne's site was part of his home ISP account at cheshire.net, at **adam.cheshire.net/~rdp/alpha**; he moved its content to www.alphalinux.org when the new site opened in October 1998. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L58904|"Fwd: Re: New Alph Website"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 29 Sep 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L6491|"www.alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, debian-alpha, 27 Oct 1998)] The web server for alphalinux.org was in California, where TheShell.com hosted it free of charge. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L701|"Re: where has alphalinux.org gone ?"]], Larry Snyder, axp-list, 2 Aug 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L28414|"alphalinux.org and linuxalpha.org (was Re: Nvidia video cards)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 31 Jan 2002)] Payne's own machine, //talisman//, was at mv.com by August 1999 and handled his alphalinux.org mail and, later, the project's mailing lists. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L2447|"Re: X Servers and availability"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 3 Aug 1999)] The site suffered repeated upstream outages; a March 1999 disappearance was caused by Pacific Bell losing every northern California circuit. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L35255|"AlphaLinux Org. web status..."]], Daniel J. Frasnelli, axp-list, 13 Mar 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L35463|"www.alphalinux.org status"]], Barrett G. Lyon, axp-list, 14 Mar 1999)] When the FTP archive went down in September 1999 its contents stayed reachable over HTTP at **%%http://www.alphalinux.org/ftpsite%%**. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-September.mbox#L12319|"ftp.alphalinux.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 7 Sep 1999)] In April 1999 Compaq donated an AlphaServer 1200 to the project.

The mailing list archives were online at **%%http://www.alphalinux.org/archives%%** by May 1999, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-May.mbox#L27981|"Re: axp-list archives"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 13 May 1999)] and searchable from April 2000 at **%%/archives/search.shtml%%**, though indexing lagged the live list by months. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-June.mbox#L3037|"Re: list archives"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 5 Jun 2000)]

In addition to the main site and FTP archive, alphalinux.org hosted several mailing lists:

  * **high_perf**, high-performance Alpha/Linux, November 1998 to November 2001
  * **milo-list**, MILO boot loader development, April 1999 to November 2001
  * **axp-hardware** and **axp-software**, distribution-neutral hardware and software discussion, launched 4 December 1999

The last two followed a suggestion by Farrer for a general Alpha and Linux list independent of Red Hat, which ran **axp-list**; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L7509|"lists (was: Re: dell Dselling alphas?)"]], Ron Farrer, axp-list, 4 Dec 1999)] Payne set up both lists the same day, initially under majordomo. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L7715|"Re: lists (was: Re: dell Dselling alphas?)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 4 Dec 1999)] By May 2000 the lists had moved to Mailman at **lists.alphalinux.org**, running on talisman.mv.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/high_perf-mbox/2000-May.mbox#L1|"Welcome to the High-perf mailing list"]], high-perf-request, high_perf, 21 May 2000)]

In September 2000 the site added a hardware compatibility database, open to submissions from anyone, to track which devices worked in which Alpha systems. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-September.mbox#L19450|"ANNOUNCE Hardware Compatibility Database"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 8 Sep 2000)]

==== Decline ====

By 2002 the site was mostly unreachable. Its domain registrant, Lyon, could no longer be reached, and a backup site at **linuxalpha.org** ran on a machine offered by a Compaq employee. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L28414|"alphalinux.org and linuxalpha.org (was Re: Nvidia video cards)"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 31 Jan 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L18553|"Re: Wither alphalinux.org?"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 23 Jan 2002)]

The domain was recovered by the end of that year, and the site then ran on a machine at crl.dec.com. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-January.mbox#L1377|"Re: What's the current website? -- alphalinux.org, alphadriven.org"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 1 Jan 2003)] The content, however, stopped being updated.

==== Revival: the wiki era (2008 to 2012) ====

By 2008 alphalinux.org had gone years without updates. In April 2008, Matt Turner emailed Petrakis proposing that he take over maintenance. Petrakis agreed and suggested starting with a wiki section to see if it could attract contributors.

Turner created a MediaWiki installation in July 2008 and announced it on **axp-list** as a central location for Alpha hardware, Alpha/Linux support, SRM versions, Alpha assembly and optimization, and Alpha software. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-July.mbox#L1157|"AlphaLinux.org gets a Wiki"]], Matt Turner, axp-list, 22 Jul 2008)]

The announcement asked the community to contribute hardware photographs, hard-to-find SRM versions, documentation, assembly programming examples, and information about important Alpha bugs. It also noted that this was the first update to the site in years, and planned a forum and a move to a content management system; the forum was proposed to the list in January 2009 and never built. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2009-January.mbox#L3395|"Trying to gauge usage of a possible AlphaLinux.org Forum"]], Matt Turner, axp-list, 13 Jan 2009)] The wiki was used to coordinate work across distributions, for instance a patches page where the remaining Alpha ports could collect links to fixes still in flight. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2009-April.mbox#L506|"AlphaLinux Wiki: Patches page created"]], Matt Turner, axp-list, 18 Apr 2009)]

Turner also kept a news section on the wiki, tracking developments in the Linux kernel, GCC, glibc, and X.Org as they related to Alpha. Notable events it recorded included:

  * **October 2009**: Keith Packard pulled fixes from Turner's xserver tree, making X.Org work on Alpha for the first time since the 1.5 series.
  * **October 2009**: Debian demoted Alpha as a release architecture.
  * **November 2009**: Turner created a DEC Alpha/Linux kernel tree to collect Alpha patches; Linus Torvalds pulled it into mainline for 2.6.32.
  * **September 2010**: Torvalds pulled patches removing the Big Kernel Lock from arch/alpha and fixing a 14-year-old signal handling bug present since Linux 1.3.95.
  * **December 2011**: Uros Bizjak fixed the long-standing GCC bug 27468, which caused GCC to emit extra sign-extending instructions.
  * **May 2012**: Måns Rullgård's patches implementing Tru64 syscalls, which let Tru64 binaries including the C compiler run on Linux, were pulled by Torvalds.

The wiki attracted a small number of regular contributors, collectively running dozens of Alpha systems ranging from EV45-based AlphaStations to quad-EV68 and dual-EV7 AlphaServers.

In June 2012 the server crashed and the wiki database, which had not been backed up, was lost.

==== Later ownership (2015 to present) ====

In 2015, Petrakis and Payne, who still held the domain, transferred alphalinux.org to Gareth Randall, who maintained the site and a modestly populated wiki until mid-2026. Randall then transferred the domain to Turner, who created the [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org|alphalinux-org]] organization on GitHub to host repositories for Alpha packages (aboot, MILO), mailing list archives, and the content of this wiki. The wiki was rebuilt on DokuWiki in September 2026.
