===== Other Hardware =====

This page covers expansion cards and peripherals used in Alpha systems: graphics, storage, network, sound, USB, serial, and input devices. Digital sold many of these as options under its own part numbers, often rebadged from other vendors. Whether a card works on an Alpha depends on three things: whether the [[software:firmware|firmware]] can initialize it (and, for storage and network cards, boot from it), whether its Linux driver works on Alpha, and whether the card's addressing and bus requirements suit the system.

==== Common Problems ====

=== x86 Option ROMs ===

PC cards carry x86 code in their option ROMs. Alpha firmware must run a graphics card's video BIOS in an x86 emulator to initialize it. The emulator in MILO "started life as an x86 emulator, and has had [234]86 instruction handling patched in. It is far from robust", and cards such as the ATI Mach32 and Mach64 "seem to be dependent on the more advanced 386/486 instructions." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-January.mbox#L3377|"Re: interesting MILO problem w/card detection"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 16 Jan 1996)] MILO could be built with Digital's own BIOS emulation, which "supports most, if not all, of the standard graphics devices available", or with a free alternative. [(>{{wiki:software:boot_loaders:milo_howto.pdf?linkonly|Alpha Miniloader Howto}}, v0.85, 15 April 1997, pp. 4-5)] The Jensen's SRM console had no BIOS emulator. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-March.mbox#L36166|"Re: S3 928 video card on Jensen"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 27 Mar 2001)]

Newer video BIOSes were often too much for older consoles. nVidia Riva cards could not be used as the primary card because of "32-bit instructions in its BIOS", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-June.mbox#L34466|"Re: Diamond Viper550 PCI"]], Jeff Garzik, axp-list, 25 Jun 1999)] and "it wasn't until nearly the last console releases (read: BIOS emulator versions) of LX/SX that *any* nVidia cards would work". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-September.mbox#L4322|"Re: Well supported PCI graphics card for 164SX"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 6 Sep 2002)] The ATI Rage 128 and Radeon "are notoriously hard on BIOS emulators and don't work on most older boxes." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-April.mbox#L1474|"Re: Looking for a "good" video card for a DS5305"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Apr 2002)] Later, the X server's own x86 emulator could "POST video cards that the SRM fails to POST". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2009-October.mbox#L5944|"Re: X11 server on alpha -- update"]], Michael Cree, debian-alpha, 27 Oct 2009)]

SRM V5.6 for the AlphaServer ES40 added "an X86 bios emulator" to support the KZPCC-CE RAID controller. [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_es40_v7.3_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer ES40 V7.3 Firmware Release Notes}}, April 2007, p. 38)] On these systems the ''show bios'' command lists controllers with an expansion ROM, and ''run bios'' runs a card's ROM, for example the KZPCC-CE's configuration utility. [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_ds20_v7.3_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer DS20 V7.3 Firmware Release Notes}}, April 2007, p. 5)] Some cards' settings could only be changed with DOS-style utilities run under AlphaBIOS, such as the Adaptec 7895. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-May.mbox#L41463|"Re: Question about SCSI on DP264 motherboard"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 18 May 1999)]

=== Sparse and Dense Bus Access ===

The core logic of older systems can only reach PCI and ISA memory through "sparse" address spaces. The Jensen "has no DENSE access to bus memory, and must use *only* SPARSE accesses, which cannot be done transparently". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-April.mbox#L36611|"Re: Ati mach64 & alpha"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 23 Apr 1999)] X servers that assume dense access, such as the nVidia server, "will NOT work on machines that require SPARSE", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L12190|"Re: XFree support of Alpha"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 17 Jun 2001)] and "Matrox Millenniums do not work under X on EV4/EV5 SPARSE boxes". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2004-October.mbox#L2929|"AC-0.9 Testing Results (was: Re: Alpha Core ISO Images)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Oct 2004)]

=== DMA Addressing ===

Alpha systems have "no PCI DMA windows in the address range 0 - 16Mb, which makes "true" ISA devices (24-bit addresses) unsupported", including ISA bus masters such as the Adaptec 1540. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-June.mbox#L34907|"Re: Kernel questions (not mine)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Jun 1999)] The kernel limits ISA DMA to 16MB on the Ruffian and to 2GB on the Sable and some Alcors. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/include/asm/dma.h?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/include/asm/dma.h]], Linux 6.10)]

PCI cards that can only address part of the 32-bit space may fail. The SB Live's EMU10K1 "is incapable of issuing addresses larger than 512MB-1". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2002-July.mbox#L11087|"Re: SoundBlaster Live (emu10k1) on PWS433"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 18 Jul 2002)] Drivers that did not use the kernel's DMA mapping interfaces broke on systems with more than 2GB of memory; for example, "the stock DAC960 driver has NOT been ported to use the large memory API." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2002-November.mbox#L8473|"Re: Mylex Extremeraid 3000 + DAC960 woes."]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 18 Nov 2002)]

Network cards that only place receive buffers on 32-bit boundaries, such as those using the VIA Rhine, leave IP headers misaligned and cause floods of unaligned access traps unless the driver copies packets. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-December.mbox#L6642|"Re: Kernel unaligned accesses"]], Ivan Kokshaysky, axp-list, 11 Dec 2001)]

=== Personal Workstation 64-bit Slots ===

The Miata (Personal Workstation a and au series) has a small table of cards allowed in its 64-bit PCI slots, which are in front of the PCI-PCI bridge. With an unknown card there, SRM "will complain and NOT LET YOU BOOT"; the card can be moved to a 32-bit slot behind the bridge. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2002-September.mbox#L2942|"Re: 3d on alpha?"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 4 Sep 2002)] The check can be disabled with ''set pci_device_override -1'', but the Pyxis core logic "has bugs, and one of them is DMA crossing page boundaries when using the scatter/gather hardware." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2003-October.mbox#L3263|"Re: pci cards supported by SRM?"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 9 Oct 2003)] The recommendation "is *not* to put anything involving data storage in the 64 bit slots of any 1st generation PWS." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2012-June.mbox#L1331|"Re: USB cards for PWS 433au"]], Michael Cree, debian-alpha, 24 Jun 2012)]

=== TGA and TGA2 ===

The ZLXp-E cards use Digital's DC21030 "TGA" chip. The ZLXp-E1 is 8-plane, the E2 24-plane, and the E3 24-plane with a Z-buffer and 16MB. The Multia "has essentially an -E1 built into the motherboard." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-October.mbox#L44573|"Re: TGA graphics stuff (feature question)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 27 Oct 1996)] The first TGA X server supported only the 8-plane card. [(>''XF86_TGA-3.1.2-README'', Jay Estabrook, 23 January 1997)] The kernel's ''tgafb'' driver supports all three. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/Documentation/fb/tgafb.rst?h=v6.10|Documentation/fb/tgafb.rst]], Linux 6.10)] The ZLXp-L cards "use quite a different accelerator chipset" and are not supported. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-November.mbox#L21028|"Re: ZLXp-L Graphics Cards"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Nov 1998)]

The TGA2 cards, the PowerStorm 3D30 and 4D20, "use the Cirrus VGA for text-mode console, and TGA2 chipset for accelerated X." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L18272|"Re: Graphics in my PWS433au Miata"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 23 Jan 2002)] XFree86 supported them from 4.0.2, at first "Limited to 1024x768 at 72Hz resolution/refresh." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-February.mbox#L2660|"Re: LinuxAlpha hardware support"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Feb 2001)] The kernel framebuffer driver does not support them, but they "can however be used with the standard VGA Text Console driver." [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/Documentation/fb/tgafb.rst?h=v6.10|Documentation/fb/tgafb.rst]], Linux 6.10)] The PBXGB-AA's switch should be set to position six (1024x768 at 72Hz); Tru64 UNIX did not support position zero. [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_es40_v7.3_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer ES40 V7.3 Firmware Release Notes}}, April 2007, p. 20)] The "PowerStorm 350 is NOT supported by XFree86; never was, never will be". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-December.mbox#L10236|"Re: 7.2 iso's and Powerstorm video card"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 9 Dec 2002)]

=== PC Graphics Cards ===

Cards that worked well in Jay Estabrook's tests and recommendations include:

  * the ELSA GLoria Synergy, which passed both console and X tests on an AlphaServer 1200, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-April.mbox#L5533|"Re: Looking for a "good" video card for a DS5305"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 4 Apr 2002)]
  * Permedia 3 cards such as the Oxygen VX1, which "have 32MB and good RAMDAC performance", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-April.mbox#L1474|"Re: Looking for a "good" video card for a DS5305"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Apr 2002)]
  * the Matrox Millennium I and II, G200, and G400, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-April.mbox#L4242|"fedora x install"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 28 Apr 2008)] which "are fine choices for 2D work", with 3D only on AGP cards, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-September.mbox#L7813|"Re: Cirrus Logic alternative chipset?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 7 Sep 2001)]
  * the 3dfx Voodoo3 3000, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-April.mbox#L4378|"Another test iso image from AC1.0"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 18 Apr 2005)] whose 3D support later stopped working on Alpha when X dropped the Glide library, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2007-April.mbox#L330|"Voodoo 3 pci"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 24 Apr 2007)]
  * ATI Radeons, which "are the best bet on the DS10/DS20, and other EV6 or later machines". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-April.mbox#L4437|"fedora x install"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 28 Apr 2008)]

The "S3 Trio64 is notorious, especially in AS200/AS400 boxes", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-March.mbox#L17110|"Re: more pain in the arse"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 10 Mar 2000)] and "the Trio64V+, maybe aka AuroraV+, does NOT work well at all". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-July.mbox#L2797|"Re: XF86 for S3 Trio 64 164SX"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 11 Jul 2003)] On the UP1000 and UP1100, AGP cards work in PCI mode, but "none have been able to successfully activate the AGP GART support". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L12190|"Re: XFree support of Alpha"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 17 Jun 2001)] XFree86 4.x's int10 emulation crashed EV5 machines back to the SRM console with 3dfx cards, which ''Option "NoInt10"'' avoided. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-July.mbox#L23263|"Re: good video card for Alphas"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 19 Jul 2001)]

By 2016, with the older X drivers removed, "we are probably down to only the radeon KMS driver working on Alpha." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2016-April.mbox#L636|"Re: Multia state of support"]], Michael Cree, debian-alpha, 3 Apr 2016)]

=== Console Restrictions ===

  * On the AlphaServer 4000 and 4100, "VGAs configured as console display devices must be in PCI0." [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_4000_4100_v6.0_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 4100/4000 Firmware Release Notes, V6.0}}, September 2001, p. 3-5)]
  * On the ES40, "Only one VGA graphic controller card, that is VGA-Enabled, is supported in PCI0 bus slots." [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_es40_v7.3_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer ES40 V7.3 Firmware Release Notes}}, April 2007, p. 20)]
  * With SRM V5.9 and earlier on the ES40, an Adaptec controller in a lower-numbered slot than an Oxygen VX1 "may cause a console hang". [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_es40_v7.3_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer ES40 V7.3 Firmware Release Notes}}, April 2007, p. 19)]
  * On the UX164 (Ruffian), the VGA card must be in a 64-bit slot, "because only those slots are in front of the built-in PCI-PCI bridge". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L26949|"Re: Installing Linux on AlphaPC 164UX"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 12 Jun 1998)]
  * The AlphaServer 800's onboard S3 Trio64 must be disabled with jumper J27 to use another VGA card. [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_800_users_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 800 User's Guide}}, November 1997, p. 11-9)]

=== Booting from SRM ===

The AlphaServer 4000 and 4100 SRM console can boot from the KFPSA, KZPDA, KZPSA, KZPSC, KZPAC, KZPBA, KZPAA, and CIPCA storage adapters, the DE435, DE450, DE500, and DEFPA network adapters, the floppy drive, and the CD-ROM. It "does not support any EISA options as boot devices." [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_4000_4100_v6.0_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 4100/4000 Firmware Release Notes, V6.0}}, September 2001, p. 3-4)] The SRM console for evaluation boards and OEM motherboards has drivers for the NCR 810 and QLogic ISP1020 SCSI controllers, and the DC21040 and DE205 Ethernet controllers. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:common:srm_console_user_guide.pdf?linkonly|Alpha SRM Console for Alpha Microprocessor Motherboards User's Guide}}, September 1997, p. 1-3)]

For third-party cards, "SCSI cards that work with SRM console are QLogic ISP 1020/1040 and most NCR/Symbios (not 885)." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-July.mbox#L505|"Re: PWS433a and tru64"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 4 Jul 2000)] Originally "only ARC and AlphaBIOS know about Adaptecs, not SRM", and the consoles "will (probably) not ever support any BusLogic cards." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-June.mbox#L15694|"Re: Any1 with a working PC164!"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 Jun 1997)] Later SRM releases added "Adaptec (but not all of them), Intel EEPro100 NIC, NCR/Symbios 896, and a couple of recent Mylex RAID controllers." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2000-September.mbox#L1114|"Re: srm and scsi controllers."]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 6 Sep 2000)] SRM could boot from Adaptec cards on the AlphaPC 164LX and 164SX with firmware 5.8, and on the DS10, DS20, ES40, and XP1000 with 5.9. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-April.mbox#L35378|"Re: Alpha PC164, SRM, and Adaptec 2940UW"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 11 Apr 2001)] Some DVD drives "are incompatible with the SRM console expectations (usually block-size related)." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-December.mbox#L12579|"Re: would like to do more upgrades"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 10 Dec 2002)]

=== QLogic ===

The Linux ''qlogicisp'' driver had "some problems" with the ISP1040B, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-May.mbox#L23207|"Re: Need help getting MILO to boot"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 May 1998)] and was "*very* poor" with tape and CD-RW drives. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L1901|"Re: QLogic ISP problem"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Jan 2002)] Jay Estabrook recommended "NCR over QLogic, especially if using the stock kernel drivers". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L25599|"Re: SUMMARY: SRM compatible UW SCSI controller"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 30 Jan 2002)] The AlphaPC 164LX errata note "Compatibility problems ... between the AlphaPC 164LX motherboard and QLogic SCSI controller chips (ISP1020 and ISP1040)." [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:pc164lx:pc164lx_technical_reference_manual_errata.pdf?linkonly|AlphaPC 164LX Motherboard Errata Sheet}}, June 1997, p. 1)]

=== Mylex DAC960 ===

Digital sold Mylex DAC960 RAID controllers with its own firmware. The Linux driver wanted firmware 2.73 or later, but "many of the older DAC960s sold by DEC, in such boxes as the 7305, have DEC custom firmware 2.70 or older." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-March.mbox#L2019|"Re: digital server 7305, DAC960PU"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 4 Mar 2003)] The kernel accepts firmware 2.70 on Alpha, which "appears to work quite well with this driver". [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/drivers/scsi/myrb.c?h=v6.10|drivers/scsi/myrb.c]], Linux 6.10)] SRM can boot from DAC960 volumes. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L19351|"DAC960 Alpha support (was: Re: Redhat 7.2 on a DEC ALPHA 2100)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 26 Feb 2003)]

In July 2000 Compaq announced Linux support for Mylex controllers on the DS10, DS20, DS20E, and ES40: the SN-KZPBC-AA with kernel 2.2.14 and SRM V5.7, and the FR-PCTAR-IB and -IC with kernel 2.2.15 and SRM V5.8, both with DAC960 driver 2.2.5. [(>''SN-KZPBC-AA Backplane Raid SCSI Is Now Supported on the DS10 System Under Linux Only'', Compaq, 6 July 2000)] [(>''FR-PCTAR-IB/-IC Raid SCSI Controllers Are Now Supported on the DS10, DS20, DS20E, and ES40 Systems Under Linux'', Compaq, 28 July 2000)]

=== Other Storage Controllers ===

  * **Adaptec:** the 2940UW and 39160 worked in 2004 tests. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2004-October.mbox#L2929|"AC-0.9 Testing Results (was: Re: Alpha Core ISO Images)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Oct 2004)] Jay Estabrook did the initial Alpha support in the ''aic7xxx'' driver. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/drivers/scsi/aic7xxx/aic7xxx_osm.c?h=v6.10|drivers/scsi/aic7xxx/aic7xxx_osm.c]], Linux 6.10)] The KZPEA "must have bus termination jumpers installed or the console may hang". [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_es40_v7.3_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer ES40 V7.3 Firmware Release Notes}}, April 2007, p. 20)]
  * **Fibre Channel:** in 2000 "the KGPSA-CA is an Emulex adapter, not a QLogic one", and Emulex's Linux drivers were binary-only for x86. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-December.mbox#L13600|"Re: FC with fabric topology"]], Peter Rival, axp-list, 12 Dec 2000)]
  * **IDE:** the AlphaPC 164 uses a CMD646 for onboard IDE. [(>{{wiki:hardware:motherboards:pc164:pc164_user_manual.pdf?linkonly|AlphaPC 164 Motherboard User's Manual}}, p. 3)] For weak onboard IDE, Jay Estabrook suggested "an add-in PCI card controller, using CMD649 or Promise or such like." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2004-April.mbox#L2047|"Second IDE port not working on PC164"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Apr 2004)]

==== Network ====

^ Digital part ^ Description ^ Chip ^ Linux driver ^
| DE205 | ISA Ethernet | | |
| DE422 | EISA Ethernet | | |
| DE425 | EISA Ethernet | | ''de4x5'', until Linux 5.18 |
| DE434, DE435 | PCI Ethernet | DC21040 | ''de2104x'' or ''tulip''; ''de4x5'' until Linux 5.18 |
| DE450 | EtherWORKS Turbo PCI 10 | DC21041 | ''de2104x'' or ''tulip''; ''de4x5'' until Linux 5.18 |
| DE500 | Fast EtherWORKS PCI 10/100 | DC2114x | ''tulip''; ''de4x5'' until Linux 5.18 |
| DE600, DE602 | PCI 10/100 Ethernet | Intel 8255x | ''e100'' (''eepro100'' in older kernels) |
| DEGPA | Gigabit Ethernet | Alteon | ''acenic'' |
| DEFEA, DEFPA | EISA and PCI FDDI | | ''defxx'' |

The DE450's chip is the DC21041. [(>''EtherWORKS Turbo PCI 10 Adapter'', Digital infosheet, 9 October 1995, p. 2)] The DE600 "is based on Intel 82557/8/9, and works with the EV6 consoles (and some EV56 like LX and SX)". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-August.mbox#L31580|"Re: How to setup my 3 Com 3C509B ISA cards for Alpha machine?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Aug 2000)] The ''defxx'' driver supports the DEFEA and DEFPA. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/drivers/net/fddi/defxx.c?h=v6.10|drivers/net/fddi/defxx.c]], Linux 6.10)] The ''de4x5'' driver was removed in Linux 5.19. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/torvalds/c/32c53420d2a0|"eth: de4x5: remove support for Generic DECchip & DIGITAL EtherWORKS PCI/EISA"]], Linux 5.19)]

For Digital's Tulip cards, Jay Estabrook said in 1997 that "the DE4X5 driver is preferred", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-January.mbox#L20086|"Re: EB164/NCR SCSI woes (once more...)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 17 Jan 1997)] while Richard Henderson used "the tulip driver exclusively over de4x5." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L21351|"Re: axp update for 2.3.13"]], Richard Henderson, axp-list, 14 Aug 1999)] At 100Mbit, the 21142 and 21143 sometimes needed boot arguments "to force the tulip.c driver to handle a specific media type". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L57005|"Re: XP1000 and Linux"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 18 Mar 1999)] The SRM console is also particular about third-party Tulip cards: one user "had to go through a pile of 15 tulip chip cards until I found one it liked." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L47109|"Re: PC164, SRM and ethernet"]], Matthew Jacob, axp-list, 29 Dec 1999)] Digital recommended against DE500 auto-negotiation when the network speed is known, "because of possible considerable delay when booting". [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_4000_4100_v6.0_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 4100/4000 Firmware Release Notes, V6.0}}, September 2001, p. 3-7)]

For the DE600 and other Intel 8255x cards, the ''e100'' driver caused "more than 10 million unaligned accesses" during one install, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-October.mbox#L1682|""e100" ethernet driver problem in AC-1.0 and Centos-4.2"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 20 Oct 2005)] and Jay Estabrook recommended ''eepro100'' instead. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-November.mbox#L517|"gnome installation problem"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 4 Nov 2008)]

Other cards reported to work include NE2000 clones, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L8828|"Re: Ethernet card question"]], David Mosberger, axp-list, 28 Mar 1996)] the RTL8139, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2002-May.mbox#L12933|"Re: Realtek 8139 metwork card"]], Falk Hueffner, debian-alpha, 18 May 2002)] the RTL8169, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-June.mbox#L30|"GigE on ES4x servers"]], Balint Cristian, axp-list, 1 Jun 2005)] and the DEGPA-SA, which with the ''acenic'' driver worked "quite well". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-June.mbox#L1|"GigE on ES4x servers"]], Gary Molenkamp, axp-list, 1 Jun 2005)]

==== Sound ====

  * ISA Sound Blaster 16 and Pro Audio Spectrum 16 cards "are known to work out of the box." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L2952|"RE: How to complete a Jensen system..."]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 10 Mar 1996)] ISA Windows Sound System cards also work. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2004-May.mbox#L10002|"Re: ALSA sound on an AS255"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 10 May 2004)]
  * The Miata "has an ES1888 builtin, which is (at least) SBPro compatible". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-July.mbox#L23336|"Re: Sound on Miata (PWS500a)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 9 Jul 1998)] Under ALSA it works with ''snd-sb8''. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-October.mbox#L9574|"Re: 2.6.0-test9 partial success"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 28 Oct 2003)]
  * Compaq sold an Ensoniq PCI audio card (SN-AVH10-AB) only with "Linux-ready" AlphaStation XP900 packages. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_xp900:alphastation_xp900_catalog.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation XP900}}, Systems and Options Catalog, September 1999, p. 6)] In 1999, the es1370 driver crashed the kernel, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-April.mbox#L1263|"Re: es1371 and alphas (fix)"]], Ivan Kokshaysky, axp-list, 1 Apr 1999)] and an ES1371 locked up an AlphaPC 164LX "any time I attempt sound I/O, in any slot." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L22368|"Re: Sound cards on the Alpha."]], Ed Hall, axp-list, 8 Mar 1999)]
  * The M-Audio Revolution 7.1 works on the XP1000 and PWS 600au. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2008-April.mbox#L1|"Re: M-Audio Revolution 7.1 sound card causing crash on Alpha"]], Michael Cree, debian-alpha, 1 Apr 2008)]
  * The SB Live cannot address Miata's DMA window (see [[#dma_addressing|DMA Addressing]]).

"The builtin USB is not recommended; your best option for USB is to install an add-in card." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2012-June.mbox#L2873|"Re: new video card (was Re: USB cards for PWS 433au)"]], Bob Tracy, debian-alpha, 28 Jun 2012)]

==== Serial and Multiport Cards ====

Drivers that access registers in bus memory need porting to Alpha: of a Cyclades driver, Jay Estabrook wrote "*NO*, it will need reworking". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L2952|"RE: How to complete a Jensen system..."]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 10 Mar 1996)] By August 1997 the Cyclades kernel driver was "already working for all boards (PCI and ISA, Cyclom-Y and Cyclades-Z)" on Alpha. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-August.mbox#L5784|"Cyclades Linux driver under Alpha"]], Ivan Passos, axp-list, 8 Aug 1997)] In 2000, Maurice Hilarius reported modifying the Comtrol RocketPort drivers "to work on Alpha". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-November.mbox#L5903|"Re: Linux Driver"]], Maurice Hilarius, axp-list, 3 Nov 2000)]

Digital's own serial options included the DigiBoard-based CXI01 multiport cards and the PBXDA PCI 4, 8, and 16-port asynchronous adapters. [(>''AlphaServer 2100A Supported Options List'', Digital, March 4, 1997)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:personal_workstation:personal_workstation_a_au_series_catalog.pdf?linkonly|Compaq DPW Enhanced a-Series & au-Series}}, Systems and Options Catalog, September 2000, p. 7)]

==== Keyboards and Mice ====

Digital's PC-style keyboards were the LK411 and the LK471 (for Windows NT and UNIX), and the LK461 had the OpenVMS layout. [(>''AlphaServer 1000A 5/333, 5/4xx, 5/5xx Supported Options List'', Digital, April 14, 1997, "KB & Mouse")] The SRM console "will assume/force serial console if you unplug the keyboard", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-July.mbox#L2188|"Re: installation on PC164LX, SRM 5.1-3, serial console"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 1 Jul 1998)] and "SRM is a bit more picky than AlphaBIOS" about keyboards. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-April.mbox#L6165|"Re: WAS: RE: SRM serial -> keyboard NOW: 5300 server won't boot"]], Ivan Kokshaysky, axp-list, 7 Apr 2000)] On the ES40, removing the mouse at the SRM console "will prevent any response from the keyboard (until the mouse is plugged back in)." [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_es40_v7.3_firmware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer ES40 V7.3 Firmware Release Notes}}, April 2007, p. 20)] The EV7 systems use USB keyboards and mice; under Tru64 UNIX they support only USB keyboards. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_es47:alphaserver_es47_tower_quickspecs.pdf?linkonly|HP AlphaServer ES47 Tower QuickSpecs}}, November 2003, pp. 15-16)]
