===== DEC 2000 AXP and DECpc 150 AXP (Jensen) =====

"The Jensen was an EISA-based system with a 21064 Alpha processor running at 150MHz." The DECpc 150 AXP and DEC 2000 AXP "differed only in the bundled software." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L4787|"Jensen-HOWTO: First pass"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 15 Mar 1996)] The DEC 2000 Model 500 was code-named Culzean. [(>{{wiki:documentation:references:alpha_axp_system_reference_manual_v6.pdf?linkonly|Alpha AXP System Reference Manual}}, Version 6, 1994, p. D-2)] Olivetti sold a rebadged version as the R7-600. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L2952|"RE: How to complete a Jensen system..."]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 10 Mar 1996)] The Jensen was the first machine to run Linux/Alpha, with a 32-bit kernel "on a JENSEN, followed by AVANTI (as400)". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2006-July.mbox#L572|"first Linux port"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 Jul 2006)]

A //Digital Technical Journal// paper traces where the machine came from. In 1990 "conventional wisdom said that the DECchip 21064 microprocessor could not be used to build low-cost computer systems", so Digital's product groups were waiting for the 21066 instead. Members of the Semiconductor Engineering Advanced Development Group, the group that had designed the 21064, built a demonstration system called Beta from February 1991 with help from the Cambridge Research Laboratory: a 100 MHz 21064 in a tabletop enclosure, cooled by a heat sink alone at about 16 W, with a 128 KB write-back cache 128 bits wide built from SRAMs meant for the Intel 82385 cache controller, and 64-bit main memory of standard 9-bit SIMMs protected by longword parity. Beta was followed by Theta, which added an EISA bus, and then by the DECpc AXP 150 itself. The design philosophy the authors give is one of the product's own advertising slogans: "It's just a PC, only faster." The base system deliberately contained little, with capabilities left to ISA and EISA option cards so that a single design could be configured for different market segments. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-June.mbox#L7939|"The Evolution of the Alpha AXP PC"]], David G. Conroy, Thomas E. Kopec, and Joseph R. Falcone, Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 7 Jun 1994)]

==== Specifications ====

  * [[wp>Alpha_21064|21064]] at 150 MHz [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L4787|"Jensen-HOWTO: First pass"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 15 Mar 1996)]
  * EISA only: it is the only Alpha platform in the kernel without PCI [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_jensen.c?h=v6.9|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_jensen.c]], Linux 6.9)]
  * No dense memory space, only sparse [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-May.mbox#L13068|"Re: Beta Xservers based on 3.1.2E for the Alpha"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 19 May 1996)]
  * The real-time clock is a PC part at a non-standard address: "The Jensen RTC is kind of strange - it's the same as a normal PC, except it's at the wrong address! (The whole Jensen IO subsystem is rather strange - DEC was just learning how to do it...)" [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-May.mbox#L6117|"Re: /dev/rtc on Jensen?"]], Linus Torvalds, axp-list, 8 May 1996)]
  * Both SRM and ARC firmware, but only one at a time: the ARC console can read the floppy drive and the SRM console cannot, and ARC cannot switch to the OSF PALcode that Linux needs [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L4787|"Jensen-HOWTO: First pass"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 15 Mar 1996)]
  * The Jensen and Noname "had only 64-bit mem bus, and thus required SIMMS in pairs, not quartets". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-December.mbox#L1027|"Alpha hardware"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 5 Dec 2008)] See Conflicting Information.
  * Digital's specification table gives the processor as the "DECchip 21064 (EV4 P2 or P3)", 1 MB of boot and diagnostic firmware ROM, and 16 MB of DRAM expandable to 128 MB. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:decpc_axp_150_dec_2000_model_300_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DECpc AXP 150 and DEC 2000 Model 300 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, EK-A0636-TM.001, p. E-2)]
  * Memory goes in eight connectors on the system module, grouped in two banks. "A memory option consists of four memory modules", every connector in a bank must be filled, the two module types must not be mixed within a bank, and bank 0 must always hold an option. The only options were 16 MB and 64 MB, and the DEC 2000 Model 300 AXP required at least 32 MB. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:decpc_axp_150_dec_2000_model_300_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DECpc AXP 150 and DEC 2000 Model 300 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, pp. 12-6 to 12-7)]
  * The deskside DECpc AXP 150 and DEC 2000 Model 300 have one keyboard port, one mouse port, two serial ports, one parallel port, and six EISA connectors; the pedestal DEC 2000 Model 500 has one serial port and no keyboard or mouse port in its specification table, the same six EISA slots, and weighs 54 kg against the 21 kg of the deskside box. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:decpc_axp_150_dec_2000_model_300_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DECpc AXP 150 and DEC 2000 Model 300 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, p. E-2)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:dec_2000_model_500_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DEC 2000 Model 500 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, EK-A0676-TM.001, p. E-2)]
  * SCSI bus letters follow EISA slot order: the system "assigns the bus letter A to the SCSI adapter in the lowest EISA slot number", and the console's ''SET SCSI_{A | B}'' command sets each adapter's host ID. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:dec_2000_model_500_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DEC 2000 Model 500 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, p. 11-6)]

==== Switching Consoles ====

Which console the machine runs is held in the ''OS_TYPE'' environment variable, whose values are ''VMS'', ''OSF'', and ''NT'', and which "you can modify ... from both firmware components". A second variable, ''AUTO_ACTION'', sets the power-up action (''RESTART'', ''HALT'', or ''BOOT'') and applies only to OpenVMS and OSF/1. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:decpc_axp_150_dec_2000_model_300_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DECpc AXP 150 and DEC 2000 Model 300 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, p. 1-2)]

From SRM, ''SET OS_TYPE NT'' followed by Ctrl/P or the halt button brings up the ARC "Boot menu", which identifies itself as "ARC Multiboot DEC Version n.nn". [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:decpc_axp_150_dec_2000_model_300_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DECpc AXP 150 and DEC 2000 Model 300 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, pp. 1-6 to 1-7)] Going back is a menu walk rather than a command: Supplementary menu, Set up the system, "Switch to OpenVMS or OSF console", then the reboot selection menu, and finally a full power cycle with a 15 second wait so that "electrostatic charges in the power supply and on the system module" dissipate. The console prompt appears on power-up, or after Ctrl/P if the system starts to boot. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:decpc_axp_150_dec_2000_model_300_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DECpc AXP 150 and DEC 2000 Model 300 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, pp. 1-8 to 1-9)]

==== Installing Linux ====

There is no MILO for the Jensen, because "Jensen is sufficiently different from the PCI-based systems that porting the MILO PALcode would be a significant undertaking", so Linux runs under the OSF PALcode of the SRM console. Since the SRM console cannot read the floppy drive, Jim Paradis's 1996 procedure partitions the disk and copies a kernel to it from the ARC console using the standalone ''minlabel.exe'' and ''copyboot.exe'' utilities, then switches the machine to the SRM console to boot. The SRM console also stores at most 8 characters of boot flags, or 16 on the command line, so his kernel understood ''INSTALL'' as an abbreviation for ''root=/dev/fd0 load_ramdisk=1''. Because SRM cannot read an fdisk-style partition table, Linux and Windows NT cannot share a disk. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L4787|"Jensen-HOWTO: First pass"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 15 Mar 1996)]

Digital later published the 1.7 firmware with an installation procedure, and early versions of aboot needed it: "You should be running firmware version 1.7". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-November.mbox#L37622|"Re: Error received Installing RedHat AXP150"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 21 Nov 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-February.mbox#L6582|"Jensen firmware 1.7 on ftp.digital.com"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 13 Feb 1998)] See Conflicting Information.

Red Hat 7.2 "has been tested on JENSEN and will boot and install directly from CD-ROM." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-August.mbox#L11016|"Re: Installing RH 7.1 on Jensen"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 14 Aug 2002)]

==== Linux ====

  * The Jensen needs its own kernel: "Jensens can't boot the generic kernel image, they need a custom one". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2002-May.mbox#L8369|"Re: Debian on Alpha DEC 2000 AXP"]], Falk Hueffner, debian-alpha, 11 May 2002)]
  * Early glibc did not implement ''inb'' and ''outb'' for the Jensen or the EB164, which broke the graphics libraries. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L8456|"Re: EB164 libraries and hardware access"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 26 Mar 1996)]
  * For X, "the only card supported on Jensen under XFree86 is an S3-based ISA card. The S3 driver had *extensive* hacking done to it to support Jensen". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-February.mbox#L13405|"Re: EISA video card for AlphaLinux Jensen XP 150"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 9 Feb 2001)]

==== Conflicting Information ====

The following sources disagree with each other or with this page. They are listed here for further investigation.

  * **Jensen firmware needed for Linux**
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-December.mbox#L16885|"VGA problems in a Jensen, was: Re: headless boot"]], Martin Ostermann, axp-list, 11 Dec 1996: "make sure you got v1.7 disks to switch back, because aboot doesn't work with later versions."
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-June.mbox#L2006|"Re: Installing linux on axp150"]], Sebastian Moeller, axp-list, 3 Jun 2000: "IIRC v1.7 is only required for the ELSA Winner 1000 ISA/EISA (twinbus)"
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-February.mbox#L19873|"RE: Downgrade firmware"]], H.C.vanderVegt@DTO.TUDelft.nl, axp-list, 20 Feb 2001: "the old aboot till 0.5 needs rom version 1.7 and the new aboot 0.7 supports the new roms (not official)"
  * **Memory modules per bank**
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-December.mbox#L1027|"Alpha hardware"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 5 Dec 2008: the Jensen and Noname "had only 64-bit mem bus, and thus required SIMMS in pairs, not quartets".
    * {{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_2000:decpc_axp_150_dec_2000_model_300_customer_technical_information.pdf?linkonly|DECpc AXP 150 and DEC 2000 Model 300 AXP Customer Technical Information}}, pp. 12-6 to 12-7: "A memory option consists of four memory modules. When you install a memory option in a memory bank, you must install a memory module in all of the connectors in that bank."
