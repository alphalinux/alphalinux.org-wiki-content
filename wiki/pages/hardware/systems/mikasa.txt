The AlphaServer 1000 is a single processor PCI/EISA server introduced in November 1994. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Owner's Guide}}, November 1994)] The CPU is on a daughtercard, so the same enclosure was sold with 21064 and 21064A CPUs as the 4/200, 4/233 [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_service_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Service Guide}}, pp. A-4, A-5)], and 4/266 models [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Technical Summary}}, p. 2)] and later with 21164 CPUs as the 5/xxx models. The AlphaServer 1000 is Mikasa and the 1000A is Noritake, and both were built with either EV4 (4/xxx) or EV5 (5/xxx) CPUs. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-March.mbox#L30004|"Re: AlphaServer 800"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Mar 1998)] The EV5 models are Mikasa-Primo, also called Mikasa-Pinnacle, and use CIA core logic rather than the APECS of the EV4 models. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L23114|"Re: kernel for mikasa"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 19 Jan 1998)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_service_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Service Guide}}, p. 5-19)] The system was sold as a deskside unit, in a cabinet, and as a rackmount unit. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Technical Summary}}, p. 2)] Current kernels support only Mikasa-Primo systems with 21164A (EV56) CPUs: Linux 6.10 removed the EV4 Mikasa and support for the original 21164 (EV5), so 6.9 was the last kernel for the 4/xxx models and for the 5/300. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/commit/?id=a4184174be36|"alpha: drop pre-EV56 support"]], Linux 6.10)] The 5/300 has a 300 MHz 21164 (EV5). [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_illustrated_parts_breakdown.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Series Illustrated Parts Breakdown}}, Figure 1, item 6)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_1000a_model_5xxx_owners_guide_supplement.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000/1000A Model 5/xxx Owner's Guide Supplement}}, p. 2-5)] The AlphaServer 1000 accepts EV56 CPU daughtercards, and the AlphaServer 1000A uses the same daughtercards; [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-April.mbox#L52630|"Re: which AlphaServers can I use"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 30 Apr 1997)] the 5/333 and 5/400 models of both have a 21164A. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000a:alphaserver_1000a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000A Technical Summary}}, p. 2)]

==== Specifications ====

Except where noted, the following describes the 4/266 model. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Technical Summary}}, pp. 2-3)]

  * 266 MHz [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21064A|21064A]] on an upgradable CPU daughtercard, with 8 KiB of serial ROM for power-up code
  * 2 MiB of direct-mapped, ECC protected level 2 cache on the daughtercard
  * 21071/21072 (APECS) core logic on EV4 models, 21171 (CIA) on the EV5 models
  * 128-bit ECC protected memory bus
  * 20 SIMM sockets in four banks of five (four data plus one for ECC), taking 16, 32, 64, 128, and 256 MB modules, 16 MB to 1 GB total; all modules in a bank must be the same capacity, and bank 0 must be populated. Banks of different sizes can be mixed, with the larger SIMMs in bank 0 [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1996-July.mbox#L7581|"SUMMARY: Mixing SIMMs of different densities on AlphaServer 1000"]], Phil Farrell, alpha-osf-managers, 23 Jul 1996)], and third-party ECC SIMMs work in sets of five [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1997-June.mbox#L6335|"SUMMARY: PC memory in Alpha 1000"]], Pulak Rakshit, alpha-osf-managers, 23 Jun 1997)]
  * Three 33 MHz PCI slots [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Owner's Guide}}, p. 1-10)]
  * Eight 8.33 MHz EISA/ISA slots, all bus master capable [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Owner's Guide}}, p. 1-10)]
  * Onboard Cirrus SVGA controller [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_service_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Service Guide}}, p. 5-20)], reported to be a Cirrus 5424 [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-November.mbox#L36383|"Re: Kernel for AlphaServer 1000 (Success!... and a question)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 Nov 1997)]
  * Onboard NCR 53C810 Fast SCSI-2 controller with an external connector [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_service_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Service Guide}}, p. 2-2)]
  * PCI-to-EISA bridge, Xbus for the real-time clock, NVRAM, FlashROM, and operator control panel
  * 1 MiB of FlashROM for firmware and diagnostics, 8 KiB of NVRAM for configuration
  * Bidirectional parallel port, two serial ports, floppy disk controller, keyboard and mouse interface, PC-style speaker
  * Wide-ready dual SCSI backplane and one internal BA350 StorageWorks shelf for up to seven 3.5-inch devices, plus 3.5-inch floppy, 5.25-inch CD-ROM, and an optional 5.25-inch tape bay
  * Optional redundant power supply
  * Both SRM and ARC firmware

==== Linux ====

  * The Mikasa should be booted only from the SRM console. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-July.mbox#L37128|"Re: AlphaServer 1000 5/xxx isntall"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 Jul 1999)] A MILO port for the Mikasa existed but was incomplete and unreliable. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-November.mbox#L12903|"Re: installing as1000a (noritake)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 7 Nov 2000)]
  * Through Linux 6.9 the kernel selected the machine vector at run time by CPU type, using ''mikasa_primo_mv'' for EV5 and EV56 and ''mikasa_mv'' otherwise. ''mikasa_mv'' no longer exists: it was removed in Linux 6.10, and current kernels have only ''mikasa_primo_mv''. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_mikasa.c?h=v6.9|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_mikasa.c]], Linux 6.9)]
  * The AlphaServer 1000 and 1000A have quite different interrupt delivery mechanisms, so a kernel built for one platform does not run on the other. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-November.mbox#L36383|"Re: Kernel for AlphaServer 1000 (Success!... and a question)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 Nov 1997)]

==== Graphics ====

The onboard Cirrus VGA can be disabled by a jumper at the top left of the motherboard, not on the CPU daughtercard, and through the EISA Configuration Utility, in order to use another card. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-November.mbox#L36383|"Re: Kernel for AlphaServer 1000 (Success!... and a question)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 Nov 1997)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000:alphaserver_1000_service_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000 Service Guide}}, p. 5-38)]

==== History ====

The 4/200 arrived in November 1994. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L1|"AlphaServer 1000 4/200 System"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Nov 1994)] Rackmount (RM) and cabinet (CAB) versions were announced at the same time, with the same CPU, memory, power supply, and I/O as the pedestal system but a different chassis with two PCI slots, seven EISA bus master slots, and one interleaved PCI/EISA slot. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L189|"AlphaServer 1000 4/200 RM and CAB Systems"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Nov 1994)] The 4/233 was announced on 24 April 1995. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-May.mbox#L860|"Press/Digital Speeds Up AlphaServer 1000 System"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 4 May 1995)] The 4/266 in turn replaced the 4/233 and raised the memory ceiling to 1 GiB. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-October.mbox#L660|"AlphaServer 1000 4/266 System"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 20 Oct 1995)]
