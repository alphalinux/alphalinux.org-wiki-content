===== AlphaServer 2100A and 2000A (Lynx) =====

The AlphaServer 2100A is the successor to the [[hardware:systems:sable|AlphaServer 2100]], with the standard I/O functions moved onto the system backplane and most of the EISA slots traded for PCI slots. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, pp. 5-6)] The AlphaServer 2000A is the two CPU version: "If it truly is an AS2000A, then that is actually DEMI-LYNX and is related to the LYNX (AS2100A) the way DEMI_SABLE is to SABLE; ie it holds only 2 CPUs and two memory boards, instead of up to 4 CPUs". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-September.mbox#L2367|"Powerup Problem on AS2000"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 26 Sep 2005)] Digital announced the 2100A LP, a low profile midrange model, with a 5/375 processor option on 7 November 1996, at the same time as the 5/375 models of the [[hardware:systems:sable|AlphaServer 2000]]. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-November.mbox#L502|"Press/Digital Reduces Prices, Adds Power to Midrange AlphaServer Systems"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 8 Nov 1996)]

==== Specifications ====

  * "The enclosure supports up to four CPUs ... Up to 2 gigabytes of high-capacity memory are supported (1 gigabyte on 4-CPU configurations)", with "8 PCI slots, 3 EISA slots", in the 4/275, 5/250, and 5/300 models [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Owner's Guide}}, pp. 1-2, 1-3)]
  * 275 MHz [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21064A|21064A]] with 1 MiB of backup cache on the 4/275 models, and a [[wp>Alpha_21164|21164]] at 291 MHz with 4 MiB of backup cache on the 5/300 models [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, p. 3)]
  * The same 128-bit system bus as the AlphaServer 2100, with 666 MB/s peak bandwidth, and memory modules of 64, 128, and 512 MB [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, pp. 4-5)]
  * 33 MHz PCI and 8.33 MHz EISA, with the standard I/O functions (1 MB flash ROM, 8 KB NVRAM, TOY clock, serial, parallel, floppy, keyboard and mouse) on the system backplane rather than a separate I/O module [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, pp. 5-6)]
  * Ethernet is an adapter rather than an onboard controller, unlike the AlphaServer 2100 [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, p. 5)]
  * A low profile rackmount version, the AlphaServer 2100A LP, was also sold [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_lp_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A LP Installation/Owner's/Service Guide}})]
  * The eight PCI slots are split by an onboard DECchip 21050 PCI-to-PCI bridge: "Physical slots 4, 5, 6, and 7 are the bottom 4 slots. These slots are in front of the bridge (primary PCI). Physical slots 0, 1, 2, and 3 are the top 4 slots. These slots are behind the bridge (secondary PCI)." [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, EK-2100A-CL.C02, June 1996, p. 1)]
  * Although the system has three EISA slots, it reuses the AlphaServer 2100's EISA Configuration Utility file, so the ECU "shows 8 EISA slots available ... EISA slots 4-7 in the ECU should be ignored". A matching harmless power-up complaint, "ECU error, slot 0, found DEC2A01, expected nothing", "does not affect system performance and should be ignored". [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, pp. 3-4)]
  * Rackmount systems take a second power supply for redundancy, activated by jumper W1 on the PCI/EISA backplane; the same jumper does not enable the optional remote server management module. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, p. 4)]

==== Option restrictions ====

Digital's release notes list which PCI options have to sit in front of the bridge. The KZPSA-BB Fast Wide Differential SCSI controller and the PB2GA-Jx S3 Trio64 graphics card must be in front of it to run Windows NT or any ARC firmware utility; behind the bridge the SRM console reports "***unable to assign PCI base address", which "can be ignored if you are running OpenVMS or Digital UNIX". The KZPSM and KZPDA adapters need SRM 4.4 or later to work behind the bridge, and the KZPSC RAID controller's own documentation forbids a position behind a bridge, but "this restriction does not apply on AlphaServer 2100A systems". At most two KFESA or KFESB EISA DSSI controllers can be fitted, and the DE422-SA, DE425-AA, DJ-ML200, PB2HA-SA, PB2SX-AA, and PBXGA adapters are not supported at all. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, pp. 1-3)]

Two console variables come with warnings: the default ''pci_arb'' setting of "Partial-Rotating" should be left alone, since changing it "could result in degraded system performance", and ''pci_read_mult'', which the firmware reference guide documents, "is no longer implemented in the SRM console as of Version 4.4". To run the SRM ''test'' command after shutting an operating system down, the machine has to be quiesced first with ''set auto_action halt'' and ''init''. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, pp. 3-4)]

==== Linux ====

  * "Interrupt delivery/management is a bit different on 2100A (LYNX) vs 2100 (SABLE)". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-September.mbox#L39379|"Re: AlphaServer 2100A compatability issue"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 21 Sep 1999)] In 2001, "2100A (aka LYNX) differs from 2100 (SABLE) in interrupt delivery details; current Linux kernels do not support it, though one of the *BSDs does now." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L1298|"Re: Debian support of AlphaServer 2100a."]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 11 Jun 2001)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-October.mbox#L7086|"Re: Problems Installing 7.1 on AlphaServer 2100A 5/300"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 10 Oct 2001)]
  * HP's Red Hat 7.2 kernels added support in 2003: "We did slip in bits for the Lynx (AS2100A). %%:-)%%" [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-April.mbox#L2551|"Fwd: Re: workable alpha kernels"]], George France, axp-list, 2 Apr 2003)] Installing Red Hat 7.2 needed HP's wrapper CD. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-July.mbox#L2911|"Installation HPWrapper ISO for ES47, ES80, 2100A and DAC960 systems for RH-7.2."]], George France, axp-list, 11 Jul 2003)]
  * Alpha Core 0.9 installed on an AlphaServer 2100A, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2004-October.mbox#L2929|"AC-0.9 Testing Results (was: Re: Alpha Core ISO Images)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Oct 2004)] but Alpha Core 1.0 did not: "Something is definitely broken WRT the SCSI drivers". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-May.mbox#L3648|"AS2100 and AlphaCore?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 May 2005)] By 2007, "SABLE/LYNX (AS2100/AS2100A) support is currently broken". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2007-March.mbox#L1087|"Torrents galore...but has anyone actually tried it?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 7 Mar 2007)]
