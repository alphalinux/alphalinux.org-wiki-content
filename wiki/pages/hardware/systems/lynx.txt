The AlphaServer 2100A is the successor to the [[hardware:systems:sable|AlphaServer 2100]], with the standard I/O functions moved onto the system backplane and most of the EISA slots traded for PCI slots. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, pp. 5-6)] The AlphaServer 2000A (Demi-Lynx) is the smaller version, related to the 2100A as the [[hardware:systems:sable|AlphaServer 2000]] is to the 2100, holding two CPUs and two memory boards instead of up to four CPUs. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-September.mbox#L2367|"Powerup Problem on AS2000"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 26 Sep 2005)] Digital announced the 2100A LP, a low profile midrange model, with a 5/375 processor option on 7 November 1996. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-November.mbox#L502|"Press/Digital Reduces Prices, Adds Power to Midrange AlphaServer Systems"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 8 Nov 1996)]

==== Specifications ====

  * Up to four CPUs, up to 2 GiB of memory (1 GiB with four CPUs), eight PCI slots, and three EISA slots, in the 4/275, 5/250, and 5/300 models [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Owner's Guide}}, pp. 1-2, 1-3)]
  * 275 MHz [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21064A|21064A]] with 1 MiB of backup cache on the 4/275 models, and a [[wp>Alpha_21164|21164]] at 291 MHz with 4 MiB of backup cache on the 5/300 models [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, p. 3)]
  * The same 128-bit system bus as the AlphaServer 2100, with 666 MB/s peak bandwidth, and memory modules of 64, 128, and 512 MiB [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, pp. 4-5)]
  * 33 MHz PCI and 8.33 MHz EISA, with the standard I/O functions (1 MB flash ROM, 8 KB NVRAM, TOY clock, serial, parallel, floppy, keyboard and mouse) on the system backplane rather than a separate I/O module [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, pp. 5-6)]
  * Ethernet is an adapter rather than an onboard controller, unlike the AlphaServer 2100 [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, p. 5)]
  * A low profile rackmount version, the AlphaServer 2100A LP, was also sold [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_lp_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A LP Installation/Owner's/Service Guide}})]
  * The eight PCI slots are split by an onboard DECchip 21050 PCI-to-PCI bridge: physical slots 4 to 7, the bottom four, are in front of the bridge on the primary PCI bus, and physical slots 0 to 3, the top four, are behind it on the secondary bus [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, EK-2100A-CL.C02, June 1996, p. 1)]
  * Although the system has three EISA slots, it reuses the AlphaServer 2100's EISA Configuration Utility file, so the ECU shows eight EISA slots; slots 4 to 7 in the ECU, and a power-up ''ECU error, slot 0, found DEC2A01, expected nothing'' message, should be ignored [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, pp. 3-4)]
  * Rackmount systems take a second power supply for redundancy. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, p. 4)]

==== Option restrictions ====

The KZPSM and KZPDA adapters need SRM 4.4 or later to work behind the PCI bridge. At most two KFESA or KFESB EISA DSSI controllers can be fitted, and the DE422-SA, DE425-AA, DJ-ML200, PB2HA-SA, PB2SX-AA, and PBXGA adapters are not supported at all. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2100a_hardware_release_notes.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2100A Hardware Release Notes}}, pp. 1-3)]

Digital did field upgrades of 2100s, with their three PCI slots, to the 2100A. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1997-April.mbox#L20733|"SUMM: 2100 -> 2100A upgrade: What is involved"]], Guy Dallaire, alpha-osf-managers, 11 Apr 1997)] See also the notes on memory and CPU upgrades on the [[hardware:systems:sable#upgrades_and_operating_systems|Sable page]].

==== Linux ====

The 2100A differs from the 2100 in interrupt delivery, and early kernels did not support it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-September.mbox#L39379|"Re: AlphaServer 2100A compatability issue"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 21 Sep 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L1298|"Re: Debian support of AlphaServer 2100a."]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 11 Jun 2001)] Support was added in HP's Red Hat 7.2 kernels in 2003. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-April.mbox#L2551|"Fwd: Re: workable alpha kernels"]], George France, axp-list, 2 Apr 2003)] The Lynx platform (''CONFIG_ALPHA_LYNX'', machine vector ''lynx_mv'') was removed in Linux 6.10, so 6.9 was the last kernel to support these systems. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_sable.c?h=v6.9|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_sable.c]], Linux 6.9)]
