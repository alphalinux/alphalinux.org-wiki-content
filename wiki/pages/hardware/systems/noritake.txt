===== AlphaServer 1000A, AlphaServer 800, and AlphaStation 600A (Noritake) =====

"AS800 is known internally as CORELLE and comes only as 5/xxx. NORTIAKE is AS1000A, and can be 4/xxx or 5/xxx (PRIMO). Also part of the NORITAKE family is AS600A, known as ALCOR-PRIMO which is also 5/xxx only. All have EISA bus". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-January.mbox#L14347|"Re: Alpha Server 1000A - Noritake Image (Kernel)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Jan 1999)] The Digital Server 3300 and 3305 are AlphaServer 800 models (see [[hardware:systems#naming|Naming]]). [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L1874|"Re: 3305 and RH6 boot problems"]], Rich Payne, axp-list, 2 Dec 1999)]

The AlphaServer 1000A is a revision of the [[hardware:systems:mikasa|AlphaServer 1000]] with most of the EISA slots traded for PCI slots behind a PCI-to-PCI bridge. It was sold as pedestal, rackmount, and cabinet systems. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000a:alphaserver_1000a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000A Technical Summary}}, p. 1)]

==== Specifications ====

=== AlphaServer 1000A ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000a:alphaserver_1000a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000A Technical Summary}}, pp. 2-3)]

  * One CPU on an upgradable daughtercard: 266 MHz [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21064A|21064A]] (Model 4/266), or 333 or 400 MHz [[wp>Alpha_21164#Alpha_21164A|21164A]] (Models 5/333 and 5/400)
  * 2 MiB of direct-mapped, ECC protected level 2 cache and 8 KiB of serial ROM on each CPU card
  * 128-bit ECC protected memory bus
  * Model 4/266: 20 SIMM sockets in banks of five (four data plus ECC); Models 5/333 and 5/400: 16 SIMM sockets in banks of four including ECC
  * 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 MB SIMMs, 32 MB to 1 GB total
  * Seven 33 MHz PCI slots, of which slots 11, 12, and 13 are on the primary bus and slots 1 to 4 behind the 21050 PCI-to-PCI bridge; some options are restricted to the primary bus [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000a:alphaserver_1000a_service_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000A Service Guide}}, p. 5-34)]
  * Two 8.33 MHz EISA/ISA slots, both bus master capable
  * Onboard fast wide SCSI-2 controller, PCI-to-EISA bridge, 1 MiB of flash ROM, 8 KB of NVRAM, TOY clock, two serial ports, bidirectional parallel port, floppy controller, keyboard and mouse interface, PC-style speaker
  * Onboard SVGA controller on the Model 4/266 only
  * Dual SCSI backplane, up to 30.1 GB of internal storage in the pedestal, optional RAID controllers and redundant power supplies [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_1000a:alphaserver_1000a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 1000A Technical Summary}}, p. 1)]

=== AlphaServer 800 ===

Except where noted, the following comes from the technical summary. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_800:alphaserver_800_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 800 Technical Summary}}, pp. 1-3)]

  * One [[wp>Alpha_21164|21164]] at 333 or 400 MHz on a CPU and cache daughtercard, with 2 MiB of third-level cache and 8 KiB of serial ROM; 500 MHz models were also sold [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_800:alphaserver_800_brochure.pdf?linkonly|DIGITAL AlphaServer 800 system}}, 1997)]
  * 256-bit wide ECC protected memory subsystem and a 64-bit PCI bus
  * Memory banks on the motherboard, expandable from 32 MiB to 1 GiB
  * "Three 32-bit PCI slots, one shared 64-bit PCI/EISA slot, and two dedicated EISA slots" [(>{{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_800_users_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 800 User's Guide}}, p. 1-5)]; the PCI bus runs at 33 MHz and the EISA bus at 8.33 MHz, and the EISA bus also carries the Xbus with the real-time clock, NVRAM, and operator control panel interface
  * Onboard SVGA controller with 1 MiB of video RAM, an S3 Trio64 [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_800:alphaserver_800_service_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 800 Service Guide}}, p. 6-18)]
  * Onboard fast wide single-ended SCSI controller, a QLogic part [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_800:alphaserver_800_service_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 800 Service Guide}}, p. 6-18)], with an UltraSCSI-ready storage backplane
  * Remote management console (RMC) for monitoring the system remotely, plus internal temperature, fan, and status sensors with variable speed fans
  * Bidirectional parallel port, two serial ports, keyboard and mouse interface
  * Up to four 3.5-inch full-height SCSI devices with hot swap, up to three 5.25-inch half-height devices, and a 3.5-inch floppy drive
  * Sold as a pedestal or as a rackmount box taking up to four internal drives

=== AlphaStation 600A ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600a:alphastation_600a_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600A User Information}}, January 1997, pp. 3-8 to 3-9)]

  * CPU on a module: the 600A 5/500 reports a 500 MHz [[wp>Alpha_21164#Alpha_21164A|21164A]] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600a:alphastation_600a_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600A User Information}}, p. 6-3)]
  * 16 SIMM sockets in four banks of four; memory options of 16, 32, 64, 128, and 256 MiB, for 16 MiB to 1 GiB, with one bank required and all modules in a bank of the same capacity
  * Primary and secondary PCI slots plus EISA slots, an onboard SCSI controller, and an onboard graphics controller that the EISA Configuration Utility can disable
  * Both SRM and AlphaBIOS, with an ''os_type'' environment variable selecting which the NVRAM boots, and a ''pci_parity'' variable whose default depends on the SCSI controller revision [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600a:alphastation_600a_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600A User Information}}, p. 5-53)]

Digital sold an upgrade from the [[hardware:systems:alcor|AlphaStation 600]], which shows how little the two share: the kit replaces the main logic board and the CPU module, and the Alcor memory towers are returned to Digital. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600a:alphastation_600_to_600a_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 to AlphaStation 600A Upgrade Information}}, January 1997, pp. 1-1 to 1-4)]

==== Booting Linux ====

  * "Please, use SRM, as there's no MILO for any of that family." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-March.mbox#L29808|"Re: AlphaServer 800"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Mar 1998)] "Use of MILO is deprecated on MIKASA, and unavailable on NORITAKE." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-March.mbox#L30004|"Re: AlphaServer 800"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Mar 1998)]
  * The AlphaServer 1000 and 1000A "have quite different interrupt delivery mechanisms, and the built-in PCI-PCI bridge on NORITAKE requires some special code to deal with it", so their kernels are not interchangeable. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-November.mbox#L36383|"Re: Kernel for AlphaServer 1000 (Success!... and a question)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 Nov 1997)]

==== Linux ====

  * "I don't believe the NORITAKEs have IDE ports". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L7551|"Re: Debian on Digital 3305"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 12 Feb 2003)]
  * Graphics cards can sit behind the onboard PCI-to-PCI bridge on this family: "On most boxes this isn't a problem - there are no slots behind a bridge to put a VGA into, but on UX, MIATA, and NORITAKE family, there are." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-August.mbox#L26968|"Re: PC164UX doesnt work - anyone got details"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 11 Aug 1998)]

==== Conflicting information ====

The following sources conflict with each other or with the account given above. They are listed here for further investigation.

  * **AlphaServer 800 CPU speeds and maximum memory**
    * {{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_800:alphaserver_800_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 800 Technical Summary}}, 1997, pp. 1, 3: two CPU cards, 333 and 400 MHz, and "incremental memory expansion from 32 MB to 1 GB"
    * {{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_800:alphaserver_800_brochure.pdf?linkonly|DIGITAL AlphaServer 800 system}}, 1997: 21164 at 333, 400, and 500 MHz, each with 2 GiB of memory
    * {{wiki:software:firmware:alphaserver_800_users_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 800 User's Guide}}, November 1997, pp. 1-3, 1-4: a 21164 at 300 to 500 MHz and memory "from 32 Mbytes to 2 Gbytes"
