===== AlphaStation 200, 250, 255, 400 and AlphaServer 300, 400 (Avanti) =====

The Avanti family is Digital's first generation of 21064 desktop workstations and small servers with PCI. Codenames: AlphaStation 200 Mustang, 205 LX3, 250 M3, 255 LX3+, 400 Avanti, and AlphaServer 300 Melmac. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-March.mbox#L30004|"Re: AlphaServer 800"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Mar 1998)] The AlphaStation 200 is "identical to AVANTIs, as MILO and the kernel see them, although the MB is not daughtercard based". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-April.mbox#L9214|"Re: AlphaStation info"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 18 Apr 1996)] These systems have both SRM and ARC.

The [[hardware:systems:xl|Alpha XL 233 and 266]] are close relatives, and some early XL machines are in fact Avantis. "Note that a true XL has a *different* motherboard than AVANTIs, and consequently the AVANTI MILOs and kernels will *not* work correctly. You can tell a true XL by the SIO (PCI<->ISA bridge chip); its a 82379AB and *not* the AVANTIs 82378IB/ZB." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L6843|"Alpha XL Support"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 22 Mar 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-July.mbox#L2473|"RE: SRM/bootp questions"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 Jul 1996)]

==== Announcement ====

Digital announced the AlphaStation 200 4/166 and 4/233 and the AlphaStation 400 4/233 on 3 November 1994, as the PCI/ISA systems that would replace its low and mid-range TURBOchannel DEC 3000 workstations. The 200 is a slimline desktop with three slots and four bays; the 400 is a mini tower with six slots and five bays. Digital rated the 4/166 at 107.7 SPECint92 and 134.8 SPECfp92, and both 4/233 models at 157.6 SPECint92 and 183.8 SPECfp92, against 81 and 110 for the EISA-based DECpc AXP 150 ([[hardware:systems:jensen|Jensen]]). All three shipped with a choice of DEC OSF/1 V3.0, OpenVMS AXP V6.1-1H1, or Windows NT Workstation V3.5, and Digital advertised an entry-level Windows NT configuration under $7,000. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L5308|"AlphaStation 200 Family of 64-bit Desktop Workstations"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Nov 1994)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L5552|"AlphaStation 400 4/233 -- 64-bit Computing in PCI/ISA Based Workstation"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Nov 1994)] The two differ in how they are upgraded, which matches how Linux sees them: the AlphaStation 200 was to be upgraded by "a user-installable chip replacement", the AlphaStation 400 "via future CPU daughter card replacement". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L5308|"AlphaStation 200 Family of 64-bit Desktop Workstations"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Nov 1994)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L5552|"AlphaStation 400 4/233 -- 64-bit Computing in PCI/ISA Based Workstation"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Nov 1994)]

Digital also sold the AlphaStation 400's boards to OEMs on their own, as the AXPpci 233XL module set, which the design guide says "is the same as that used in Digital's AlphaStation 400 Model 4/233 system". The guide gives the set as a 233 MHz 21064A with 16 KB instruction and 16 KB write-through data caches on chip and a 512 KB 12 ns 128-bit write-back parity-protected backup cache, a 64-bit memory bus with two parity bits taking 1Mx36 to 8Mx36 70 ns SIMMs from 16 MB to 192 MB, 2 MB of flash ROM with a 64 KB diagnostic ROM and an 8 KB serial ROM, an NCR 53C810 Fast SCSI-2 controller with internal and external connectors, two PCI slots, three ISA slots, one shared PCI/ISA slot, and no integral graphics. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_400:axppci_233xl_module_set_oem_design_guide.pdf?linkonly|Digital AXPpci 233XL Module Set OEM Design Guide}}, EK-AXPXL-DG.A01, January 1995, pp. 1-2)]

The family grew downward and sideways on 2 March 1995 with the AlphaStation 200 4/100, an entry-level desktop aimed at the $5,000 to $6,000 range, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-March.mbox#L902|"AlphaStation 200 4/100 Entry Level 64-bit Desktop Workstation"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 2 Mar 1995)] and the AlphaServer 400 4/166, a 166 MHz uniprocessor server in the mini tower enclosure, taking up to 192 MB of SIMM memory with an integrated fast SCSI-2 controller and remote management over a serial link. Digital pitched it at workgroups migrating from the MicroVAX 3100. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-March.mbox#L1177|"AlphaServer 400 4/166 Mini Tower Server"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 2 Mar 1995)]

The press releases for that day put numbers to both machines. The AlphaStation 200 4/100 was rated at 74.59 SPECint92 and 95.23 SPECfp92, with 512 KB of board-level cache and up to 192 MB of memory, and started at $4,285 with Windows NT, 16 MB of memory, and a 340 MB disk. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-February.mbox#L1524|"Press/New Entry-Level Alpha Workstation Is Price/Performance Leader"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 24 Feb 1995)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-February.mbox#L1660|"Fact Sheet/AlphaStation 200 4/100 Workstation"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 24 Feb 1995)] The AlphaServer 400 4/166 started "under $10,000", at $9,315 with Windows NT Server or $9,980 with OpenVMS or DEC OSF/1, in both cases with 32 MB of memory, a 1.05 GB drive, a CD-ROM, and Ethernet; it was rated at 107.7 SPECint92, 134.8 SPECfp92, and an estimated 100 transactions per second, with 512 KB of secondary cache, two PCI, three ISA, and one combined PCI/ISA slot, and 8.4 GB of internal storage. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-February.mbox#L1784|"Press/AlphaServer Offers Large System Features At Small System Prices"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 24 Feb 1995)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-February.mbox#L1918|"Fact Sheet/AlphaServer 400 4/166 System"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 24 Feb 1995)]

Digital announced the AlphaStation 250 4/266 on 3 April 1995 as "the world's fastest workstation", at $19,995 for a fully configured system with Digital UNIX or OpenVMS, 32 MB of memory, a 1.05 GB disk, a CD-ROM, ZLXp-E1 graphics, and a 21-inch color monitor. It was rated at 198.6 SPECint92, 262.5 SPECfp92, and 17.03 Xmark95, with 2 MB of onboard cache, a 128-bit memory bus, three I/O slots, and support for 256 MB of memory, "512 MBytes in the future". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L1|"Press/Digital Announces World's Fastest Workstation"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 17 Apr 1995)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L145|"Fact Sheet/AlphaStation 250 4/266 Workstation"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 17 Apr 1995)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L3721|"AlphaStation 250 4/266 Midrange Performance Workstation"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 17 Apr 1995)]

The AlphaStation 255/233 and 255/300 followed on 8 March 1996, starting at $7,399, with 1 MB of secondary cache, a 128-bit memory bus, two PCI, one PCI/ISA, and one ISA slot, and up to 6 GB of internal storage in four drive bays. They were rated at 3.8 and 4.51 SPECint95 and 5.09 and 5.71 SPECfp95, and could be upgraded to an [[hardware:systems:alcor|AlphaStation 500]] within the same enclosure. Digital announced the PowerStorm PCI graphics family in the same release. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-March.mbox#L150|"Press/AlphaStation 255 and 500 Workstations Lead In Price/Performance"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 11 Mar 1996)]

==== Specifications ====

=== AlphaServer 300 ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_300:alphaserver_300_users_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 300 User's Guide}}, pp. 1-1, A-2)]

  * DECchip [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21064A|21064A]] at 266 MHz
  * DECchip 21072-AA (APECS) core logic
  * 2 MB write-back secondary cache
  * Memory controller with a 128-bit data bus to main memory and a 128-bit bus to the secondary cache, longword parity protected
  * Expandable to 512 MB of memory, added as options of four SIMMs each in 32, 64, 128, or 256 MB sizes [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_300:alphaserver_300_users_guide.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 300 User's Guide}}, p. 4-7)]
  * 33 MHz PCI: one PCI-only slot, one shared PCI/ISA slot, and one half-size ISA-only slot
  * PCI-based embedded SCSI-2 controller supporting seven peripherals, and a PCI-based Ethernet adapter
  * Floppy disk controller supporting a floppy drive and one CD drive
  * Keyboard and mouse ports, two serial ports, one enhanced bidirectional parallel port

=== AlphaStation 250 ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_250:alphastation_250_user_information.pdf?linkonly|Digital AlphaStation 250 Series User Information}}, pp. 2-1, C-2)]

  * DECchip [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21064A|21064A]], with a jumper pair (J7 and J32) setting the CPU clock rate
  * DECchip 21072-AA core logic
  * 2 MB write-back secondary cache
  * 128-bit data bus to main memory and a 128-bit bus to the secondary cache, longword parity protected
  * Memory expandable to 256 MB, 33 MHz PCI
  * One PCI-only slot, one PCI/ISA slot, and one half-size ISA-only slot, on a riser card
  * PCI-based embedded SCSI-2 controller for up to seven devices, a PCI Ethernet adapter, and a sound card
  * Floppy disk controller supporting a floppy drive and one further drive, keyboard and mouse ports, two serial ports, one enhanced bidirectional parallel port
  * A jumper (J27) controls the automatic SCSI terminator, which turns off when an external SCSI cable is attached; with sensing disabled an external terminator is always required [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_250:alphastation_250_user_information.pdf?linkonly|Digital AlphaStation 250 Series User Information}}, p. C-8)]
  * IRQ 11 is the SCSI controller, IRQ 10 the built-in PCI Ethernet, and IRQ 9 and 15 the two riser PCI slots [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_250:alphastation_250_user_information.pdf?linkonly|Digital AlphaStation 250 Series User Information}}, p. D-2)]

=== AlphaStation 255 ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_255:alphastation_255_user_information.pdf?linkonly|Digital AlphaStation 255 Family User Information}}, pp. 2-9, B-2)]

  * Socketed DECchip [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21064A|21064A]] with DECchip 21071-AA core logic
  * 1 MB write-back secondary cache
  * Two PCI, one PCI/ISA combination, and one ISA-only slot on a riser card, PCI at 33.33 MHz
  * Two banks of four SIMMs, expandable to 1 GB "when 128-MB SIMMs are available"
  * Twisted-pair Ethernet on the motherboard, with an optional media adapter unit for ThinWire, and onboard audio jacks
  * A switchpack replaces the AlphaStation 250's SROM and boot jumpers: switches 1 and 2 select the SROM mini-console, booting the console image from floppy, or clearing the RTC and NVRAM [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_255:alphastation_255_user_information.pdf?linkonly|Digital AlphaStation 255 Family User Information}}, p. B-11)]

=== AlphaStation 400 ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_400:alphastation_400_user_information.pdf?linkonly|Digital AlphaStation 400 Series User Information}}, pp. 2-1, C-1)]

  * CPU on a daughtercard: the PB50C-BA module carries a 233 MHz DECchip 21064A (EV4S) with 16 KB instruction and 16 KB data caches, DECchip 21071-AA core logic, 512 KB of direct-mapped writeback secondary cache, 1 MB of flashROM holding the ARC and SRM consoles, PALcode, and diagnostics, a 64 KB diagnostic ROM, 8 KB of NVRAM, and an 8-bit diagnostic LED display [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_400:alphastation_400_cpu_module_information.pdf?linkonly|Digital AlphaStation 400 Series CPU Module Information}}, p. 1-1)]
  * Six SIMM sockets on the motherboard, expandable to 192 MB
  * 32-bit PCI at 33 MHz: two PCI slots, three ISA slots, and one combination PCI/ISA slot
  * SCSI-2 controller with internal and external buses for up to seven devices
  * Floppy disk controller supporting two floppy drives or one floppy and one tape drive, keyboard and mouse ports, two serial ports, one enhanced bidirectional parallel port
  * Three 5.25-inch half-height bays, one hidden 3.5-inch half-height bay, and one 3.5-inch diskette drive, on a 300 W power supply

==== Trade-In Upgrades ====

Digital sold the family partly as an upgrade path off its older workstations, shipping a new system unit and a manual describing how to move the drives and options across and return the old machine. The AlphaStation 250 4/266 kit covered the DECstation 2100, 3100, and 5000, the VAXstation 4000, and the DEC 3000 Models 300, 300L, 300X, and 300LX. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_250:alphastation_250_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 250 Model 4/266 Upgrade Information}}, May 1995, p. 1-1)] The AlphaStation 400 kit covered the DECpc AXP 150 ([[hardware:systems:jensen|Jensen]]) and the DEC 3000 Models 300, 400, 500, and 600, and carried over monitors and BA350 and BA353 expansion boxes as well. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_400:alphastation_400_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|Digital AlphaStation 400 Series Upgrade Information}}, pp. 1-1 to 1-2)]

==== Firmware ====

A February 1996 letter to AlphaStation 255 owners warned that clearing the real-time clock and NVRAM with the configuration switches leaves firmware V1.3 with ''console = serial'', so that power-up messages go to COM1 and the graphics head shows only a blinking cursor, and with ''bus_probe_algorithm = old'', which Digital UNIX V3.2c and later require to be set to ''new''. Both defaults were to change in the following firmware release. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_255:alphastation_255_customer_letter.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 255 customer letter}}, EK-VLLXA-FL.A01, 8 February 1996)]

==== Booting Linux ====

  * MILO must not be written to flash: "You MUST NOT, as the AVANTI family is not supported for doing this." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-July.mbox#L59698|"Re: Questions."]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 23 Jul 1998)] Richard Henderson: "Booting and rebooting from SRM should be clean. That is how I boot my Avanti." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-December.mbox#L16159|"Re: Kernel 2.1.131 compiles but hangs..."]], Richard Henderson, axp-list, 7 Dec 1998)]
  * Early MILO images for the family came from gatekeeper.dec.com, in TGA and VGA variants, and were loaded from the ARC console with ''linload.exe''. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L5863|"RE: booting Linux from ARC"]], David Rusling, axp-list, 19 Mar 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-March.mbox#L5932|"Re: booting Linux from ARC"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 19 Mar 1996)]

==== Linux ====

  * The built-in 21040 and 21041 Ethernet controllers on the AlphaStation 200, 250, 255, and AlphaServer 300 "give problems with the current default driver ("de2104x")". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2006-January.mbox#L4129|""no link beat found" on digital alpha-station 255"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 17 Jan 2006)]
  * The S3 Trio64 "is notorious, especially in AS200/AS400 boxes" (see [[hardware:other#pc_graphics_cards|Other Hardware]]).
  * On an AlphaStation 200 4/166, a Microsoft Sound System card at its default IRQ 11 conflicted with the SCSI controller. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-August.mbox#L9692|"Re: MSS sound and Avanti (was: MSS sound and UDB)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Aug 1996)]
