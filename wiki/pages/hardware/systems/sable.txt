===== AlphaServer 2000 and 2100 (Sable) =====

The AlphaServer 2100 is a symmetric multiprocessing PCI/EISA server supporting up to four CPUs, and the AlphaServer 2000 (Demi-Sable) is a two CPU version of it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-September.mbox#L2324|"Powerup Problem on AS2000"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 26 Sep 2005)] Both use the T2 core logic, and were sold as 4/275, 5/250, and 5/300 models, the last running at 291 MHz with a 4 MiB secondary cache. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, p. 3)] Earlier 4/200 and 4/233 models were also sold. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L15109|"Re: 2.4.20 kernel panics on boot w/ AS2100"]], Soren Harward, debian-alpha, 27 Feb 2003)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2002-April.mbox#L22932|"Re: I take a deep breath, and I suck down my pride..."]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 30 Apr 2002)] Systems equipped with EV5 CPUs are called Gamma. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-May.mbox#L678|"Re: Help with aboot / kernel startup"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 1 May 2000)] The successors are the [[hardware:systems:lynx|AlphaServer 2100A and 2000A]].

Digital introduced the machine in April 1994 as the **Digital 2100 Server A500MP** and renamed it the AlphaServer 2100 4/200 in November 1994. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L485|"AlphaServer 2100 4/200 and 4/275"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Nov 1994)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L308|"AlphaServer 2000 4/200"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Nov 1994)]

The systems were sold as pedestal, rackmount, and cabinet systems; a single cabinet could hold a three node, twelve CPU cluster. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, pp. 1-2)]

The AlphaServer 2000 5/250 arrived in August 1995, bringing the 250 MHz 21164 with a 4 MiB third level cache to the two processor system, and Digital offered 4/200, 4/233, and 4/275 owners a processor board swap, keeping the existing memory. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L1|"AlphaServer 2000 5/250 System"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 2 Aug 1995)] The EV5 module is the KN470, designed to be swap-compatible with the first-generation KN460 while adding a synchronous third level cache and a write-invalidate coherence protocol on the system bus. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L9056|"The Second-generation Processor Module for AlphaServer 2100 Systems"]], Nitin D. Godiwala and Barry A. Maskas, Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Aug 1995)] 5/375 models of the AlphaServer 2000 followed on 7 November 1996. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-November.mbox#L502|"Press/Digital Reduces Prices, Adds Power to Midrange AlphaServer Systems"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 8 Nov 1996)]

Two //Digital Technical Journal// papers describe the architecture. The system bus carries four processing nodes, four memory nodes holding up to 2 GiB, and two I/O nodes, and was designed to support several generations of Alpha processors. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-February.mbox#L637|"Design of the AlphaServer Multiprocessor Server Systems"]], Fidelma M. Hayes, Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Feb 1995)] The I/O side is a two-level hierarchy: a 32-bit PCI bus joined to the system bus by a custom bridge chip, with an EISA bus below it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-February.mbox#L1649|"The AlphaServer 2100 I/O Subsystem"]], Andrew P. Russo, Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Feb 1995)]

==== Specifications ====

Except where noted, the following comes from the technical summary. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000, AlphaServer 2100, and AlphaServer 2100A Technical Summary}}, pp. 3-6)]

  * Up to four CPU modules on the AlphaServer 2100 and two on the AlphaServer 2000, in a shared set of system bus slots with the memory modules; slot CPU0 must be filled
  * 275 MHz [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21064A|21064A]] with 1 MiB of backup cache on the 4/275 models, [[wp>Alpha_21164|21164]] at 250 or 291 MHz on the 5/250 and 5/300 models (KN470 and KN480 modules), and [[wp>Alpha_21164#Alpha_21164A|21164A]] at 375 MHz on the 5/375 models (KN490 module) [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2x00_2100a_cpu_upgrade_rev_c.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2x00/2100A Series CPU Upgrade}}, "KN470, KN480, and KN490 Module Technical Specifications")]; CPU modules carry 8 KiB of serial ROM, a power-up diagnostic microprocessor, the system bus arbiter, and 1 or 4 MiB of direct-mapped backup cache
  * 128-bit multiplexed address and data system bus with central arbitration, longword parity, 32-byte transfers, and 666 MB/s peak bandwidth
  * AlphaServer 2100: up to four memory modules, 2 GiB total, in 64, 128, and 512 MiB modules, interleaved two or four ways
  * AlphaServer 2000: up to two memory modules, 1 GiB total, built from 4 MiB (1M x 36) or 16 MiB (4M x 36) 70 ns 72-pin SIMMs, four banks per module, interleaved two ways
  * 256-bit interface to the DRAMs, ECC and address parity, read stream and write transaction buffers
  * Three 33 MHz 32-bit PCI slots on both systems
  * Eight 8.33 MHz EISA slots on the AlphaServer 2100 and seven on the AlphaServer 2000, all bus master capable
  * PCI Ethernet controller with AUI or twisted-pair, onboard on the AlphaServer 2100 and an adapter on the AlphaServer 2000
  * PCI single-ended 8-bit Fast SCSI-2 controller; wide SCSI is supported through option controllers [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_2100a_wide_scsi_technical_bulletin.pdf?linkonly|Application of Wide SCSI on AlphaServer 2000/2100/2100A}}, Version 1.5, December 1995)]
  * CPU types cannot be mixed: KN450 and KN460 CPU modules cannot be used in the same system [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_2100:alphaserver_2000_2100_kn450_kn460_cpu_module_installation.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 2000/2100 Series KN450/KN460 CPU Module Installation and Configuration}}, EK-EV450-IN.B01, October 1994, pp. 1-2)]
  * 1 MiB of flash ROM, 8 KB of NVRAM, TOY clock, two serial ports, bidirectional parallel port, floppy controller, PS/2 keyboard and mouse, PC-style speaker
  * On the AlphaServer 2100 this I/O is on a separate standard I/O module; on the AlphaServer 2000 it is on the I/O backplane
  * BA35E StorageWorks shelves holding up to eight 3.5-inch SCSI drives each, configurable as a single or dual bus

==== Upgrades and operating systems ====

  * CPUs of different speeds are not supported together; one 2100A ran with a 5/300 in slot 0 and a 5/250 in slot 1, the second CPU running at the speed of the first. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L13814|"Summary: CPU's of different speeds in an Alpha"]], Trevor Stott, tru64-unix-managers, 18 Mar 1999)]

==== Linux ====

The Sable boots only from the SRM console; there is no MILO for it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-March.mbox#L22902|"RE: Alpha Server 2000:Up and Running:THANKS!!"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 20 Mar 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2001-July.mbox#L2646|"Re: AS2100"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 16 Jul 2001)] Linux 6.10 removed the early EV4 Sable machines (''sable_mv'') and kept only the Gamma systems (''sable_gamma_mv''), [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/torvalds/c/d2b1e353dacc|"alpha: sable: remove early machine support"]], Linux 6.10)] and the same release dropped support for the original 21164 (EV5), so current kernels support only Gamma systems with 21164A (EV56) CPUs, that is, 5/375 systems with KN490 modules. 6.9 was the last kernel for the 4/xxx models and for 5/250 and 5/300 CPUs. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/commit/?id=a4184174be36|"alpha: drop pre-EV56 support"]], Linux 6.10)]

  * As of 2008, the AlphaServer 2100 ran uniprocessor kernels but crashed with SMP kernels. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-April.mbox#L560|"Fedora 8-based distro /UP2000+"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Apr 2008)]
  * The T2 core logic uses a host address extension (HAE) register for PCI access, which in 1999 made X unreliable on SMP Sable systems. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L52247|"axp update for 2.3.15"]], Richard Henderson, axp-list, 30 Aug 1999)]
