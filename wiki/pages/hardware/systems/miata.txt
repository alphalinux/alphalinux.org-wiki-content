===== Personal Workstation a and au series (Miata) =====

"The internal code-name for the PWS series is 'Miata'." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-June.mbox#L4503|"Re: Personal Workstation 433a"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 3 Jun 1997)] The a-series models run Windows NT and the au-series Tru64 UNIX and OpenVMS; the models are named for their clock speed, such as the 433a, 500a, 500au, and 600au. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:personal_workstation:personal_workstation_a_au_series_catalog.pdf?linkonly|Compaq DPW Enhanced a-Series & au-Series}}, Systems and Options Catalog, September 2000, p. 1)] "MIATAs were never built with EV5, only with EV56". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-April.mbox#L1885|"Re: 2.1.92 and PWS 433a (21164 CPU, not 21164A!)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Apr 1998)] The flash holds both SRM and AlphaBIOS. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2005-June.mbox#L4706|"Re: PWS AlphaBIOS to SRM"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 9 Jun 2005)]

The first two models, the Personal Workstation 433a and 500a, were announced on 8 January 1997 for Windows NT, each shipping with Windows NT Workstation 4.0 and [[software:emulators#fx32|FX!32]] factory installed, which let them run x86 applications such as Microsoft Office 95 and Adobe Photoshop. Digital credited "the DIGITAL-designed Pyxis chipset, which implements a high-bandwidth, low-latency 128-bit memory bus" for the results: 56 minutes for the 500a and 61 for the 433a on the Texas Instruments Pro/Engineer benchmark, against 79 minutes for an SGI Indigo2 R10000 Solid IMPACT and 111 for an HP B160L. The systems took 32 to 384 MB of ECC synchronous DRAM and shipped with an Ultrawide SCSI controller, a 2.1 GB disk, an 8X EIDE CD-ROM, 10BaseT and 10Base2 Ethernet, and 16-bit audio; graphics options were Digital's PowerStorm, the Matrox Millennium, and the AccelGraphics AccelPro series. The 433a started at $4,995 with 64 MiB of memory and Matrox graphics, and the 500a with 64 MiB and AccelPro 2500 graphics came in under $10,000. Digital said the same day that it had shipped its 50,000th personal workstation, to United Space Alliance. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-January.mbox#L1|"Press/New Alpha-Based Personal Workstations Boost Digital's WinNT Lead"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 9 Jan 1997)]

==== Specifications ====

The following describes the later "Enhanced" 500 and 600 MHz models. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:personal_workstation:personal_workstation_a_au_series_catalog.pdf?linkonly|Compaq DPW Enhanced a-Series & au-Series}}, Systems and Options Catalog, September 2000, p. 1)]

  * 500 or 600 MHz [[wp>Alpha_21164#Alpha_21164A|21164A]] with an optional 4 MiB cache
  * 21174 (Pyxis) core logic, the same as on the [[hardware:motherboards:pc164lx|164LX]], [[hardware:motherboards:pc164sx|164SX]], and [[hardware:motherboards:pc164ux|164UX]] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-August.mbox#L34900|"Re: PWS-500a ext cache question..."]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 Aug 1998)]
  * Three DIMM banks (six slots), 1.5 GiB maximum
  * Five I/O slots: two PCI and three PCI/ISA. "MIATA motherboard has 2 64-bit PCI slots in front of a builtin PCI-PCI bridge, which is where the remaining 3 32-bit PCI slots live." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-August.mbox#L34900|"Re: PWS-500a ext cache question..."]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 Aug 1998)]
  * Onboard QLogic Wide UltraSCSI adapter (on the Enhanced models only, see below)
  * Onboard 16-bit audio, an ES1888 that is "(at least) SBPro compatible", not supported under OpenVMS [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-July.mbox#L23336|"Re: Sound on Miata (PWS500a)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 9 Jul 1998)]
  * 10/100BaseT Ethernet, twisted pair, with ThinWire optional
  * Short tower with six storage bays: two 3.5-inch disk bays, a floppy bay, a dedicated 5.25-inch CD-ROM bay, and two further removable media bays
  * 300 W power supply

The first user manual, from October 1996, describes the same chassis with a smaller specification: five option slots, made up of one 32-bit half-size PCI slot, one 64-bit full-size PCI slot, two PCI/ISA combination slots (one 32-bit half-size, one 64-bit full-size), and one 32-bit full-size PCI/ISA slot; six drive bays; a 300 W supply; 10Base2 Ethernet with 10BaseT optional; and eight diagnostic LEDs. The main logic board carries the 21164 in a ZIF socket with a CPU voltage regulator, cache connectors, six DIMM sockets numbered 0 at the bottom to 5 at the top, an SROM diagnostic serial port "(manufacturing use)", and a CPU clock frequency selection switch bank. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:personal_workstation:personal_workstation_alpha_user_information.pdf?linkonly|Personal Workstation (Alpha) User Information}}, EK-ALMIA-UI.A01, October 1996, pp. 2-1, 2-9)]

The a-series and au-series differ mainly in firmware and SCSI adapter: "the a's have an adaptec and the au's have a Qlogic (1040a) scsi card", the KZPBA-CA. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-misc-mbox/googlegroups-1999-February.mbox#L917|"Re: PWS 433a -> 433au"]], James W. Caldwell, comp.unix.osf.misc, 24 Feb 1999)] A retail QLA1040 is cheaper, but "at least some versions of DPW firmware (5.1 for sure) are very restrictive at identifying PCI devices, and may not accept some revisions of QLA1040 as KZPBA-CA". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-misc-mbox/googlegroups-1999-February.mbox#L997|"Re: PWS 433a -> 433au"]], Serguei Patchkovskii, comp.unix.osf.misc, 25 Feb 1999)] The au-series needs Digital UNIX V4.0C or later, a release made for it alone. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1997-May.mbox#L21624|"Digital UNIX V4.0C media distribution"]], Tom Blinn, alpha-osf-managers, 8 May 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1997-July.mbox#L106|"Summary: Digital Unix 4.0B and 500/au workstations."]], Jim Lochowitz, alpha-osf-managers, 31 Jul 1997)]

==== Upgrading from the Intel models ====

Digital sold the Personal Workstation in Intel (i-series) and Alpha (a-series) forms in the same enclosure, and sold conversion kits between them. The 433a kit "describes the upgrade of your DIGITAL Personal Workstation Model 180i, 200i, or 200i(2) to a DIGITAL Personal Workstation Model 433a", and the 600a kit covers the "Model 266i or higher". Each kit is a main logic board, two SDRAM DIMMs, software diskettes, a wrist strap, and a speed button for the front bezel; the procedure is to swap the board, install cache memory, move the power connector on the riser card, change the speed button, and reinstall Windows NT. The 600a kit adds a new fan mount and fan pipe. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:personal_workstation:personal_workstation_433a_upgrade_guide.pdf?linkonly|DIGITAL Personal Workstation 433a Upgrade Guide}}, EK-ALMIA-KU.A01, April 1997, pp. 1-1 to 1-2)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:personal_workstation:personal_workstation_600a_upgrade_guide.pdf?linkonly|DIGITAL Personal Workstation 600a Upgrade Guide}}, EK-ALMIA-KU.C01, pp. 1-1 to 1-2)] The cache was a customer-installable option too: the 433 a-series cache kit is a single 2 MiB module. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:personal_workstation:personal_workstation_433a_cache_memory_installation_guide.pdf?linkonly|Personal Workstation 433 a-Series System Cache Memory Installation Guide}}, EK-ALMIA-CU.A01, March 1997, p. 1)]

==== Generations ====

"There are two flavours of the Miata: the old MX5 with the CMD646 IDE controller and a newer MiataGL with a Cypress 82C693 IDE controller." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-January.mbox#L9612|"Re: IDE disk problems with RH7.2 on DPW500a"]], Magnus Lindstrom, axp-list, 22 Jan 2003)] The Miata GL has an onboard QLogic SCSI controller, "And it was never made obvious from the model numbers". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-December.mbox#L22318|"Re: Can someone check the specs of this machine?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 11 Dec 1999)] Compaq's catalog calls the later models "Enhanced" and the earlier ones "Classic Digital Personal Workstations (DPW without onboard SCSI)"; a classic machine could be upgraded to an enhanced one with a new motherboard and riser. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:personal_workstation:personal_workstation_a_au_series_catalog.pdf?linkonly|Compaq DPW Enhanced a-Series & au-Series}}, Systems and Options Catalog, September 2000, p. 8)]

"There are two versions of MIATA motherboards: they differ in the PCI "slot" where the onboard PCI-to-PCI bridge lives". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L2291|"Re: PWS 433a"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 3 Jan 1998)]

==== DMA bug ====

The 21174 "has a bug that corrupts DMA transfers that crosses a page boundary ... on the primary PCI bus, but that is where the IDE controller is located." Use an add-on IDE controller "in one of the free 32-bit PCI-slots. Don't use a 64-bit slot." "The MiataGL doesn't have the DMA bug, it was fixed with a factory installed hardware patch." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2003-January.mbox#L9612|"Re: IDE disk problems with RH7.2 on DPW500a"]], Magnus Lindstrom, axp-list, 22 Jan 2003)] "Early MIATA had the most problems, LX and SX many fewer, and final MIATA (aka MIATA-GL) none to speak of". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2004-April.mbox#L2335|"Second IDE port not working on PC164"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Apr 2004)] See also [[hardware:other#personal_workstation_64-bit_slots|Personal Workstation 64-bit Slots]].

On a first generation PWS 433a, the ''cmd64x'' driver ignores ''ide=nodma'', so DMA has to be disabled with ''hdparm''. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2007-August.mbox#L7000|"Re: Hmm, IDE DMA seems broken with Etch on my PWS433a (Miata MX5)."]], Lennart Sorensen, debian-alpha, 31 Aug 2007)]

==== Linux ====

  * Kernels before 2.2.18 limited Pyxis systems to 1 GB: "a problem a while ago, with PYXIS support (MIATA/LX/SX) being limited to 1GB of RAM ... This was fixed". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-March.mbox#L38336|"Re: PYXIS machine checks w/ 1.5GB RAM"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 Mar 2001)]
  * The XP1000, DS20, and Miata GL share a Cypress IDE quirk, fixed in Jay Estabrook's 2008 kernels: "The problem on CYPRESS IDE machines (XP1000, DS20, MIATA) is fixed." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-January.mbox#L289|"My lucky day"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 9 Jan 2008)]

  * A 2004 installation report found the Miata's IDE CD-ROM at SRM device ''dka0'' rather than the ''dqa0'' the documentation expected, and warned against the QLogic ISP1020 driver of early 2.6 kernels, which users blamed for file system corruption. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/gentoo-alpha-mbox/2004-August.mbox#L1289|"[gentoo-alpha] Woo-hoo!  (or "Successful Miata Installation Notes")"]], Rich J., gentoo-alpha, 25 Aug 2004)]

==== Kryotech ====

With Kryotech, Compaq sold "a 767Mhz EV56 chilled with the Kryotech "refrigerator" slapped onto one side of the normal Miata GL case". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2001-June.mbox#L2778|"RE: Chilled Miata ... anyone?"]], Jeff Donsbach, axp-list, 6 Jun 2001)]
