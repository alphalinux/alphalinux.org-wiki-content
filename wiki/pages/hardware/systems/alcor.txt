===== AlphaStation 500 and 600 and Alpha XLT =====

The AlphaStation 600 (Alcor), AlphaStation 500 (Maverick and Bret), and Alpha XL 300, 366, and 433 (XLT) share the CIA core logic, EISA, and one kernel platform. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-March.mbox#L30004|"Re: AlphaServer 800"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Mar 1998)] [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/Kconfig?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/Kconfig]], Linux 6.10)] "XLT stands for XL-Turbo, not XL-True". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-December.mbox#L30289|"Re: Can't load RAMDISK on XL-266"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 23 Dec 1997)]

==== Announcement ====

The AlphaStation 600 family, announced in July 1995, comprised "the first Digital workstations to use the Alpha 21164 chip". The mid-tower enclosure held up to 1 GB of ECC memory, six internal storage slots for up to 17.2 GB of SCSI disk, and eight PCI and EISA option slots, with a 256-bit memory bus and "the first-in-the-industry 64-bit workstation PCI I/O bus that runs at 264 MB per second at peak". The 5/266 came with 2 MB or 4 MiB of board-level cache and the 5/300 with 4 MiB, rated at 288 SPECint92 and 428 SPECfp92 and at 334 and 503 respectively. Digital UNIX and OpenVMS were supported at launch and Windows NT from the fourth quarter of 1995, which is also when the 5/266 to 5/300 upgrade shipped, as a field service board swap that brought the 300 MHz CPU and the 4 MiB cache together under one part number. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-July.mbox#L3391|"AlphaStation 600 Family of High Performance Workstations"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 19 Jul 1995)] Rackmount versions of both models followed in August 1995, with the same functionality and performance as the deskside system, sold as system building blocks for factory or on-site integration. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L2277|"AlphaStation 600 5/266 and 5/300 Rackmount Workstations"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 15 Aug 1995)]

The AlphaStation 600 5/333 followed on 29 January 1996 with the 333 MHz 21164, at 9.19 SPECint95 and 13.2 SPECfp95; Digital called it "the world's first workstation to break the 400 SPECint92 barrier" at 412.4 SPECint92 and 545.2 SPECfp92, for $84.9 per SPECint92. The ZLXp-L1 and ZLXp-L2 midrange 3D graphics boards for Windows NT were announced in the same release. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L1951|"Press/New High-End AlphaStation Raises Ante In Workstation Market"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 30 Jan 1996)]

The AlphaStation 500 series, announced on 8 March 1996 alongside the [[hardware:systems:avanti|AlphaStation 255]], started at $15,863 for the 500/266 and ran to the 500/333 and 500/400. Each had 2 MB of L3 cache, a 256-bit memory bus, up to 512 MB of RAM, up to 8 GB of internal storage in five drive bays, four PCI slots, dual fast and wide SCSI-2 channels, and Ethernet and Fast Ethernet. Digital rated the three models at 7.29, 8.8, and 11 SPECint95 and 10.5, 11.6, and 14 SPECfp95. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-March.mbox#L150|"Press/AlphaStation 255 and 500 Workstations Lead In Price/Performance"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 11 Mar 1996)]

A //Digital Technical Journal// paper describes the AlphaStation 600 5-series design: a uniprocessor built on the 21164 with a 64-bit PCI and an EISA bus, in which "Six CMOS ASICs provide high-bandwidth, low-latency interconnects between the CPU, the main memory, and the I/O subsystem", that is the DECchip 21171 chipset. The team verified it with directed pseudorandom tests on a Verilog model, and then moved to hardware-based verification for throughput, using graphics cards to emulate generic DMA devices while a PCI hardware demon was still being built. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L10964|"Design and Verification of the AlphaStation 600 5-series Workstation"]], John H. Zurawski, John E. Murray, and Paul J. Lemmon, Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Aug 1995)]

=== AlphaStation 500 ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_500:alphastation_500_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 500 Series User Information}}, January 1997, pp. 3-7 to 3-8, C-1)]

  * CIA bridge and cache on the system board, with either 2 MiB or 8 MiB of cache depending on the model
  * Memory as two banks of four 8-byte DIMMs, up to 1 GiB; a bank must be filled completely with identical DIMMs, and Digital recommended one vendor per bank
  * 33 MHz PCI, 32-bit and 64-bit, on a riser card, plus an EISA-attached audio card and a media adapter unit header on the system board
  * The interrupt table matches what the kernel expects: PCI slot 6 is the Ethernet controller and PCI slot 9 the SCSI controller, with the riser slots on PCI slots 7, 8, 11, and 12 [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_500:alphastation_500_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 500 Series User Information}}, p. D-3)] [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_alcor.c?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_alcor.c]], Linux 6.10)]
  * Four of the interrupt inputs are driven by jumpers rather than devices: IRQ 20 is the alternate console jumper and IRQ 21 the secure console jumper [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_500:alphastation_500_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 500 Series User Information}}, p. D-4)]
  * Models were upgraded by swapping the whole system board, keeping the memory, options, and drives [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_500:alphastation_500_system_board_upgrade.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 500 Series System Board Upgrade}}, October 1996, p. vii)]

=== AlphaStation 600 ===

The technical reference manual describes the machine "from the perspective of the system programmer". [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_technical_reference_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 Series Technical Reference Information}}, July 1995, pp. 1-1 to 1-5)]

  * EV5 CPU with a 128 KiB serial boot ROM; the whole system is synchronous to the EV5 clock, so the PCI clock, cache timing, and memory timing are integer multiples of the CPU frequency and not every speed bin gives optimal timing
  * Five ASICs of two designs: one CIA, which takes address and command from the EV5, drives the memory array, and bridges to PCI, and four DSW data slice ASICs, which give a 256-bit memory datapath here although the ASIC can also be configured for 128 bits
  * A sixth, small GRU ASIC on the IOD bus handles the PCI interrupt logic, the memory and cache presence detect logic, and the 8-bit "Lemmon Bus" used to reach the flash ROMs and the optional system LEDs
  * Direct-mapped, write-back, ECC protected board-level cache split across three SIMMs, all of which must be present, with a fixed 64-byte block size and no duplicate tag store; designs ran from 2 MB to 16 MB, with 4 MB "typical". DMA writes invalidate the cache
  * Two memory motherboards, which must both be present and symmetrically populated, each holding up to 16 standard 36-bit SIMMs, for 32 MiB to 1 GiB and a 180 ns memory latency with 60 ns SIMMs. The chipset supports 8 GiB of physical memory but "the workstation implementation is limited to 4 GB"
  * Eight option slots: three 64-bit PCI, one 32-bit PCI, one shared PCI/EISA, and three (E)ISA, of which the graphics, the I/O subsystem module, and the audio card take three, leaving five
  * 33 MHz PCI only, and although the system generates and accepts 64-bit data it "does not generate 64-bit PCI addresses"; fast back-to-back cycles are issued and accepted in dense space only, and can be disabled through a CSR
  * The CIA holds a 32-entry scatter/gather TLB, configured as 8 entries of 4 contiguous PTEs, so both physical and virtual DMA are available
  * Intel 82374EB and 82375EB PCI-EISA bridge, Intel 8242 keyboard and mouse controller, Dallas 1287 time-of-year clock, Dallas 1225 8 KiB battery-backed SRAM, and an 87312 combo chip for the serial and parallel ports and the floppy
  * The I/O subsystem module is a separate PCI card carrying two QLogic ISP1020 SCSI chips, a DECchip 21040 Tulip Ethernet controller, and a DECchip 21050 PCI-to-PCI bridge, which matches what the axp-list description of the "COMBO card" above says
  * Early systems instead shipped with a KZPAA-AX single-ended narrow SCSI controller and either a DE435 Etherworks Turbo PCI card or a P2SE module; the KZPAA-AX external connector is unused in that configuration and the rear bulkhead must carry an active terminator [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_io_subsystem_information_supplement.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 Series I/O Subsystem Information Supplement}}, February 1995, pp. 1-3)]
  * Memory is added in eight-SIMM increments, four per tower, in matching locations in each tower quadrant [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 Series User Information}}, June 1995, pp. 3-8 to 3-10)]
  * The cache module hold-down bracket, which the user manual describes as being for shipping only, "should not be removed from the memory tower": Digital reversed that instruction in a January 1997 field service notification, because the bracket supports the cache modules in use [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_cache_module_hold_down_bracket_notification.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 Series Cache Module Hold-Down Bracket Field Service Notification}}, EK-AS800-IS.A01, January 1997)]
  * The 333 MHz upgrade is not a CPU swap: the kit replaces the system board and the cache modules together, and was to be installed by a Digital service provider [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_333_mhz_upgrade_information_sheet.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 333 MHz Upgrade Information Sheet}}, EK-PB62U-IS.A01)]

==== Kernels ====

  * The kernel has separate Alcor and XLT machine vectors, and tells them apart "by looking for a 21040 TULIP in slot 6, which is built into XLT and BRET/MAVERICK, but not available on ALCOR." [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_alcor.c?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_alcor.c]], Linux 6.10)] "Running an ALCOR kernel on a true XLT will cause additional bits in the interrupt summary register to be treated as real interrupts, causing im most circumstances what looks like a hang but is really just taking continuous bogus interrupts." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-January.mbox#L79961|"Re: Alpha XL300 (aka XLT) problems"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 28 Jan 1999)] Before that was fixed in 1998, an Alcor kernel or MILO on an XLT or AlphaStation 500 produced "infinite messages". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-July.mbox#L51981|"Re: 5.1 on PC164 woes"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 20 Jul 1998)]
  * On the AlphaStation 500, "You should have the best luck with an XLT MILO, as the BRET and XLT are extremely close, architecture-wise." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2003-April.mbox#L18099|"Re: AlphaStation 500 MILO problems + Voodoo questions"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 28 Apr 2003)]

==== Firmware ====

  * The AlphaStation 600 is best booted from SRM: "I don't recommend running an ALCOR from ARC console, booting MILO ... It would be much better to run it from SRM console, booting a kernel directly (via "aboot" of course, or BOOTP). This is how Linus runs his". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L10317|"Re: Kernel 2.1.125 testing"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Oct 1998)]
  * "There is no *official* SRM console for the XLT; it was designed/sold strictly for WindowsNT." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-October.mbox#L20901|"Re: Milo booting other OSes?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 Oct 1997)] "It's best to keep using MILO with XLT, as well as XL." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2004-October.mbox#L3405|"AC-0.9 Testing Results (was: Re: Alpha Core ISO Images)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 18 Oct 2004)]

==== CIA erratum ====

"the CIA-1 core logic in AS500/333 as well as EB164 and XLT ... is flawed", which the 2.4.13 PCI changes exposed. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-June.mbox#L8071|"Re: [LONG] CIA breakage in 2.4.13 (was Re: AS500 and 2.4 SCSI blues)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 9 Jun 2002)] The kernel still avoids the affected DMA windows on CIA revision 1. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/core_cia.c?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/kernel/core_cia.c]], Linux 6.10)]

==== SCSI ====

The AlphaStation 500 and AlphaServer 800 have a built-in QLogic 1020, which failed with the Red Hat 7.1 ''qlogicisp'' driver but worked with the newer QLogic driver. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L1901|"Re: QLogic ISP problem"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Jan 2002)]
