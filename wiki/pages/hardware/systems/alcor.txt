===== AlphaStation 500 and 600 and Alpha XLT =====

The AlphaStation 600 (Alcor), AlphaStation 500 (Maverick and Bret), and Alpha XL 300, 366, and 433 (XLT) share the CIA core logic, EISA, and one kernel platform. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-March.mbox#L30004|"Re: AlphaServer 800"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Mar 1998)] [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/Kconfig?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/Kconfig]], Linux 6.10)] "XLT stands for XL-Turbo, not XL-True". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-December.mbox#L30289|"Re: Can't load RAMDISK on XL-266"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 23 Dec 1997)]

==== Specifications ====

  * [[wp>Alpha_21164|21164]] and [[wp>Alpha_21164#Alpha_21164A|21164A]] CPUs with 21171 (CIA) core logic, PCI and EISA [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-May.mbox#L17455|"Re: Linux on Alpha 600"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 May 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-March.mbox#L30004|"Re: AlphaServer 800"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Mar 1998)]
  * The AlphaStation 600 "has the EV5 CPU, plus PCI and EISA busses. There's a TGA in there ... also a COMBO card as well, with a DEC TULIP Ethernet controller on it and a pair of QLogic 1020ISP SCSI F/W controllers on it." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-May.mbox#L17455|"Re: Linux on Alpha 600"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 May 1996)]
  * The AlphaStation 500 has a built-in QLogic 1020 and "has no IDE"; its onboard sound "was actually on an EISA bus" [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-January.mbox#L1901|"Re: QLogic ISP problem"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Jan 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2006-March.mbox#L1770|"[Fwd: Re: Qlogic 1280 driver blues]"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 24 Mar 2006)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2006-February.mbox#L51|"as500/500 soundcard?"]], Jeff Donsbach, axp-list, 8 Feb 2006)]
  * "the Maverick takes EDO SIMM memory, which is hard to find, while the Alcor takes more normal SIMMs, and has a great many more slots for it." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2003-February.mbox#L2057|"Re: Alphas worth saving?"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 5 Feb 2003)]
  * The XLT and the AlphaStation 500 (Bret and Maverick) have a built-in 21040 Tulip Ethernet controller in PCI slot 6, which the AlphaStation 600 does not [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_alcor.c?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_alcor.c]], Linux 6.10)]

=== AlphaStation 500 ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_500:alphastation_500_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 500 Series User Information}}, January 1997, pp. 3-7 to 3-8, C-1)]

  * CIA bridge and cache on the system board, with either 2 MiB or 8 MiB of cache depending on the model
  * Memory as two banks of four 8-byte DIMMs, up to 1 GiB; a bank must be filled completely with identical DIMMs, and Digital recommended one vendor per bank
  * 33 MHz PCI, 32-bit and 64-bit, on a riser card, plus an EISA-attached audio card and a media adapter unit header on the system board
  * Models were upgraded by swapping the whole system board, keeping the memory, options, and drives [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_500:alphastation_500_system_board_upgrade.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 500 Series System Board Upgrade}}, October 1996, p. vii)]

=== AlphaStation 600 ===

[(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_technical_reference_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 Series Technical Reference Information}}, July 1995, pp. 1-1 to 1-5)]

  * EV5 CPU with a 128 KiB serial boot ROM; the whole system is synchronous to the EV5 clock, so the PCI clock, cache timing, and memory timing are integer multiples of the CPU frequency
  * Five ASICs of two designs: one CIA, which drives the memory array and bridges to PCI, and four DSW data slice ASICs giving a 256-bit memory datapath; a sixth, small GRU ASIC handles PCI interrupts, memory and cache presence detect, and the bus to the flash ROMs
  * Direct-mapped, write-back, ECC protected board-level cache split across three SIMMs, all of which must be present; designs ran from 2 MB to 16 MB, with 4 MB "typical"
  * Two memory motherboards, which must both be present and symmetrically populated, each holding up to 16 standard 36-bit SIMMs, for 32 MiB to 1 GiB. The chipset supports 8 GiB of physical memory but "the workstation implementation is limited to 4 GB"
  * Eight option slots: three 64-bit PCI, one 32-bit PCI, one shared PCI/EISA, and three (E)ISA, of which the graphics, the I/O subsystem module, and the audio card take three, leaving five
  * 33 MHz PCI only, and although the system generates and accepts 64-bit data it "does not generate 64-bit PCI addresses"
  * Intel 82374EB and 82375EB PCI-EISA bridge, Intel 8242 keyboard and mouse controller, Dallas 1287 time-of-year clock, Dallas 1225 8 KiB battery-backed SRAM, and an 87312 combo chip for the serial and parallel ports and the floppy
  * The I/O subsystem module is a separate PCI card carrying two QLogic ISP1020 SCSI chips, a DECchip 21040 Tulip Ethernet controller, and a DECchip 21050 PCI-to-PCI bridge, which matches what the axp-list description of the "COMBO card" above says
  * Early systems instead shipped with a KZPAA-AX single-ended narrow SCSI controller and either a DE435 Etherworks Turbo PCI card or a P2SE module [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_io_subsystem_information_supplement.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 Series I/O Subsystem Information Supplement}}, February 1995, pp. 1-3)]
  * Memory is added in eight-SIMM increments, four per tower, in matching locations in each tower quadrant [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_user_information.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 Series User Information}}, June 1995, pp. 3-8 to 3-10)]
  * The cache module hold-down bracket "should not be removed from the memory tower", although the user manual describes it as being for shipping only: it supports the cache modules in use [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_cache_module_hold_down_bracket_notification.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 Series Cache Module Hold-Down Bracket Field Service Notification}}, EK-AS800-IS.A01, January 1997)]
  * The 333 MHz upgrade is not a CPU swap: the kit replaces the system board and the cache modules together [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphastation_600:alphastation_600_333_mhz_upgrade_information_sheet.pdf?linkonly|AlphaStation 600 333 MHz Upgrade Information Sheet}}, EK-PB62U-IS.A01)]

==== Firmware ====

  * The AlphaStation 600 is best booted from SRM: "I don't recommend running an ALCOR from ARC console, booting MILO ... It would be much better to run it from SRM console, booting a kernel directly (via "aboot" of course, or BOOTP). This is how Linus runs his". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L10317|"Re: Kernel 2.1.125 testing"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Oct 1998)]
  * "There is no *official* SRM console for the XLT; it was designed/sold strictly for WindowsNT." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-October.mbox#L20901|"Re: Milo booting other OSes?"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 29 Oct 1997)] "It's best to keep using MILO with XLT, as well as XL." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2004-October.mbox#L3405|"AC-0.9 Testing Results (was: Re: Alpha Core ISO Images)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 18 Oct 2004)]
  * On the AlphaStation 500, "You should have the best luck with an XLT MILO, as the BRET and XLT are extremely close, architecture-wise." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2003-April.mbox#L18099|"Re: AlphaStation 500 MILO problems + Voodoo questions"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 28 Apr 2003)]

==== Kernels ====

  * The kernel has separate Alcor and XLT machine vectors, and tells them apart "by looking for a 21040 TULIP in slot 6, which is built into XLT and BRET/MAVERICK, but not available on ALCOR." [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_alcor.c?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_alcor.c]], Linux 6.10)] Running an Alcor kernel on a true XLT makes it take "continuous bogus interrupts", which looks like a hang. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-January.mbox#L79961|"Re: Alpha XL300 (aka XLT) problems"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 28 Jan 1999)]
  * "the CIA-1 core logic in AS500/333 as well as EB164 and XLT ... is flawed", which the 2.4.13 PCI changes exposed. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-June.mbox#L8071|"Re: [LONG] CIA breakage in 2.4.13 (was Re: AS500 and 2.4 SCSI blues)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 9 Jun 2002)] The kernel still avoids the affected DMA windows on CIA revision 1. [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/core_cia.c?h=v6.10|arch/alpha/kernel/core_cia.c]], Linux 6.10)]

==== History ====

The AlphaStation 600 family, announced in July 1995, comprised "the first Digital workstations to use the Alpha 21164 chip", with "the first-in-the-industry 64-bit workstation PCI I/O bus". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-July.mbox#L3391|"AlphaStation 600 Family of High Performance Workstations"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 19 Jul 1995)] A //Digital Technical Journal// paper describes its design. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L10964|"Design and Verification of the AlphaStation 600 5-series Workstation"]], John H. Zurawski, John E. Murray, and Paul J. Lemmon, Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Aug 1995)] The AlphaStation 600 5/333 followed on 29 January 1996, which Digital called "the world's first workstation to break the 400 SPECint92 barrier". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L1951|"Press/New High-End AlphaStation Raises Ante In Workstation Market"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 30 Jan 1996)]

The AlphaStation 500 series was announced on 8 March 1996 alongside the [[hardware:systems:avanti|AlphaStation 255]], starting at $15,863 for the 500/266 and running to the 500/333 and 500/400. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-March.mbox#L150|"Press/AlphaStation 255 and 500 Workstations Lead In Price/Performance"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 11 Mar 1996)]
