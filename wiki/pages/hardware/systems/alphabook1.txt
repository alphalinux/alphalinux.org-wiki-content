===== AlphaBook 1 =====

The ALPHAbook 1 is the only Alpha laptop ever sold. It was built by Tadpole Technology, announced at DECUS on 5 December 1995, and shipped from early January 1996 under a strategic agreement with Digital announced in May 1994. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L23398|"Re: Alpha Notebooks, Notebooks with hardware MPEG"]], Britt Lysaa, comp.sys.dec, 5 Jan 1996, quoting a Business Wire release of 5 Dec 1995)] Tadpole had built three generations of SPARCbook notebooks, and the ALPHAbook 1 applied the same design to Digital's 21066A. It was aimed at software development, defense, communications, and engineering customers who needed a portable OpenVMS machine; Digital's own low-power notebook projects, Alpha LEAN and Burns, never shipped. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L25785|"Re: New Alpha XLs????"]], Terry C. Shannon, comp.sys.dec, 11 Jan 1996)]

It is closely related to the [[hardware:motherboards:axppci33|AXPpci 33]] and the Multia: the same 21066A with its integrated PCI and memory controller, plus power management and a Cirrus PCI-to-PCMCIA controller. Jason Thorpe described it for NetBSD as "basically AXPpci33 machines + power management and a Cirrus PCI-PCMCIA controller". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/netbsd-port-alpha-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L7388|"First-draft AlphaBook support"]], Jason Thorpe, port-alpha, 25 Jun 1998)] It is nonetheless a separate Linux platform, ''ALPHABOOK1'', rather than part of Noname. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-March.mbox#L30004|"Re: AlphaServer 800"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Mar 1998)]

Few were built. Terry Shannon reported total sales in the double digits in 1996 and guessed a worldwide installed base of perhaps 50 in 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-December.mbox#L8963|"Re: affordable Alpha laptops?"]], Terry C. Shannon, comp.sys.dec, 18 Dec 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1999-December.mbox#L14133|"Re: VMS on PC's ???"]], Terry C. Shannon, comp.sys.dec, 23 Dec 1999)] Jay Estabrook of Digital, who had a pair, remembered them costing about $10,000 each and called the machine "an Alpha laptop running RH 4.2 on LCA45 at 233MHz". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-October.mbox#L2651|"New AC2 release candidate test boot cd image"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 21 Oct 2005)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2005-October.mbox#L1892|"New AC2 release candidate test boot cd image"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 20 Oct 2005)]

==== Specifications ====

All of the following comes from the announcement unless noted otherwise. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L23398|"Re: Alpha Notebooks, Notebooks with hardware MPEG"]], Britt Lysaa, comp.sys.dec, 5 Jan 1996, quoting a Business Wire release of 5 Dec 1995)]

  * DECchip [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21066A|21066A]] (LCA45) with a variable speed of up to 233 MHz, and 512 KB of write-back level 2 cache
  * PCI bus and memory controller integrated into the CPU, feeding the graphics, PCMCIA, and SCSI interfaces
  * Industry-standard SIMMs, user upgradable, 32 MB to 128 MB
  * Removable 2.5-inch SCSI-2 hard disk, 520 MB, 810 MB, or 1.2 GB
  * Western Digital WD90C24A2 graphics controller with 1 MB of video RAM [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-October.mbox#L12148|"Tadpole Alpha1 notebook"]], Nigel Metheringham, axp-list, 11 Oct 1996)]
  * 10.4-inch true-color TFT panel, 800x600 with 256 colors from a palette of 262,144; up to 1024x768 on an external display
  * Two PCMCIA slots taking two Type I/II cards or one Type III card, driven by a Cirrus 6729 PCI-to-PCMCIA controller [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_sio.c?h=v6.9|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_sio.c]], Linux 6.9)]
  * 3Com Ethernet PC Card included; optional 28.8K fax/modem PC Card; two docking station options
  * Integrated 16-bit stereo audio with internal microphone and speaker and in/out ports
  * Hitachi H8/337 microcontroller monitoring battery charging and CPU temperature [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-alpha-mbox/googlegroups-1998-November.mbox#L20960|"REQ: Tadpole AlphaBook1 H8/337 power/thermal monitor info"]], Thomas Pfau, comp.os.linux.alpha, 13 Nov 1998)]
  * Rugged magnesium case, NiMH battery, 7.5 pounds

==== Heat and clock throttling ====

The machine's defining problem is that the 21066A cannot be cooled in a notebook case. The firmware drops the clock when the system gets warm, and it rarely ran at its rated speed. Jay Estabrook: "AlphaBook1 was based on LCA45 @ 233MHz (same as top-end UDB), but typically it could run only at 116 MHz (or even 57 MHz) because of heat". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-April.mbox#L9388|"Re: Alpha laptops"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 Apr 2000)]

Owners saw the same thing. Jerry Leslie, who used one for OpenVMS trade show demos, reported that "the system will not keep running at its maximum CPU speed without an external cooling source, such as a fan", and that an external fan was too obtrusive for a customer site. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1997-August.mbox#L35804|"Need Quiet Cooling for Tadpole ALPHABook"]], Jerry Leslie, comp.sys.dec, 6 Aug 1997)] Fred Kleinsorge of Digital replied that "the best way to cool a Tadpole is idle time", and that his own machine stayed within normal temperatures except under sustained compute load. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1997-August.mbox#L28441|"Re: Need Quiet Cooling for Tadpole ALPHABook"]], Fred Kleinsorge, comp.sys.dec, 8 Aug 1997)] A reseller's first suggestion was to unfold the rear legs for airflow underneath. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1997-August.mbox#L27104|"Re: Need Quiet Cooling for Tadpole ALPHABook"]], Al Herb, comp.sys.dec, 7 Aug 1997)] Stephen Doney's site gave up on theirs: "found they ran too hot and the cpu slowed down to much. The combination of heat and the slow performance stopped us using them." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-alpha-mbox/googlegroups-1998-November.mbox#L21044|"Re: Tadpole AlphaBook1 H8/337 power/thermal monitor info"]], Stephen Doney, comp.os.linux.alpha, 13 Nov 1998)] Estabrook also remembered it as "*very* heavy (metal case), and a NiMH battery that was down to 70% when finished booting (from a full charge)", but praised the TFT panel and the PCMCIA slots. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-April.mbox#L9388|"Re: Alpha laptops"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 Apr 2000)]

==== Firmware ====

The AlphaBook 1 has only the SRM console. Jay Estabrook: "You boot it from SRM console (that's the only thing available, anyway :-)". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-August.mbox#L12298|"Re: Alpha based notebook available????"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Aug 1997)] Digital shipped its firmware in an ''alphabook1'' directory on the Alpha firmware CD-ROM and on ''gatekeeper.dec.com'', alongside the other SRM systems. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/netbsd-port-alpha-mbox/1997-February.mbox#L2455|"Re: Support for AXP Motherboard"]], Carl S. Shapiro, port-alpha, 21 Feb 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-October.mbox#L7397|"Re: How to flash MILO? ( Not a faq )"]], Peter Rival, axp-list, 6 Oct 1999)] Because there is no ARC console, Linux boots from SRM with aboot; MILO nevertheless carried AlphaBook1 support in Nikita Schmidt's builds. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/1998-September.mbox#L59|"MILO"]], Nikita Schmidt, debian-alpha, 3 Sep 1998)]

==== Linux ====

Linux support came early and from inside Digital. Jim Paradis had Linux "up and creaking on a early-prototype Tadpole laptop" in May 1996 while trying to get PCMCIA working. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-May.mbox#L7828|"insmod troubles -- any clues?"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 10 May 1996)] Richard Evans of Tadpole ran Red Hat 3.0.3 on a production machine the same month, with PCMCIA working, after local kernel and PCMCIA changes. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-May.mbox#L11707|"Re: insmod troubles -- any clues?"]], Richard Evans, axp-list, 17 May 1996)] The platform reached Jay Estabrook's public patch kit in July 1997, listed as "AlphaBook1 aka the HOT laptop :-)", and Estabrook also had an X server for it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-July.mbox#L20316|"NEW and IMPROVED Alpha Patches and MILO Sources!"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 17 Jul 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-August.mbox#L12298|"Re: Alpha based notebook available????"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Aug 1997)]

The kernel platform is ''CONFIG_ALPHA_ALPHABOOK1'', described in the configuration help simply as "Alpha laptop". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-November.mbox#L3174|"Re: linux-2.4.0-test10 compiling errors"]], Christopher C. Chimelis, axp-list, 2 Nov 2000)] It shares ''sys_sio.c'' with the Noname and Avanti platforms but has its own machine vector, which fixes the console at 800x600 and disables SCSI burst mode because "the PCMCIA chip (Cirrus 6729) is sensitive to PCI bus bursts". [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/kernel/sys_sio.c?h=v6.9|arch/alpha/kernel/sys_sio.c]], Linux 6.9)] Debian's installer treated it as the ''alphabook1'' subarchitecture. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L2671|"Re: booting/installing debian-alpha"]], Kristoffer Rose, debian-alpha, 11 Jun 1998)] Support was removed along with the rest of Alpha after Linux 6.9.

X was always awkward. The WD90C24A2 is not handled by XFree86 4.x, and the XFree86 3.3.6 ''XF86_SVGA'' server as shipped for Alpha was built without the ''wd90c24'' driver. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2002-March.mbox#L3205|"Xfree 3.3.6 and a wd90c24(a) videocard"]], g, debian-alpha, 28 Mar 2002)] Peter Petrakis produced XFree86 3.3.6 patches and Debian packages for the AlphaBook in April 2002. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2002-April.mbox#L22858|"speaking of patches, alphabook X support"]], Peter Petrakis, axp-list, 20 Apr 2002)]

==== Known issues ====

  * **PCMCIA versus SCSI:** Davis Johnson could build a kernel that saw the SCSI controller and disk, or one that saw the PCMCIA bridge, but never both; with the PCMCIA bridge probed, the disk disappeared and the machine could not find its root filesystem. Since the only networking is a PCMCIA card, this makes a networked, disk-booted machine hard to achieve. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2009-September.mbox#L976|"[RFC] Dropping Alpha sparse mapping support from X"]], Davis Johnson, axp-list, 20 Sep 2009)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2015-April.mbox#L946|"still someone here ?"]], Davis Johnson, axp-list, 7 Apr 2015)] He described the cause as "that weird PCMCIA bridge mucking up DMA on the Alphabook", and no fix was ever found on the list. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2015-April.mbox#L885|"still someone here ?"]], Davis Johnson, axp-list, 7 Apr 2015)]
  * **Disk size:** the drive is a 2.5-inch SCSI unit, and these are scarce; the largest reported in an AlphaBook or a Multia is 1.2 GB. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/netbsd-port-alpha-mbox/2002-February.mbox#L2356|"Re: RAM and drive for Multia?"]], Julian Coleman, port-alpha, 14 Feb 2002)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2015-April.mbox#L946|"still someone here ?"]], Davis Johnson, axp-list, 7 Apr 2015)]
  * **Memory ceiling:** 128 MB is the maximum, which limits what can usefully be built on the machine itself. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2015-April.mbox#L946|"still someone here ?"]], Davis Johnson, axp-list, 7 Apr 2015)]
  * **Installation media:** there is no CD-ROM drive, so installation is done over the network from a PCMCIA card or from an external disk. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-alpha-mbox/googlegroups-2000-November.mbox#L2418|"Tadpole AlphaBook 1 with linux.."]], KaV, comp.os.linux.alpha, 20 Nov 2000)]

==== Other operating systems ====

  * **OpenVMS** was the launch operating system, together with DECwindows/Motif, TCP/IP Services for OpenVMS, and OpenVMS Cluster Client. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L23398|"Re: Alpha Notebooks, Notebooks with hardware MPEG"]], Britt Lysaa, comp.sys.dec, 5 Jan 1996, quoting a Business Wire release of 5 Dec 1995)] This made the machine unusual: OpenVMS did not otherwise support 21066-based systems such as the AXPpci 33 and the Multia, and support was "only recently added" for the AlphaBook. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1997-April.mbox#L9285|"Re: Help: AXPpci33 (was Re: Help: DEC PCI33 M/B)"]], Stephen Hoffman, comp.sys.dec, 25 Apr 1997)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-March.mbox#L21056|"Re: AXP hardware support for VMS"]], Vance R. Haemmerle, comp.sys.dec, 14 Mar 1996)] OpenVMS V7.3-1 was the last release to support it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-tru64-mbox/googlegroups-2002-January.mbox#L1983|"Re: Where does VMS fit in here? (Was Re: HP admits...)"]], david20@alpha1.mdx.ac.uk, comp.unix.tru64, 30 Jan 2002)]
  * **Digital UNIX** was promised for 1996 and could be ordered by August 1997. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1997-August.mbox#L27104|"Re: Need Quiet Cooling for Tadpole ALPHABook"]], Al Herb, comp.sys.dec, 7 Aug 1997)]
  * **Windows NT** was never supported. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-April.mbox#L10601|"Re: NT on Alpha chart?"]], Jeff Monasch, comp.sys.dec, 26 Apr 1996)] There were no technical obstacles, but at a list price running from about $15,000 to $22,000 there was no market for an Alpha NT notebook against Pentium machines at under $5,000. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-May.mbox#L29152|"Re: NT on Alpha chart?"]], Al Herb, comp.sys.dec, 10 May 1996)]
  * **NetBSD** gained preliminary support in June 1998, committed by Jason Thorpe as a copy of the AXPpci 33 code with the interrupt mapping adjusted, without power management and with an incomplete PCI-PCMCIA driver. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/netbsd-port-alpha-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L7388|"First-draft AlphaBook support"]], Jason Thorpe, port-alpha, 25 Jun 1998)] It appeared as a new platform in NetBSD 1.4, with the kernel option ''DEC_ALPHABOOK1''. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/netbsd-port-alpha-mbox/1999-April.mbox#L9301|"NetBSD-1.4_ALPHA/alpha available [actually BETA]"]], Ross Harvey, port-alpha, 22 Apr 1999)]

==== Availability ====

List prices started at $13,950 for 32 MB of memory, a 520 MB disk, the 3Com Ethernet card, and bundled OpenVMS, and a fully loaded machine ran to $25,000. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L23398|"Re: Alpha Notebooks, Notebooks with hardware MPEG"]], Britt Lysaa, comp.sys.dec, 5 Jan 1996, quoting a Business Wire release of 5 Dec 1995)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-os-linux-alpha-mbox/googlegroups-2001-October.mbox#L1403|"Re: ALPHAbook1"]], Chan F. Y. K., comp.os.linux.alpha, 28 Oct 2001, quoting Information Week, 11 Dec 1995, p. 28)] Production had ended by 1999. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1999-July.mbox#L11339|"Re: wanted : alphabook"]], Harry Bligth, comp.sys.dec, 22 Jul 1999)] Ron Farrer was quoted about $18,000 for one of Tadpole's remaining machines around 1998, and in 2000 Jay Estabrook thought Tadpole might still build one "if someone offered to pay enough (>$10K)". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-April.mbox#L8935|"Re: Alpha laptops"]], Ron Farrer, axp-list, 13 Apr 2000)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-April.mbox#L9388|"Re: Alpha laptops"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 13 Apr 2000)]

==== Conflicting information ====

The following sources conflict with each other or with the account given above. They are listed here for further investigation.

  * **Codename**
    * The kernel's system variation tables and the lists posted by Digital engineers give the codename Burns (see [[hardware:systems|Systems]]).
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L25785|"Re: New Alpha XLs????"]], Terry C. Shannon, comp.sys.dec, 11 Jan 1996: Burns, "a.k.a. Green Machine", was a Digital prototype, the successor to Alpha LEAN and designed to run Windows NT, and distinct from the AlphaBook 1.
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1999-November.mbox#L19749|"Re: Digital Miata Computer"]], Fred Kleinsorge, comp.sys.dec, 11 Nov 1999: the design underlying both the Multia and the AlphaBook 1 was "Mustang 1", an LCA-based machine.
  * **Relationship to the AXPpci 33**
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-April.mbox#L8935|"Re: Alpha laptops"]], Ron Farrer, axp-list, 13 Apr 2000: "It was based on the AXPpci33 motherboard (21066)."
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/netbsd-port-alpha-mbox/1998-June.mbox#L7388|"First-draft AlphaBook support"]], Jason Thorpe, port-alpha, 25 Jun 1998: "basically AXPpci33 machines + power management and a Cirrus PCI-PCMCIA controller", which is a statement about the programming model rather than a shared board.
  * **CPU**
    * The announcement, and most later reports, say 21066A at up to 233 MHz.
    * The overview table in [[hardware:systems|Systems]], following the kernel, lists the platform under the 21066.
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-sys-dec-mbox/googlegroups-1996-January.mbox#L23617|"Re: Alpha Notebooks, Notebooks with hardware MPEG"]], Steve Lionel, comp.sys.dec, 5 Jan 1996: "a 21066-AB processor running at 233MHz".
