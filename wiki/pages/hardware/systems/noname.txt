===== Multia and AXPpci 33 (Noname) =====

The Multia, or UDB ("Universal Desktop Box"), is a small desktop with a 21066 or 21066A CPU, onboard TGA graphics, a 21040 Ethernet controller, and one PCI slot. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L329|"BETA Xserver for TGA Available"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Feb 1996)] "DEC had an agreement with Microsoft that every "Multia" system would be shipped with Windows NT installed." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L4880|"Re: Unable to install redhat 5.0 on multia through serial port"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 7 Jan 1998)] Digital did not sell it as a general purpose workstation: the Multia family was announced on 1 December 1994 as a "MultiClient Desktop", a network ready thin client built on the application server model, with user applications and data held on central servers and executed locally. Multia Base Software V2.5 was factory installed, combining Windows NT Workstation V3.5, "a highly optimized X11R6 X server", terminal emulators for legacy applications, and configuration management software that let administrators control the desktop centrally over a LAN or WAN. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-December.mbox#L61|"Multia Family of MultiClient Desktops"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 1 Dec 1994)] The [[hardware:motherboards:axppci33|AXPpci 33]] is a Baby-AT motherboard: "The AXPpci33 (aka NoName), is based on the EB66 (includes the Multia UDB). This design was produced by Digital's Technical OEM (TOEM) group". [(>[[https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/arch/alpha/Kconfig?h=v6.9|arch/alpha/Kconfig]], Linux 6.9)] The two "use the same CPUs and have the same interrupt handling mechanisms", [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-September.mbox#L1751|"Re: interesting benchmarks"]], David A Rusling, axp-list, 3 Sep 1996)] and "we use the same identical MILO for both UDB and NONAME platforms." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L949|"Re: fmu on PC164"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Jan 1998)]

Digital's fact sheet gives the machine it sold: a 166 MHz Alpha AXP CPU, 24 MiB of memory expandable to 256 MiB, Windows NT Workstation 3.5, a PCI graphics accelerator, 16-bit audio, autosensing PCI Ethernet for ThinWire, thickwire, and 10BaseT, PCMCIA and PCI expansion, and a Kensington lock, with the X11R6 server and IBM terminal emulation as options due "within 90 days of Multia's first commercial shipment". Four standard configurations were priced between $3,495 and $6,000; the $3,495 VX40A-N3 system box had 24 MB of memory, a 340 MB SCSI disk, two PCMCIA slots, and no floppy drive. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L3588|"Fact Sheet/Multia MultiClient Desktop"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 3 Nov 1994)] The pitch was administrative cost rather than performance: Digital cited Nolan, Norton & Co. on how many employees had networked PCs and the Gartner Group on PC administration being "a nightmare that will likely get worse before it gets better". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L3876|"Backgrounder/Multia MultiClient Desktop"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 3 Nov 1994)]

In May 1995 Digital added a 233 MHz model at the top of the family, about 44 percent faster than the 166 MHz model with 512 KiB of cache against 256 KiB, and a new 166 MHz system box (VX41A-F2) with an upgradable CPU, cache expandable from 256 KB to 1 MB, and a 528 MB 2.5-inch SCSI disk. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-May.mbox#L3734|"Multia MultiClient Desktop Device with New Configurations"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 30 May 1995)] The software was revised at the same time: Multia X Server Option V3.0 added XIE, the Shape extension, X sessions over DECnet, TCP/IP, or LAT, XDMCP, and NFS font and file access, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-May.mbox#L3267|"Multia X Server Option V3.0"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 30 May 1995)] while Multia Base Software V3.0 added DECnet and LAT support and a Remote Maintenance Facility for software updates. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-May.mbox#L4306|"Multia Base Software V3.0 and Multia System Administrator Kit V3.0"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 30 May 1995)] The line later moved off Alpha: the Multia Enterprise Client of November 1995 put the same software on 100 MHz Intel Pentium models alongside the Alpha ones. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-November.mbox#L1554|"Multia Enterprise Client with Pentium Processor"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 15 Nov 1995)]

The 21066 at the heart of the Multia was "the first Alpha AXP microprocessor to target cost-focused system applications", a 0.675 micron CMOS dual-issue design that integrated the memory controller, a PCI I/O controller, graphics assisting hardware, the primary caches, and an external cache controller on one die, with an on-chip analog phase-locked loop and cost-saving packaging to suit personal computers and desktop systems. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-June.mbox#L8845|"Digital's DECchip 21066: The First Cost-focused Alpha AXP Chip"]], Dina L. McKinney et al., Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 7 Jun 1994)]

==== Specifications ====

The following describes the Multia; for the motherboard see [[hardware:motherboards:axppci33|AXPpci 33]].

  * [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21066|21066]] or [[wp>Alpha_21064#Alpha_21066A|21066A]] at 166 or 233 MHz, with the memory and PCI controllers on the CPU die [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L4880|"Re: Unable to install redhat 5.0 on multia through serial port"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 7 Jan 1998)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-April.mbox#L14880|"Re: UDB's"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 7 Apr 1997)]
  * 64-bit memory bus, so SIMMs go in pairs rather than quartets, and parity SIMMs are required; parity cannot be disabled [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2008-December.mbox#L1027|"Alpha hardware"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 5 Dec 2008)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-July.mbox#L9098|"Re: UDB RAM question"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 23 Jul 1996)] EDO SIMMs do not work. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-July.mbox#L1649|"RE: Upgrading RAM on Multia"]], Lambert Hofstra, alphant, 4 Jul 1996)]
  * Onboard TGA (21030) graphics and a 21040 Ethernet controller [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L329|"BETA Xserver for TGA Available"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Feb 1996)]
  * The BNC connector is wired unusually: under Windows NT, autodetection found the 21040 but could not then drive BNC, because "the BNC connector on the Multia is handled in a non-standard way, relative to other NT DC21040 implementations", and the machine's own entry had to be picked from the driver list instead of the autodetected DC21040. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L7695|"RE: Multia's BNC connection?"]], James Livingston, alphant, 23 Feb 1996)]
  * Onboard SCSI, at IRQ 11, with an external SCSI port; riser cards came in several configurations [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-August.mbox#L9692|"Re: MSS sound and Avanti (was: MSS sound and UDB)"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 8 Aug 1996)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-April.mbox#L7305|"RE: Multia/UDB SCSI termination problems"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Apr 1996)]
  * PCMCIA slots, supported in Linux from 1996 with Jim Paradis's patches to ''pcmcia-cs'' and the kernel [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-April.mbox#L402|"Patches for PCMCIA on UDB"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 1 Apr 1996)]
  * One PCI slot [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L329|"BETA Xserver for TGA Available"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 2 Feb 1996)]
  * Flash holding SRM in two chips and ARC in the third [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-October.mbox#L47543|"UDB flash and failsafe loader experiments"]], Eric Smith, axp-list, 29 Oct 1996)] Digital never shipped a way to replace either: its own Windows NT support page for the Multia said that there was no mechanism for updating the machine's firmware. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-July.mbox#L1322|"FWD: Latest firmware for Multia?"]], David Nieters, alphant, 3 Jul 1996)]

==== As sold ====

Setting one up was a job in itself, because the machine expected to be managed rather than owned. There was no printed documentation: the Administrator's Reference came as PostScript in a separately ordered System Administrators Kit. A new Multia booted into a startup screen that wanted to search the network for a configuration server, so the first machine on a site had to be made its own configuration server before anything else, through a configuration application, a workgroup, and a desktop entry, each repeating the same settings. The default login was QSUSER with the password QSUSER, and the ZLXp drivers shipped with the machine did not work, so a dual-head Multia needed newer ones from Digital or the Alpha NT archive. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L2288|"Re: Multia Documentation? (SUMMARY)"]], Lennart Börjeson, alphant, 8 Feb 1996)]

==== Models and jumpers ====

Digital's service information separates the three Alpha models: "The VX40 model has a fixed 166 MHz Alpha CPU and fixed 256K cache memory", the VX41 has an upgradable 166 MHz CPU in a ZIF socket and upgradable 256 KiB cache, and the VX42 has an upgradable 233 MHz CPU and 512 KiB of cache, with 256 KiB, 512 KiB, and 1 MiB cache options across the family. All ship with 24 MiB of ECC memory expandable to 128 MiB, two type 1 or 2 PCMCIA slots, autosensing 10Base5, 10Base2, and 10BaseT Ethernet, two serial lines with full modem support, and a short-card PCI slot on machines with the 2.5-inch disk; the floppy drive and the SCSI-2 connector are standard on the VX41 and VX42 and optional on the VX40. The VX51 is the Intel model, a 100 MHz Pentium. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:multia:multia_service_information.pdf?linkonly|Multia MultiClient Desktop Service Information}}, EK-MULTS-IN.C01, pp. 1-2)]

The system board jumpers differ between the VX40 and the later two. W9 on a VX40, or W12 on a VX41 or VX42, selects the mini console; W8 or W10 is the normal power-up sequence; W7 or W11 forces a floppy boot fail safe load. W6 to W3, the same on all three, "determine the resolution driven to the graphics display" under SRM, coding for VGA, ERGO VGA, SVGA, ERGO SVGA, VESA 1024x768, Digital 1024x768, and Digital 1024x864 modes. J8 set 2 to 3, the default, makes the front switch a reset, and set 1 to 2 makes it a halt request interrupt. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:multia:multia_service_information.pdf?linkonly|Multia MultiClient Desktop Service Information}}, pp. 102-103)]

==== Performance ====

The 21066 "takes an EV4 processor core, removes half of the on-chip L1 cache, and adds a PCI interface on-chip. These two modifications tend to really bottleneck the memory access." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-April.mbox#L14880|"Re: UDB's"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 7 Apr 1997)] "A 166-MHz UDB performs about like a 90 or 100MHz Pentium. The 233-MHz UDB performs like a 120 or 133MHz Pentium." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-January.mbox#L4880|"Re: Unable to install redhat 5.0 on multia through serial port"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 7 Jan 1998)]

==== Booting Linux ====

MILO was originally written for the Noname, "to run LINUX on them without requiring SRM console (and its license fee)". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-December.mbox#L32014|"Re: Linux_is_Great!"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 15 Dec 1998)] MILO booted from SRM "defaults to 24Mb" of memory. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-July.mbox#L64302|"Re: UDB only sees 24M of RAM"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 26 Jul 1998)] For Alpha Core 2, use a text mode install "as the TGA in the Multia will prolly not work for GUI install". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2006-February.mbox#L3031|"Installing Alpha Core 2 on Multia"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 22 Feb 2006)]

==== Heat and power ====

"The UDB's case is so campact that many folks have discovered that they have problems with their machines due to it overheating." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-September.mbox#L10163|"Re: dec hardware"]], Vernard Martin, axp-list, 12 Sep 1997)] The 3.3V wires of its power connector discolor and fail. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-September.mbox#L7408|"UDB Power Problem Solved"]], Michael Brennen, axp-list, 10 Sep 1997)]

==== Linux ====

The ''de2104x'' driver "was known to fail on those machines (AS250/AS500/Multia) that have a builtin 21040 (old/original) TULIP"; use ''de4x5''. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2006-February.mbox#L8047|"Re: testing wanted: debian-installer, now with 2.6.15"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 10 Feb 2006)]
