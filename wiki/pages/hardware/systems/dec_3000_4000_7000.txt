===== DEC 3000, DEC 4000, and DEC 7000 =====

These are Digital's first Alpha systems, predating PCI. "The DEC 3000 series was the first Alpha-based workstation ever produced. Its system bus was TurboChannel". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L63171|"Re: DEC 3000"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 6 Oct 1998)] The DEC 3000 Models 300 are Pelican, the 400, 600, and 700 Sandpiper, and the 500, 800, and 900 Flamingo; the Models 700 and 900 have EV45 CPUs. The DEC 4000 (Cobra and Fang) is a Futurebus+ server with up to two CPUs, and the DEC 7000 and 10000 (Ruby) have up to six. [(>{{wiki:documentation:references:alpha_axp_system_reference_manual_v6.pdf?linkonly|Alpha AXP System Reference Manual}}, Version 6, 1994, pp. D-2, D-5, D-6)] Digital's UK UNIX support gave the individual system codenames in 1993 as Laser for the DEC 7000 and Cobra for the DEC 4000, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1993-May.mbox#L3809|"Re: Alpha codenames?"]], Stephen Carpenter, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 4 May 1993)] and a Digital engineer added Blazer for the DEC 10000: "Blazer is a 'Big laser' with some serious battery backup and more!!" [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1993-May.mbox#L3934|"Re: Alpha codenames?"]], Joe Smith, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 4 May 1993)]

Digital announced the fastest DEC 3000 models in August 1994. The Model 900, available that month with DEC OSF/1 AXP V2.1 and OpenVMS AXP V6.1, was sold as the "fastest workstation in the world" on the strength of 16.82 XMARKS with the standard ZLX-E1 graphics card: a 275 MHz 21064A with 2 MB of cache, 32 MB to 1 GB of memory, two 10 MB/s Fast SCSI-2 controllers, and six 100 MB/s TURBOchannel slots, one of them taken by the graphics card. Model 800 owners could upgrade by a field service board swap, while Model 500 and 500X owners received a new system box. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-August.mbox#L4889|"DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Workstation Info Sheet"]], DECnews for Education, biz.digital.articles, 10 Aug 1994)] The Model 700 announced alongside it started at $23,000 for a 64 MB building block, or $27,698 with a 21-inch color monitor, 64 MB of memory, a 1 GB disk, and a CD-ROM, and the Model 600 was repriced at the same time to become "the fastest workstation under $20,000". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-August.mbox#L4776|"DEC 3000 Model 700 AXP and Reduced-Price Model 600 AXP"]], DECnews for Education, biz.digital.articles, 10 Aug 1994)] A rackmount Model 900, announced a week earlier for Digital's 19-inch ANSI/EIA cabinets or third-party enclosures, rated 189 SPECint92 and 264 SPECfp92, up to 35 percent faster than the Model 800 it upgraded from by a CPU swap. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-August.mbox#L2333|"Rackmount DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Servers and Workstations"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 1 Aug 1994)] At the launch prices of July 1993, the Model 300L listed at $8,700 with 32 MB, a 16-inch color monitor, and a 1 GB disk, rated 45.9 SPECint92 and 63.6 SPECfp92, and the Model 300 at $13,000 with a 19-inch monitor, rated 66.2 and 91.5. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1993-July.mbox#L1153|"Re: Summary: Alpha Performance"]], Steve Chapin, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 24 Jul 1993)] At the low end, the Model 300LX and 300X replaced the Model 300L and Model 300 in June 1994, sold as three-user packages with two VXT 2000+ X terminals. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-June.mbox#L587|"DEC 3000 Model 300X and 300LX Workstation Companions"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 6 Jun 1994)]

Digital sold the DEC 3000 partly as an upgrade path off its older MIPS and VAX workstations. Each model's upgrade kit is a whole new system box into which the customer moves drives, memory, and TURBOchannel graphics, and the old machine goes back to Digital in the packing material the new one arrived in. The Model 300X/300LX kit covers the DECstation 2100/3100, DECstation 5000 Model 20/25/33/50 and 120/125/133/150, and DECsystem 5000 Model 200/240/260; the Model 600/600S and 800/800S kits cover the DECstation and DECsystem 5000 families and the DECsystem 3100; the Model 700 kit covers the DEC 3000 Model 400 and the DECstation 5000 lines; and the Model 900 kit covers the DEC 3000 Model 500/500X, the DECstation 5000 Model 240/260, and the VAXstation 4000 Model 90/90A. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_3000:dec_3000_model_300x_300lx_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|DEC 3000 Model 300X/300LX AXP Upgrade Information}}, EK-D33XP-UP.A01)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_3000:dec_3000_model_900_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Upgrade Information}}, EK-D39AX-UP.A01, pp. 4-1 to 4-2)] Factory-installed disks arrive at SCSI ID 3, so a drive carried over from the old machine that is also at ID 3 has to be re-jumpered; ID 7 is reserved for the controller, and "TURBOchannel modules and memory modules do not have SCSI addresses." [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_3000:dec_3000_model_900_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Upgrade Information}}, pp. 4-1, A-1)]

The DEC 3000 ran OpenVMS and DEC OSF/1, and never ran Windows NT. Asked in 1996 whether a DEC 3000 Model 300LX could run Windows NT Server, Digital's James Livingston gave three reasons why the TurboChannel machines, internally "the Flamingo", would not: "there exists no firmware (console) that will support booting Windows NT on these machines", there is "no HAL to mate them to the Windows NT O/S", and "there's no business case which justifies the (significant) software engineering effort required to implement solutions to the technical problems." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L4643|"RE: Re[2]: Win/NT on AXP 3000-300LX?"]], James Livingston, alphant, 15 Feb 1996)] Digital's Windows NT support team put it more bluntly: "It is not that we do not support it. It absolutely does NOT work". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L4280|"Re: Win/NT on AXP 3000-300LX?"]], Scott Bingham, alphant, 14 Feb 1996)] Tom Blinn of Digital's UNIX group added that the first Alpha system to ship with an ARC console was the DECpc AXP 150. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-June.mbox#L12058|"Running Windows NT on a DEC 3000 workstation"]], Tom Blinn, alpha-osf-managers, 7 Jun 1995)] Tru64 UNIX support ended with V5.0A on the DEC 3000; see [[software:operating_systems#end_of_support_for_older_systems|Operating Systems]].

==== Specifications ====

=== DEC 3000 ===

To the operating system the Pelican, Sandpiper, and Flamingo variants are much the same machine. Tom Blinn: the Models 400 and 500 differ "in the enclosure and the number of TC slots", the 600, 800, and 900 "share the same basic design", and all the Flamingo and Sandpiper variants use the kernel configuration identifier ''DEC3000_500''. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-June.mbox#L1279|"SUMMARY: Changes needed on 3000/400 -> 3000/600 upgrade?"]], Dan Cambron, alpha-osf-managers, 29 Jun 1995)]

  * **Memory:** the Model 400 takes up to 512 MB [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-September.mbox#L12559|"SUMMARY - max memory for 3000/400"]], Kent Adams, alpha-osf-managers, 12 Sep 1995)] and the Model 500 1 GB with 32 MB SIMMs (MS15-FA), with sizes mixed only between banks. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-May.mbox#L4900|"Summary, memories for 3000/500"]], Eugene Chu, alpha-osf-managers, 19 May 1995)] On the Model 600 a bank of eight SIMMs is the top, or the bottom, row across all four memory daughter cards. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1997-December.mbox#L2280|"SUMMARY: Memory Specs for 3000 Model 600"]], Jonathan Rodgers, alpha-osf-managers, 22 Dec 1997)] Model 300 system boards below revision K01 have a bus timing error with Digital's faster Samsung memory, fixed by Engineering Change Order EQ-01702-01. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-January.mbox#L10043|"CLARIFICATION of SUMMARY - memory expansion problems"]], Roger Holman, alpha-osf-managers, 9 Jan 1995)]
  * **SCSI:** models up to the 500 run their SCSI buses at a fixed 5 MB/s. The Model 600 and later can run each bus at 5 or 10 MB/s, set by the console variables ''FAST_SCSI_A'' (internal) and ''FAST_SCSI_B'' (external). Of the TURBOchannel SCSI option cards, the PMAZB is fixed at 5 MB/s and the PMAZC is selectable. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1997-May.mbox#L14164|"SUMMARY: SCSI bus speed on Turbochannel machines"]], Martyn Johnson, alpha-osf-managers, 15 May 1997)] Neither is wide; GENROCO's FWDS-20T offered fast-wide differential SCSI for TURBOchannel, and its FWDS-20F did the same for Futurebus+ on the DEC 4000 and 7000. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1994-May.mbox#L8411|"Re: Fast-wide controllers for the DEC 3000 400 or 500"]], Don Woelz, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 23 May 1994)]
  * **Graphics:** the video connector on the TURBOchannel graphics cards is a 3W3 D-sub, the size of a DB15 with three coaxial sockets. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1996-August.mbox#L14363|"SUMMARY: Turbochannel connectors on 3000/600"]], Cary Talbot, alpha-osf-managers, 16 Aug 1996)] DECstation 5000 PXG boards also work, with the Open3D layered product, [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1994-January.mbox#L4154|"Re: Swapping HX and PXG Turbo boards?"]], Fred Velijanian, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 26 Jan 1994)] until Digital UNIX V4.0 retired PXG support. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-October.mbox#L19004|"PXG option support in Digital UNIX V3.2C"]], Tom Blinn, alpha-osf-managers, 2 Oct 1995)] The workstations have 8 kHz mono audio, the ''bba'' (base board audio) device. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L7857|"ULTRIX and DEC OSF/1 AXP Common Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)"]], Steve Lionel, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 21 Aug 1995)]
  * **CPU:** Model 300L systems shipped with both pass 2.1 and pass 3.0 21064 chips; pass 2.1 "is a valid chip for that system". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1993-September.mbox#L6434|"Re: AXP 300L: Processor Version?"]], Gail L. Grant, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 10 Sep 1993)]

=== DEC 4000 ===

The DEC 4000 Model 600 series is built around the KN430 CPU module, which "houses the DECchip 21064 processor", with up to two of them in a symmetric multiprocessing configuration. The system bus is a synchronous multiplexed interconnect carrying "a 34-bit address or a 128-bit data with 32-bit parity in a single cycle", with a snooping protocol keeping each CPU's write-through primary cache and write-back secondary cache consistent with the other CPU and with the I/O port. The arbitration logic and the system bus clock generators live on the CPU 1 module. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_4000:dec_4000_model_600_rackmount_installation_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|DEC 4000 Model 600 AXP Rackmount Installation/Owner's Guide}}, EK-BA641-IN.A01, p. 9-6)]

A second KN430 must be at the same firmware level as the first, or the two may run different PALcode. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-misc-mbox/googlegroups-1996-May.mbox#L872|"Re: Two CPU' on a DEC 4000/610 (620 wannabe)"]], Nick Hill, comp.unix.osf.misc, 22 May 1996)]

  * One to four memory modules, with error detection and correction, read prefetch buffers, write transaction buffers, block exchange, read data wrapping, and "memory interleaving across two or four modules of like sizes"
  * The I/O module carries one or two Ethernet ports based on the third-generation Ethernet chip (TGEC), each switchable between thickwire and ThinWire, a Futurebus+ Profile B interface doing both 32-bit and 64-bit transfers, 512 KB of FEPROM for the console and diagnostic firmware, 8 KB of EEROM, a Dallas Semiconductor DS1287 time-of-year clock, and two asynchronous serial lines, one of them the console
  * The I/O daughter board adds four SCSI-2/DSSI buses for external fixed-media devices, each taking up to seven devices, and one SCSI-2 bus for internal removable and fixed media [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_4000:dec_4000_model_600_rackmount_installation_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|DEC 4000 Model 600 AXP Rackmount Installation/Owner's Guide}}, pp. 9-6 to 9-8)]

=== DEC 7000 and DEC 10000 ===

The CPU is the KN7AA module. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_7000:dec_7000_10000_kn7aa_cpu_technical_manual.pdf?linkonly|DEC 7000/10000 AXP KN7AA CPU Technical Manual}}, pp. 1-1, 1-2, 2-1)]

  * [[wp>Alpha_21064|DECchip 21064]] at a peak clock rate of 200 MHz, in single or multiprocessor configurations
  * 4 MiB physically addressed, direct-mapped, write-back backup cache (B-cache), a superset of the CPU's 8 KiB instruction and 8 KiB data primary caches
  * LSB system bus, connecting CPUs, memory, and the I/O port (IOP) module; all backplane slots except slot 8, which is reserved for the IOP, take CPU or memory modules, and the first CPU should be in slot 0
  * Memory is accessed in 64-byte blocks; the top 256 MiB of the address space is I/O space, divided into LSB CSR space, local Gbus space, and broadcast space
  * Onboard ROMs provide power-up self-test diagnostics and boot support
  * Runs OpenVMS AXP and DEC OSF/1 AXP

The LSB bus itself moves 64-byte memory blocks over a 128-bit data path with a 20 ns cycle time, for a usable bandwidth of 640 MB/s, and "supports writeback caches". Address and data are multiplexed on the same signals, arbitration is distributed and overlaps data transfer, and as many as three transactions can be in progress at once. A populated bus holds "one to six processor nodes, one to seven memory nodes, and a single I/O port node". [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_7000:dec_7000_10000_platform_technical_manual.pdf?linkonly|DEC 7000/10000 AXP and VAX 7000/10000 Platform Technical Manual}}, EK-7000A-TM.001, p. 2-2)] I/O hangs off the IOP as plug-in units (PIUs) filling cabinet quadrants: an XMI PIU takes two quadrants, a Futurebus+ PIU one quadrant and must sit at the rear of the cabinet, a VAXBI PIU two quadrants in the same cabinet as its XMI, and DSSI or SCSI disk PIUs one quadrant each, oriented so the arrow on the rear panel points at the blower. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_7000:dec_7000_10000_platform_technical_manual.pdf?linkonly|DEC 7000/10000 AXP and VAX 7000/10000 Platform Technical Manual}}, p. 1-8)]

The console is the familiar SRM one, with per-processor prompts: ''set cpu 1'' designates the CPU in slot 1 as the primary and the prompt changes from ''P00>>>'' to ''P01>>>''. ''show configuration'' lists the LSB slots by module name, type, revision, and mnemonic, and ''set host'' connects to a device's own diagnostic utilities. Ctrl/P halts the processor and enters the console program when the terminal is in program mode and not secured. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_7000:dec_7000_console_reference_manual.pdf?linkonly|DEC 7000 AXP System and VAX 7000 Console Reference Manual}}, EK-70C0B-TM.002, pp. 2-5, 3-29, 3-31)]

A DEC 7000 could be upgraded in its cabinet to an [[hardware:systems:turbolaser|AlphaServer 8400]] by changing the centerplane, since the 8400 "uses the same DEC/VAX 7000 cabinet, 3-phase power and cooling systems, and I/O options". [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Systems Technical Summary}}, Digital, 1996, p. 4)]

==== Firmware and known issues ====

  * **Firmware:** the Model 600 needs console firmware V3.0 or later. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1994-June.mbox#L5580|"Re: Does osf/1 1.3 run on Dec 3000/600"]], Dave Parker, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 22 Jun 1994)] The firmware CD carries three DEC 3000 images, one for the Model 300 series, one for the 400, 600, and 700, and one for the 500, 800, and 900, matching the Pelican, Sandpiper, and Flamingo families. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L23565|"Re: (?) Firmware update for DecStation 3000"]], Andrew Gallatin, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 4 Apr 1995)] On the Model 400 the firmware update jumper can only be reached by nearly dismantling the machine. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1994-September.mbox#L6787|"Re: Will DEC OSF/1 become usable on "small" machines?"]], Andrew Gallatin, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 18 Sep 1994)]
  * **Serial ROM before firmware V6.1:** on the Model 400, firmware V6.1 with a serial ROM older than V2.1 leaves the machine unbootable, and recovery needs field service. The SROM version appears in ''show config'' (e.g. ''sV2.1''), and the SROM is the only socketed chip on the system board. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-July.mbox#L824|"SUMMARY: v6.1 Firmware upgrade on 3000/400's"]], Thomas Owano, alpha-osf-managers, 28 Jul 1995)] One affected owner reported that only a new system board fixed it. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/tru64-unix-managers-mbox/1995-August.mbox#L6181|"Summary: Firmware and sROM on 3000/400"]], John P. Speno, alpha-osf-managers, 23 Aug 1995)]
  * **Machine check 0x660:** on the Model 300 this is "a parity error on a DMA read from memory by the onboard SCSI channel", usually from the memory SIMMs or the backup cache on the CPU daughter card. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1994-November.mbox#L7955|"Re: MACHINE ERROR 0x660 (DEC 300's)"]], Raj K. Ramanujan, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 16 Nov 1994)]
  * **Keyboards:** LK401-AA keyboards before revision D autorepeated spuriously and lost modifier state on the Model 300 series, which Digital fixed in revision D. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1994-June.mbox#L8861|"Re: LK401-AA Rev C01 keyboards"]], Mark Jeffery, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 23 Jun 1994)]
  * **Ethernet:** the port is chosen at the console with ''set ethernet thick'' or ''set ethernet tenbt'', on the DEC 4000 as well. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L8638|"DEC OSF/1 AXP Frequently Asked Questions"]], Steve Lionel, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 21 Aug 1995)]
  * **Disks:** the built-in RZ disks on Digital's workstations do not spin up at power-on but under software control, so one moved to another system may need a jumper change. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1993-July.mbox#L5649|"comp.unix.ultrix Common Frequently Asked Questions"]], Win Treese, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 2 Jul 1993)]

  * "Support for TC devices should work for DEC 3000 out of the box as drivers are platform-independent. Glue code is for the core chipset is missing". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-July.mbox#L6100|"Re: Flogging a dead computer - DEC 3000/300"]], Maciej W. Rozycki, axp-list, 21 Jul 2000)] Attempts at a port in 1999 and plans in 2009 did not result in support. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L22983|"tbia() on DEC 3000 question"]], Craig P Prescott, axp-list, 16 Aug 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2009-September.mbox#L528|"[RFC] Dropping Alpha sparse mapping support from X"]], Maciej W. Rozycki, axp-list, 4 Sep 2009)]
  * "NetBSD has been ported to the TurboChannel machines." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-November.mbox#L45909|"Re: Install Linux AXP"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 25 Nov 1996)]
