===== DEC 3000, DEC 4000, and DEC 7000 =====

These are Digital's first Alpha systems, predating PCI. "The DEC 3000 series was the first Alpha-based workstation ever produced. Its system bus was TurboChannel". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1998-October.mbox#L63171|"Re: DEC 3000"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 6 Oct 1998)] The DEC 3000 Models 300 are Pelican, the 400, 600, and 700 Sandpiper, and the 500, 800, and 900 Flamingo; the Models 700 and 900 have EV45 CPUs. The DEC 4000 (Cobra and Fang) is a Futurebus+ server with up to two CPUs, and the DEC 7000 and 10000 (Ruby) have up to six. [(>{{wiki:documentation:references:alpha_axp_system_reference_manual_v6.pdf?linkonly|Alpha AXP System Reference Manual}}, Version 6, 1994, pp. D-2, D-5, D-6)]

Digital announced the fastest DEC 3000 models in August 1994. The Model 900, available that month with DEC OSF/1 AXP V2.1 and OpenVMS AXP V6.1, was sold as the "fastest workstation in the world" on the strength of 16.82 XMARKS with the standard ZLX-E1 graphics card: a 275 MHz 21064A with 2 MB of cache, 32 MB to 1 GB of memory, two 10 MB/s Fast SCSI-2 controllers, and six 100 MB/s TURBOchannel slots, one of them taken by the graphics card. Model 800 owners could upgrade by a field service board swap, while Model 500 and 500X owners received a new system box. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-August.mbox#L4889|"DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Workstation Info Sheet"]], DECnews for Education, biz.digital.articles, 10 Aug 1994)] The Model 700 announced alongside it started at $23,000 for a 64 MB building block, or $27,698 with a 21-inch color monitor, 64 MB of memory, a 1 GB disk, and a CD-ROM, and the Model 600 was repriced at the same time to become "the fastest workstation under $20,000". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-August.mbox#L4776|"DEC 3000 Model 700 AXP and Reduced-Price Model 600 AXP"]], DECnews for Education, biz.digital.articles, 10 Aug 1994)] A rackmount Model 900, announced a week earlier for Digital's 19-inch ANSI/EIA cabinets or third-party enclosures, rated 189 SPECint92 and 264 SPECfp92, up to 35 percent faster than the Model 800 it upgraded from by a CPU swap. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-August.mbox#L2333|"Rackmount DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Servers and Workstations"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 1 Aug 1994)] At the low end, the Model 300LX and 300X replaced the Model 300L and Model 300 in June 1994, sold as three-user packages with two VXT 2000+ X terminals. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-June.mbox#L587|"DEC 3000 Model 300X and 300LX Workstation Companions"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 6 Jun 1994)]

Digital sold the DEC 3000 partly as an upgrade path off its older MIPS and VAX workstations. Each model's upgrade kit is a whole new system box into which the customer moves drives, memory, and TURBOchannel graphics, and the old machine goes back to Digital in the packing material the new one arrived in. The Model 300X/300LX kit covers the DECstation 2100/3100, DECstation 5000 Model 20/25/33/50 and 120/125/133/150, and DECsystem 5000 Model 200/240/260; the Model 600/600S and 800/800S kits cover the DECstation and DECsystem 5000 families and the DECsystem 3100; the Model 700 kit covers the DEC 3000 Model 400 and the DECstation 5000 lines; and the Model 900 kit covers the DEC 3000 Model 500/500X, the DECstation 5000 Model 240/260, and the VAXstation 4000 Model 90/90A. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_3000:dec_3000_model_300x_300lx_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|DEC 3000 Model 300X/300LX AXP Upgrade Information}}, EK-D33XP-UP.A01)] [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_3000:dec_3000_model_900_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Upgrade Information}}, EK-D39AX-UP.A01, pp. 4-1 to 4-2)] Factory-installed disks arrive at SCSI ID 3, so a drive carried over from the old machine that is also at ID 3 has to be re-jumpered; ID 7 is reserved for the controller, and "TURBOchannel modules and memory modules do not have SCSI addresses." [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_3000:dec_3000_model_900_upgrade_information.pdf?linkonly|DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Upgrade Information}}, pp. 4-1, A-1)]

The DEC 3000 ran OpenVMS and DEC OSF/1, and never ran Windows NT. Asked in 1996 whether a DEC 3000 Model 300LX could run Windows NT Server, Digital's James Livingston gave three reasons why the TurboChannel machines, internally "the Flamingo", would not: "there exists no firmware (console) that will support booting Windows NT on these machines", there is "no HAL to mate them to the Windows NT O/S", and "there's no business case which justifies the (significant) software engineering effort required to implement solutions to the technical problems." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L4643|"RE: Re[2]: Win/NT on AXP 3000-300LX?"]], James Livingston, alphant, 15 Feb 1996)] Digital's Windows NT support team put it more bluntly: "It is not that we do not support it. It absolutely does NOT work". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/alphant-mbox/1996-February.mbox#L4280|"Re: Win/NT on AXP 3000-300LX?"]], Scott Bingham, alphant, 14 Feb 1996)]

==== Specifications ====

=== DEC 4000 ===

The DEC 4000 Model 600 series is built around the KN430 CPU module, which "houses the DECchip 21064 processor", with up to two of them in a symmetric multiprocessing configuration. The system bus is a synchronous multiplexed interconnect carrying "a 34-bit address or a 128-bit data with 32-bit parity in a single cycle", with a snooping protocol keeping each CPU's write-through primary cache and write-back secondary cache consistent with the other CPU and with the I/O port. The arbitration logic and the system bus clock generators live on the CPU 1 module. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_4000:dec_4000_model_600_rackmount_installation_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|DEC 4000 Model 600 AXP Rackmount Installation/Owner's Guide}}, EK-BA641-IN.A01, p. 9-6)]

  * One to four memory modules, with error detection and correction, read prefetch buffers, write transaction buffers, block exchange, read data wrapping, and "memory interleaving across two or four modules of like sizes"
  * The I/O module carries one or two Ethernet ports based on the third-generation Ethernet chip (TGEC), each switchable between thickwire and ThinWire, a Futurebus+ Profile B interface doing both 32-bit and 64-bit transfers, 512 KB of FEPROM for the console and diagnostic firmware, 8 KB of EEROM, a Dallas Semiconductor DS1287 time-of-year clock, and two asynchronous serial lines, one of them the console
  * The I/O daughter board adds four SCSI-2/DSSI buses for external fixed-media devices, each taking up to seven devices, and one SCSI-2 bus for internal removable and fixed media [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_4000:dec_4000_model_600_rackmount_installation_owners_guide.pdf?linkonly|DEC 4000 Model 600 AXP Rackmount Installation/Owner's Guide}}, pp. 9-6 to 9-8)]

=== DEC 7000 and DEC 10000 ===

The CPU is the KN7AA module. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_7000:dec_7000_10000_kn7aa_cpu_technical_manual.pdf?linkonly|DEC 7000/10000 AXP KN7AA CPU Technical Manual}}, pp. 1-1, 1-2, 2-1)]

  * [[wp>Alpha_21064|DECchip 21064]] at a peak clock rate of 200 MHz, in single or multiprocessor configurations
  * 4 MB physically addressed, direct-mapped, write-back backup cache (B-cache), a superset of the CPU's 8 KB instruction and 8 KB data primary caches
  * LSB system bus, connecting CPUs, memory, and the I/O port (IOP) module; all backplane slots except slot 8, which is reserved for the IOP, take CPU or memory modules, and the first CPU should be in slot 0
  * Memory is accessed in 64-byte blocks; the top 256 MB of the address space is I/O space, divided into LSB CSR space, local Gbus space, and broadcast space
  * Onboard ROMs provide power-up self-test diagnostics and boot support
  * Runs OpenVMS AXP and DEC OSF/1 AXP

The LSB bus itself moves 64-byte memory blocks over a 128-bit data path with a 20 ns cycle time, for a usable bandwidth of 640 MB/s, and "supports writeback caches". Address and data are multiplexed on the same signals, arbitration is distributed and overlaps data transfer, and as many as three transactions can be in progress at once. A populated bus holds "one to six processor nodes, one to seven memory nodes, and a single I/O port node". [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_7000:dec_7000_10000_platform_technical_manual.pdf?linkonly|DEC 7000/10000 AXP and VAX 7000/10000 Platform Technical Manual}}, EK-7000A-TM.001, p. 2-2)] I/O hangs off the IOP as plug-in units (PIUs) filling cabinet quadrants: an XMI PIU takes two quadrants, a Futurebus+ PIU one quadrant and must sit at the rear of the cabinet, a VAXBI PIU two quadrants in the same cabinet as its XMI, and DSSI or SCSI disk PIUs one quadrant each, oriented so the arrow on the rear panel points at the blower. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_7000:dec_7000_10000_platform_technical_manual.pdf?linkonly|DEC 7000/10000 AXP and VAX 7000/10000 Platform Technical Manual}}, p. 1-8)]

The console is the familiar SRM one, with per-processor prompts: ''set cpu 1'' designates the CPU in slot 1 as the primary and the prompt changes from ''P00>>>'' to ''P01>>>''. ''show configuration'' lists the LSB slots by module name, type, revision, and mnemonic, and ''set host'' connects to a device's own diagnostic utilities. Ctrl/P halts the processor and enters the console program when the terminal is in program mode and not secured. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:dec_7000:dec_7000_console_reference_manual.pdf?linkonly|DEC 7000 AXP System and VAX 7000 Console Reference Manual}}, EK-70C0B-TM.002, pp. 2-5, 3-29, 3-31)]

A DEC 7000 could be upgraded in its cabinet to an [[hardware:systems:turbolaser|AlphaServer 8400]] by changing the centerplane, since the 8400 "uses the same DEC/VAX 7000 cabinet, 3-phase power and cooling systems, and I/O options". [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Systems Technical Summary}}, Digital, 1996, p. 4)]

==== Linux ====

Linux never supported these systems.

  * "Support for TC devices should work for DEC 3000 out of the box as drivers are platform-independent. Glue code is for the core chipset is missing". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2000-July.mbox#L6100|"Re: Flogging a dead computer - DEC 3000/300"]], Maciej W. Rozycki, axp-list, 21 Jul 2000)] Attempts at a port in 1999 and plans in 2009 did not result in support. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-August.mbox#L22983|"tbia() on DEC 3000 question"]], Craig P Prescott, axp-list, 16 Aug 1999)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2009-September.mbox#L528|"[RFC] Dropping Alpha sparse mapping support from X"]], Maciej W. Rozycki, axp-list, 4 Sep 2009)]
  * "NetBSD has been ported to the TurboChannel machines." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1996-November.mbox#L45909|"Re: Install Linux AXP"]], Jim Paradis, axp-list, 25 Nov 1996)]

    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-August.mbox#L4889|"DEC 3000 Model 900 AXP Workstation Info Sheet"]], DECnews for Education, biz.digital.articles, 10 Aug 1994, gives "21064A/275 MHz".
  * **DEC 3000 codename**
    * [[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/2004-February.mbox#L1437|"XL-266 install"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 25 Feb 2004, refers to the "3000, aka Flamingo".
    * {{wiki:documentation:references:alpha_axp_system_reference_manual_v6.pdf?linkonly|Alpha AXP System Reference Manual}}, Version 6, p. D-2, gives Flamingo only for the Models 500, 800, and 900.
