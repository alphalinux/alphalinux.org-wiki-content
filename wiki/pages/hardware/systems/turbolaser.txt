===== AlphaServer 8200 and 8400 (TurboLaser) =====

The AlphaServer 8200 and 8400 are the largest Alpha systems Digital built around the 21164, and they "use the same system bus, processor modules, memory modules, and I/O port modules". [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Systems Technical Summary}}, p. 1)] The 8200 is a 19-inch rackmount cabinet with single-phase power for office and laboratory use, while the 8400 uses the DEC 7000 cabinet with three-phase power for computer rooms. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Systems Technical Summary}}, p. 4)] A DEC 7000 could be upgraded to an 8400 in its cabinet by swapping the card cage and centerplane; even the SIMMs from its MS7AA-FA memory modules carried over. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Systems Technical Summary}}, p. 4)] The EV6 [[hardware:systems:gs140|AlphaServer GS60 and GS140]] are the same machines with 21264 CPUs.

==== Announcement ====

Digital announced both systems in April 1995 as the first machines built on the 300 MHz 21164. The 8400, its "top-of-the-line enterprise server", took up to 12 processors, 14 GB of memory, and 1.2 GB per second of peak I/O, with up to 144 PCI slots on 12 separate PCI buses, and was rated at over 3,000 transactions per second and more than 2 gigaflops on LINPACK 1000x1000. DEC 7000 and VAX 7000 systems could be upgraded to it "in a few hours". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L1626|"AlphaServer 8400 5/300 Enterprise Server"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 17 Apr 1995)] The 8200 had a five-slot system bus rather than nine, taking up to six processors and 6 GB of memory with up to 108 PCI slots, and was rated at over 2,000 transactions per second and 3.6 gigaflops. Its base system included a 2 GB SCSI system disk, an in-cabinet CD-ROM reader, an I/O module with three fast wide differential SCSI ports, one single-ended SCSI port, two Ethernet ports, and one I/O hose port, with FDDI and Prestoserve daughter cards optional. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L1934|"AlphaServer 8200 5/300 Server"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 17 Apr 1995)] Digital gave peak figures of 3.6 gigaflops for six CPUs and 7.2 gigaflops for twelve, and promised PCI memory channel clustering, "promising power up to 56 GF", as a coming option. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L2172|"AlphaServer 8400 and 8200 High Performance Power"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 17 Apr 1995)] It sold the machines partly as a consolidation play against installed VAX and MIPS hardware, putting the annual cost of operating an AlphaServer 8400 5/300 at $5,514 against $39,359 for a VAX 8810, and rating a uniprocessor 8200 or 8400 at five to six times the compute performance of a VAX 7610. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-April.mbox#L2499|"Upgrading to AlphaServer 8200 and 8400 Systems"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 17 Apr 1995)]

A rackmount 8200 5/300 followed in October 1995, slide mounted in a 19-inch industry standard cabinet, and offering PCI, XMI, and VME I/O together on OpenVMS and Digital UNIX. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-October.mbox#L5219|"Rackmount AlphaServer 8200 5/300 System"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 3 Oct 1995)]

The //Digital Technical Journal// paper on the platform explains what the design aimed at: the team "set aggressive system data bandwidth and memory read latency targets", and factored the low-latency criterion into each of the seven layers of platform development. The platform was meant to carry "at least three generations of products". Digital reported a uniprocessor SPECfp92 of 512 for both machines, 1.4 times the SGI Power Challenge XL, and SPECrate levels for 1 to 12 processors more than 3.5 times those of the HP9000-800 T500. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L7024|"The AlphaServer 8000 Series: High-end Server Platform Development"]], David M. Fenwick, Denis J. Foley, William B. Gist, Stephen R. VanDoren, and Daniel Wissell, Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Aug 1995)]

==== Specifications ====

Except where noted, the following comes from the technical summary. [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Systems Technical Summary}}, pp. 2, 4-5)]

  * TLSB system bus with a 40-bit command and address path and a separate 256-bit data path with 32 ECC bits, run synchronously at a submultiple of the CPU clock: 75 MHz with 300 MHz CPUs for 1.6 GB/s, and 87.4 MHz with 350 and 440 MHz CPUs for 1.87 GB/s
  * AlphaServer 8400: nine bus slots, up to six processor modules (12 CPUs), up to seven memory modules (28 GB), up to three I/O modules (12 I/O buses), 1260 MB/s of I/O bandwidth
  * AlphaServer 8200: five bus slots, up to three processor modules (6 CPUs), up to three memory modules (12 GB), up to three I/O modules, 945 MB/s of I/O bandwidth
  * Processor modules carry one or two [[wp>Alpha_21164|21164]] CPUs at 300, 350, or 440 MHz, each with its own data and address path and a 4 MB third-level cache on the module; the 440 MHz module was sold only as a dual
  * The minimum configuration is one processor module, one memory module, and one I/O adapter
  * Memory modules of 128, 256, and 512 MB and 1, 2, and 4 GB, built from SIMMs; modules of 2 GB and smaller interleave two ways onboard, the 4 GB module four ways, and two to eight ways between modules
  * I/O connects through "hoses" to 12-slot PCI or, on the 8400 only, 12-slot XMI channels. "In an 8200, only PCI buses are supported, regardless of operating system." [(>{{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_operations_manual.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Operations Manual}}, pp. 1-2, 1-7)]
  * The KFTHA I/O port module drives external I/O buses; the KFTIA adds direct SCSI, two Ethernet ports, optional FDDI, and an optional 4 MB Prestoserve
  * Multiple power regulators, optional battery backup, and an optional integrated uninterruptible power supply

==== Linux ====

Linux never supported them. In 1997, "It doesn't currently run on the 8000 series, AFAIK. I believe NetBSD *does*, however, but only single CPU." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1997-June.mbox#L11094|"Re: AlphaServer 8400"]], Jay Estabrook, axp-list, 10 Jun 1997)] In 2003, "Correct; no Linux support for 8200/8400-class machines. Trade it in for a Wildfire or Marvel... ;-}" [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/debian-alpha-mbox/2003-September.mbox#L17415|"Re: AlphaServer GS140 (8400)"]], Jay Estabrook, debian-alpha, 23 Sep 2003)]

Matthew Jacob, of NASA Ames: "I started getting somewhere with a Linux port a while back but dropped it. Instead we ported NetBSD to it". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/axp-list-mbox/1999-March.mbox#L27651|"Re: AS 8400 (was: Re: Prices for AlphaLinux systems with 4-8GB RAM"]], Matthew Jacob, axp-list, 10 Mar 1999)]

==== Conflicting information ====

The following sources conflict with each other or with the account given above. They are listed here for further investigation.

  * **AlphaServer 8400 maximum CPUs**
    * {{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_operations_manual.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Operations Manual}}, March 1998, p. 1-7: "up to seven processor modules for a total of 14 CPUs"
    * {{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Systems Technical Summary}}, 1996, pp. 2, 5: "Up to 6 CPU modules, for a maximum of 12 CPUs"
    * {{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_windows_nt_systems_catalog.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200 and 8400 Windows NT Systems}}, June 1999, p. 2.4: "maximum of four CPU modules (total of 8 CPUs)"
  * **AlphaServer 8200 maximum CPUs**
    * {{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_operations_manual.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Operations Manual}}, March 1998, p. 1-7: "up to three processor modules for a total of six CPUs"
    * {{wiki:hardware:systems:alphaserver_8400:alphaserver_8200_8400_technical_summary.pdf?linkonly|AlphaServer 8200/8400 Systems Technical Summary}}, 1996, p. 5, agrees, giving up to 6 CPUs on 3 modules.
