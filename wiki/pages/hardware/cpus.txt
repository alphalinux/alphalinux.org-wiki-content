==== Names and type codes ====

==== Performance notes ====

The early systems' direct-mapped backup caches made performance depend on luck. Dick Sites explained run-to-run variations of about 20 percent in benchmarks as the result of virtual to physical page mapping: "Some runs then by fluke get page mappings that thrash in the direct-mapped cache. This effect is most pronounced in benchmarks (Murphy rules :-))." [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-osf1-mbox/googlegroups-1993-May.mbox#L2600|"Re: AXP 3000/300 oddity #1 -- CPU timings"]], Dick Sites, comp.unix.osf.osf1, 11 May 1993)] Alpha also has no integer divide instruction: a program that spent "more than half of it's cycles in *integer* division" ran slowly on Alpha because division is "a library routine on Alpha and a hardware instruction on MIPS". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/comp-unix-osf-misc-mbox/googlegroups-1994-January.mbox#L2040|"Re: Optimization on ALPHA OSF1 ??"]], Burkhard Neidecker-Lutz, comp.unix.osf.misc, 27 Jan 1994)]

==== Announcements and prices ====

Digital Semiconductor sold Alpha chips on the merchant market, and its own announcements record the speed ladder and the list prices. In June 1994 it cut prices across the family and replaced the 225 MHz 21064A with a 233 MHz version, whose clock suited the 33 MHz 21071 and 21072 core logic introduced that January. Prices per chip in quantities of 5,000, effective 3 July 1994, were $186 for the 21068-66, $347 for the 21066-166, $404 for the 21064-166, $544 for the 21064-200, $788 for the 21064A-233, and $1,083 for the 21064A-275. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1994-June.mbox#L980|"Press/New Reduced Prices For Alpha AXP Microprocessors"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 7 Jun 1994)]

The 21164 was announced on 7 September 1994 as "the world's first commercially available microprocessor capable of issuing more than one billion instructions per second", and the first to break 300 MHz, with 9.3 million transistors. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1994-September.mbox#L855|"Press/Digital Microprocessor Posts World Record"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 7 Sep 1994)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1994-September.mbox#L1003|"Press/Digital Extends Performance Leadership With New Microprocessors"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 9 Sep 1994)] [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-October.mbox#L5837|"New Digital Alpha AXP MicroProcessor Press Release"]], DECnews for Education, biz.digital.articles, 11 Oct 1994)] Two versions came at once: the 300 MHz part, estimated at 330 SPECint92, 500 SPECfp92, and 600 transactions per second, and the 266 MHz part at 290 and 440. The chip carried "the industry's first two-level, on-chip cache", 8 KiB of instruction cache and 8 KiB of data cache over a 96 KiB unified second-level cache, which removed the need for an external cache in many designs. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1994-September.mbox#L7010|"Alpha AXP 21164 Microprocessor -- The World's Fastest Chip"]], Customer Update, biz.digital.articles, 23 Sep 1994)] Digital had first silicon in February 1994, samples in October 1994, and volume shipments of the 266 MHz part in January 1995 and of the 300 MHz part in March 1995. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1994-October.mbox#L1942|"Press/Digital's Alpha 21164 Remains Top Spot"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 24 Oct 1994)]

Three //Digital Technical Journal// papers on the 21164, posted in full in August 1995, give the implementation. The chip was built in a 0.5 micron CMOS process with four levels of metal, 16.5 mm by 18.1 mm, 9.3 million transistors, running at 3.3 V with 3.3 V and 5.0 V interfaces and dissipating 50 W, with a peak of 1,200 mips and 600 MFLOPS. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L11905|"Circuit Implementation of a 300-MHz 64-bit Second-generation CMOS Alpha CPU"]], William J. Bowhill et al., Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Aug 1995)] It is quad-issue and superscalar, with two 64-bit integer and two 64-bit floating-point pipelines and write-back caches kept coherent across processors, reaching an estimated 345 SPECint92 and 505 SPECfp92 and "the highest performance of any commercially available microprocessor in the world as of January 1995". [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L13096|"Internal Organization of the Alpha 21164"]], John H. Edmondson et al., Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Aug 1995)] Verification was done by simulation with implementation-directed pseudorandom exercisers and hand-written tests: "Only eight logical bugs, all unobtrusive, were detected in the first prototype design", multiple operating systems booted on those prototypes, and all the bugs were fixed before any 21164 system shipped. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-articles-mbox/googlegroups-1995-August.mbox#L14454|"Functional Verification of a Multiple-issue, Pipelined, Superscalar Alpha Processor -- the Alpha 21164 CPU Chip"]], Michael Kantrowitz and Lisa M. Noack, Digital Technical Journal, biz.digital.articles, 16 Aug 1995)]

Faster parts followed. In October 1995 Digital announced the 21164-333, estimated at 400 SPECint92 and 570 SPECfp92, and the 21064A-300 at 220 and 300, both sampling and pin-compatible with the parts they succeeded. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-October.mbox#L270|"Press/Speed Upgrades To Alpha Microprocessors Up To 333Mhz"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 3 Oct 1995)] In November 1995 it began sampling a 0.35 micron 21164 rated above 500 SPECint92, 750 SPECfp92, 11 SPECint95, and 17 SPECfp95, and said it expected 1000 SPECint92 in 1997 from the next-generation chip after the 21164. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1995-November.mbox#L1505|"Press/Digital To Ship Samples Of Latest Alpha Chip"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 15 Nov 1995)] In July 1996 came the 433 MHz and 500 MHz parts, at an estimated 13.3 and 15.4 SPECint95 and 18.4 and 21.1 SPECfp95, with a peak rate of 2 BIPS; prices per 1,000 units were $695 for the 300 MHz, $950 for the 366 MHz, and $1,492 for the 433 MHz part, with the 500 MHz part sampling and due in September. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1996-July.mbox#L721|"Press/Alpha Microprocessor Hits 500MHz; Tops for WinNT Visual Computing"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 8 Jul 1996)] The line ended at 600 MHz, announced on 31 March 1997 at 2.4 BIPS and an estimated 18.0 SPECint95 and 27.0 SPECfp95, alongside the ATX form factor [[hardware:motherboards:pc164lx|AlphaPC 164LX]] motherboard, due that summer. [(>[[https://github.com/alphalinux-org/mailing-list-archives/blob/main/biz-digital-announce-mbox/googlegroups-1997-April.mbox#L1|"Press/Alpha 21164 Microprocessor Streaks to 600MHz, Sets New Records"]], Digital Press and Analysts News, biz.digital.announce, 2 Apr 1997)]
